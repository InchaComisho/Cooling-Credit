# Episode 21: Night Talk Among Players

[← Top / README](../../README.md)

Episode 21 is a quieter moment of uncertainty after Yuto has drawn his line.

While institutions, companies, and daily life continue to move at the same time, another layer begins to rise beneath Yuto's feet: the player community.

---

Wednesday.

After seeing a customer off at the convenience-store register, Soma Yuto let out a small breath.

The lunchtime rush had passed.

Only a few unsold ice bars remained in the freezer case.

The store's air conditioning had been set slightly weaker than usual.

“It's hot today.”

A high school girl waiting at the register said it with a smile.

“It is.”

Yuto answered mechanically while, in a corner of his mind, calculating the boundary between the heat outside and the cool air inside the store.

Over the past few days, the numbers had been building little by little.

0.534 CC.

Beyond the 0.5 where he had drawn his line.

He had submitted an opinion on Cooling Commons.

He had also commented on a corporate project.

“But I still haven't really talked to other players all that much.”

He had exchanged many messages with WindArc.

He had once spoken at length with ShiroReflect.

But he had hardly ever spoken directly with the other players moving through his town.

When he finished work and left the store, the evening air was heavy and humid.

The setting sun poured in through gaps between low buildings.

His phone vibrated softly.

“You have an invitation to a nearby player chat.  
Theme: casual talk about daily cooling logs and Cooling Commons.”

“…Casual talk, huh.”

A semi-official chat room prepared by the system.

The participants were players in the same area at Bronze rank or higher.

Yuto hesitated for a moment, then tapped **Join**.

Several names appeared on the screen.

WindArc.

A few unfamiliar Bronze-rank handles.

One Silver-rank player.

One hidden-rank participant.

“WindArc:  
Today's theme is: how do we keep enjoying cooling after deciding how much to share?”

“…Even the title sounds troubled.”

Yuto began reading the chat log.

“Bronze Player A:  
At first, posting logs to Cooling Commons was fun,  
but lately I've started feeling pressure, like maybe I should share everything.”

“Bronze Player B:  
When the institutional side sends messages like ‘more detail would help,’  
it gets hard to refuse.”

“Silver Player:  
To be honest, I also shared everything at first.  
But at some point, I chose a few places to keep as personal escape spaces.”

“Hidden-rank:  
Drawing a line may itself be part of cooling activity.”

Looking at the screen, Yuto remembered his own notebook.

“Things I will not share:  
- My own room  
- Detailed logs from days when my health was near the limit  
- Escape spaces”

“Everyone is thinking similar things.”

He tried writing a short message into the chat.

“Yuto:  
I've decided to share only places that people use on the assumption that they are shared.  
On the other hand, I draw the line at things like my own room or routes I only take on exhausted days.”

A few seconds later, WindArc replied.

“WindArc:  
We submitted that idea to the institutional feedback call too.  
The proposal that ‘escape-space logs should be outside the scope’ seems to have become an official discussion item.”

“Bronze Player A:  
Escape space…  
Just hearing that phrase makes me feel a little relieved.”

“Silver Player:  
The institutional side is also gradually shifting from ‘we want you to share everything’  
to ‘we want to make the best use of whatever range you are willing to share.’”

“Gradually shifting, huh.”

Yuto felt something catch on that phrase.

“So that means it hasn't fully shifted yet.”

The chat continued.

“Hidden-rank:  
The issue is how much the system respects the line after players draw it.  
Both institutions and companies always contain pressure that tries to widen the line.”

“Bronze Player B:  
That sounds like overtime at work…  
They say ‘only within what you can do,’ and then it keeps growing.”

Yuto gave a wry smile at the screen.

“Cooling activity and work both end up being about lines.”

Then a longer message appeared in the chat.

“Silver Player:  
For resident players, I think the important thing is to increase the number of allies who help protect the line.

For example:  
- Other players recognizing that ‘this is a line you don't need to share beyond’  
- People like WindArc and Yuto who can propose such lines to the institutional side

Without both of those, Cooling Commons could become a place where the more you share, the more you lose.”

“A place where the more you share, the more you lose…”

The phrase felt heavy to Yuto.

Until now, he had mainly seen the positive side: the more you shared, the cooler the town became; the more you shared, the more recognition you gained.

But sharing too much could also wear down one's own life.

The chat then drifted in a slightly softer direction.

“Bronze Player A:  
But I do like the time I spend writing logs.  
After walking through town, I write in my notebook and feel a little satisfied.”

“Yuto:  
I understand.  
I feel tired in a different way on days when I write even a little, compared with days when I write nothing.”

“Bronze Player B:  
Maybe because we like writing, we need to decide what range not to publish…”

The atmosphere in the chat room was gentle, but serious.

Backstage talk about institutions.

Rumors about corporate projects.

Boasts about cooling logs.

Those things were mixed in from time to time.

But the theme of the night was clearly the previous issue of not stepping too far in and the need for allies to protect lines.

“WindArc:  
For the COOL STREET idea call,  
I submitted a proposal that it should be limited to public-leaning places.  
We haven't received an answer yet, but at least I don't think it will move toward forcing people to submit their rooms.”

“Hidden-rank:  
In the draft of the formal version of Cooling Commons, wording that excludes life-base logs is also being considered.  
However, it still has annotations.”

“Annotations…”

Yuto sensed a kind of instability in that word.

Excluded.

However, exceptions under certain conditions.

Such wording could slip in easily.

“Even so, the fact that the word ‘excluded’ is in the draft is already a big step.”

The chat gradually moved toward its end.

“Silver Player:  
One last thing.  
The more you enjoy writing cooling logs,  
the more you should decide for yourself what range you do not need to write about.  
I think that is the key to continuing for a long time.”

“Yuto:  
That feels like the right summary for today.”

When he left the chat room, the night air had become slightly cooler.

On the way back to his apartment, Yuto breathed out softly.

“That really was a night talk among players.”

Residents.

Institution-oriented players.

Corporate-leaning players.

Their different positions mixed together, and the idea of lines was shared just a little.

Back in his room, he checked the air-conditioner setting.

The room was pleasantly cool.

Yuto opened his notebook and wrote the day's summary.

“Today's events:  
- Joined a nearby player chat  
Theme: how to keep enjoying cooling after deciding how much to share  
→ Casual talk about escape-space logs, life-base logs, and the scope of Cooling Commons  
→ Silver and hidden-rank players said allies are needed to protect lines”

“Today's observations:  
- Drawing a line alone is not enough; other players and the institutional side need to know about that line  
- People who like writing cooling logs especially need to decide the range they do not have to write about  
- The player community itself may become a buffer that helps balance Cooling Commons”

Finally, he added his usual line.

“Today's line:  
A line is not something you draw and protect alone. It is something several people share and protect together.”

When he checked his phone, the day's numbers had barely moved.

“Today's gain: 0.008 CC  
Cumulative total: 0.542 CC”

“Numerically, this was almost a rest day.”

Yuto smiled a little and closed his notebook.

“But learning that there are allies to protect the line…  
that matters more than the number.”

The world was still hot.

But within that heat, the players' night talk had begun to settle like a thin cushion between Cooling Commons and the lives of ordinary residents.

Thank you for reading Episode 21.

This episode depicted a moment after Yuto crossed 0.5 CC and drew a line for himself, then shared that line through a chat among nearby players.

Through casual conversation with WindArc, other Bronze and Silver players, and a hidden-rank participant, themes such as escape-space logs, life bases, and the risk that those who share more may lose more gradually become shared language.

By giving Yuto the sense that a line is not something one draws and protects alone, but something several people share and protect together, this episode prepares the ground for him to move into the formal version of Cooling Commons and larger projects not as an isolated resident, but as a player who protects boundaries as part of a community.

From here, I hope you will continue following how the player community balances itself against institutions and companies, and what position Yuto chooses within that balance.
