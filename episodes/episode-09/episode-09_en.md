# Episode 9: Adoption and Discomfort

[← Top / README](../../README.md)

In Episode 9, Yuto's cooling logs are used through WindArc as part of a store proposal, and part of that proposal is actually adopted.

At the same time, Yuto begins to feel the first small disturbance around the question of whose name his ideas are passing under.

---

Saturday.

From the morning, Soma Yuto stared at his phone with a restless feeling.

At the suburban chain store, WindArc's part-time workplace, part of the cooling proposal was scheduled to be introduced on a trial basis.

Yesterday, WindArc had sent him this message:

“I submitted the proposal to the store.  
Permission was granted for a trial introduction of the entrance banner flags and rear-passage danger-avoidance route.  
The changes are scheduled to start this afternoon.”

“…I have to go.”

Yuto let out a small breath while biting into bread.

His logs had become materials for a proposal.

That proposal would actually be reflected in a store site.

It had a weight different from the world of notes and reports he had known until now.

After finishing a short convenience-store shift in the morning, Yuto headed to the suburban chain store.

The intersection along the main road.

The wide parking lot.

The family restaurant sign.

The same scenery as yesterday.

And yet, it looked somehow different.

“The banner flags…”

He looked near the entrance.

The banner flags that had stood diagonally until yesterday had been moved slightly closer to the store.

The way their shadows fell had changed.

Along the path from the parking lot to the store entrance, the shaded zone had widened a little.

Yuto opened **ThermoBalance** and scanned the shade range.

“Entrance shaded zone: +1.8㎡ compared with previous day  
Surface temperature around entrance: Equivalent to -1.2°C compared with previous day  
Human standing positions: Slight increase in bias toward shaded side”

“…The numbers are appearing.”

A small change.

Even so, the fact that more shade existed was clearly reflected in the environment.

Several customers were near the entrance.

People waiting for someone.

People looking at their phones.

Their feet were slightly more often in the shade than yesterday.

“Some people will probably feel this place is a little more comfortable.”

At that moment, a uniformed staff member came out of the store.

“It's hot today.”

With a casual voice, he looked around the entrance.

While checking the position of the banner flags, he made a small note.

“Store Cooling Management Log:  
Entrance shaded zone expansion setting, Day 1”

Through his phone screen, Yuto could see a fragment of that log.

“So the site side is aware this was a setting change…”

Yuto felt a little relieved.

His idea had not been changed without anyone knowing.

It had been entered into the store's management screen as an official setting.

When he went around to the rear passage, the chain position had changed too.

In front of the outdoor units, a new warning notice had been posted.

“High-temperature area beyond this point.  
Staff members, please use the avoidance route on the right.”

“…So this is it.”

A short explanation.

But enough.

The chain was stretched slightly farther back, preventing people from standing in front of the outdoor units.

The avoidance route had a little shade and a path where air could pass.

His phone vibrated.

“Rear-passage danger-avoidance route setting detected  
Staff heat-stress index: Predicted equivalent of -9% compared with previous day  
Safety contribution: Medium”

“Safety, medium…”

This was different from the slight improvements so far.

This rear-passage setting change seemed to have considerable meaning for the staff on-site.

“WindArc really got this through.”

Thinking that, Yuto looked at the store's back entrance.

Then the rear door opened, and a familiar face appeared.

“You came.”

“I did.”

WindArc wore a kitchen apron and was sweating a little.

But his expression was bright.

“What do you think? The entrance banners and the rear passage.”

“The entrance has more shade. The rear passage feels like the danger zone is clearly visible now.”

“Right?”

WindArc showed a slightly relieved smile.

“The store's reaction wasn't too bad either.”

“What was it like?”

“For the entrance, they said it was fine as long as visibility didn't become too cluttered.  
For the rear passage, they said they would try it if it reduced staff burden.”

“It passes more easily than I expected.”

“It took quite a bit of adjustment before it passed.”

WindArc briefly explained the proposal.

For the entrance part, the shade-zone diagram Yuto had organized in his report had been used.

For the rear-passage proposal, Yuto's idea of danger-avoidance flow lines had been incorporated.

“The proposal itself was submitted under my name, though.”

“That makes sense.”

Yuto deliberately answered lightly.

A site staff member.

A store part-timer.

A proposal to the company.

Putting a name on it was WindArc's role as someone working there.

Yuto was only a material provider.

One person who had written a report.

“But I did include your report ID as a reference material inside the proposal.”

“Report ID?”

“The store-side management screen can reference cooling reports.”

Hearing that, some tension left Yuto's shoulders.

It had not been used completely unsigned.

At least within the system, the fact that materials had been taken from his report remained.

“Then if this store's cooling results are evaluated…”

“In the system, it should be treated as WindArc's proposal plus Yuto's report reference.”

For his current self, that was enough.

In the afternoon, Yuto watched the store from a little distance.

The parking lot entrance.

The shadows of the banner flags.

The movement line through the rear passage.

He checked how people actually moved.

As more people arrived toward evening, the percentage of people stopping on the shaded side did indeed increase.

The staff passing through the rear passage also avoided the front of the outdoor units and used the avoidance route.

“Seeing something adopted like this…”

Yuto murmured softly.

“It feels like I became a small part of this place.”

His phone vibrated.

“Initial results of cooperative proposal detected  
Proposal contribution: WindArc 70% / Yuto 30% (provisional)  
Cooperative bonus: +0.012 CC”

“It even shows contribution percentages…?”

Surprised, Yuto stared at the screen.

The main proposer was WindArc.

As a material provider, Yuto also had a certain contribution.

The system evaluated it that way.

It was one form of fairness based on the rules of this world.

That night, after returning to his apartment, Yuto opened his notebook and summarized the day's events.

“Initial results at suburban chain store:  
- Entrance banner position adjustment → shaded zone expansion → standing positions shift toward shade  
- Rear-passage danger-avoidance route → predicted reduction in staff heat stress  
- My report ID was recorded as a reference in the proposal”

“It should be good…”

When he finished writing, his pen stopped.

A small discomfort remained somewhere in his heart.

“If the store publishes this as a success case, the name that appears will probably be WindArc's.”

Corporate publicity.

Success case.

Cooling project.

The name shown there would probably be “store staff WindArc.”

Even if his ID remained inside the system as the source report, it would not appear outside.

“I can't really say that's wrong…”

WindArc was the one moving on-site.

WindArc was the one who submitted the proposal.

But part of the idea was Yuto's.

The skeleton of the log had come from his daily movement route perspective.

That difference slowly cast a shadow in his mind.

His phone vibrated.

“Notification:  
Your cooling report was referenced as part of a store cooling success case.  
*When publicly disclosed externally, credit display includes only proposer name and store name.”

“…Right.”

Yuto stared silently at the screen for a while.

The system recorded the reference to his report.

But in external publication — store publicity or case introductions — only the proposer name and store name would come to the front.

Other people would not know where the original idea came from.

“Because the report ID remains, I guess the system considers it fine…”

It was not that he could not accept it.

But he felt that something was missing.

Maybe it was just ego.

Maybe it was only a desire to have his name shown.

But he also felt it was not simply desire.

“If I seriously make cooling design my work someday, this will be an important issue.”

Names.

Credit.

Return of value.

Cooling reports.

Proposals.

Success cases.

The lines between them still had ambiguous parts in the current system.

In a corner of his notebook, Yuto wrote with slightly stronger strokes:

“Today's discomfort:  
- Inside the system, the reference source is recorded, but it disappears in external publication  
- It is natural for the proposer to be named, but ordinary people cannot see traces of the original idea  
- As it is, proper return to the idea side may become too weak”

“I'm not really in a position to complain yet…”

Convenience-store work.

Living expenses.

0.318 CC cumulative.

He was still just a regular person and a beginner player.

Even so, he could see something that might become a hole in the system.

At that moment, a new notification arrived on his phone.

“Message from nearby player  
Sender: Rank hidden  
Message:  
‘Would you like to talk sometime about the relationship between success cases and report references?  
There is something slightly concerning about how ideas and credit are handled.’”

“…So I'm not the only one bothered by it.”

Rank hidden.

He did not know who it was.

But they were probably Silver tier or higher.

A line separate from WindArc.

Someone with a more system-oriented perspective.

“I'll hear them out.”

Yuto wrote a short reply.

“Reply:  
‘I would like to talk.  
I also felt a slight discomfort today.’”

When he pressed send, the phone vibrated softly.

“Emergence of systemic question detected  
Interest in cooling system design level: Low → Medium”

“The system sees even that…?”

With a wry smile, Yuto placed the phone face down.

Today was the day he had learned two things at once: the fact that something had been adopted, and the reality that names can disappear in external publication.

Holding both of them, Yuto quietly closed his notebook.
