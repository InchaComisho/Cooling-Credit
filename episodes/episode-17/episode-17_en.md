# Episode 17: Happiness and Unease

[← Top / README](../../README.md)

In Episode 17, Yuto hears that partial route adjustments have begun at the suburban shopping center and goes to see them for himself, experiencing both happiness and a small unease.

The still-unsettled parts of Cooling Commons — the treatment of ideas and the return of value — begin to feel slightly unstable within the changes taking place on-site.

---

Sunday.

Soma Yuto stared absentmindedly at the bus-stop timetable.

Toward the neighboring city.

Toward the suburban shopping center.

There were not many buses.

Even so, a fair number of people used them in the morning.

Yesterday's notification remained on his phone screen.

“Trial operation of route adjustments at the suburban shopping center has begun.  
Reference material: Resident comment by Soma Yuto”

“…I decided to go see it, so I might as well go.”

He could have written the comment and left it at that.

But once they had told him it was being used as reference material, he wanted to see the result with his own eyes.

The bus arrived, and Yuto tapped his IC card and boarded.

The vehicle was moderately filled with families and shoppers.

The air conditioning was neither too strong nor too weak.

Outside the window, the scenery gradually shifted from residential streets to the outskirts of the city.

When he got off at the shopping-center stop, a vast parking lot spread out before him.

Asphalt.

White lines.

Cars.

The outer walls of the stores.

Yuto first opened **ThermoBalance** in simplified mode.

“Parking-lot surface temperature: +4.3°C compared with surroundings  
Shaded zones: Limited to entrance area and some passages  
Cooling potential: Medium to high”

“By the numbers, it isn't very different from the suburban chain store near my town.”

Only the scale was different.

There were multiple stores and a much larger parking lot.

“The question is how far they've gone with making the easier route visible.”

Yuto looked at the route from the bus stop to the entrance.

The shortest path went straight ahead, but much of it was exposed to the sun.

Beside it, however, was a narrow passage with a small sign.

“Cooler route this way →”

“…They actually did it.”

The sign was neither too prominent nor too subtle.

Along the passage, shade from street trees and simple canopies connected together.

Watching the flow of people, Yuto saw that about half chose the cooler route.

His phone vibrated.

“Cooler-route usage rate: +37% compared with before trial  
Effect of visualizing the ‘easier route’ confirmed (provisional)”

“So they really picked up my comment that simply knowing which route is easier changes how it feels.”

That made him honestly happy.

The bench near the entrance had also been moved slightly.

Apparently, it used to stick halfway into the sun.

Now, it fit entirely within the roof's shadow.

At its base was a small plate.

“This bench was positioned with reference to resident logs.  
(Cooling Commons trial)”

“They've put the plate here too.”

A parent and child returning from shopping sat on the bench.

The child had ice cream.

The parent looked at a phone.

Both were fully inside the shade.

Yuto watched from a short distance away.

“Looking only at this, it feels perfect.”

The cooler route.

A shaded bench.

A clearly marked place where people were allowed to sit.

The time spent walking from the parking lot to the stores had changed from time endured in the heat into time that was at least a little easier.

“The problem is what comes after this.”

Yuto moved toward the edge of the parking lot, away from the entrance.

It was not the shortest route, and there was almost no shade or wind.

Old benches and vending machines outside the trial area remained there.

People stopped.

Direct sun.

No shade.

**ThermoBalance** displayed the area like this:

“Non-trial area:  
Shaded zones: Almost none  
Stay comfort: Low  
Cooling potential: Medium”

“So this is what the difference between trial and non-trial areas looks like.”

The improved areas had clearly become easier to use.

The areas that had not been improved were unchanged.

Yuto felt a slight roughness inside himself.

“People live their lives here too.”

Some people used the trial route, and some did not.

Depending on where they parked and the habits of their route, some people naturally never entered the easier path.

“I understand that you can't efficiently change everything at once, so you start with one section…”

Cooling Commons.

Institutions.

Companies.

All of them worked on the principle of beginning with a limited test.

At that moment, his phone vibrated again.

“Cooling Commons trial log: Suburban shopping-center route adjustment

Notes, excerpt:  
‘Improvement effects have been confirmed in the trial area, but resident feedback concerning the gap with non-trial areas must also be collected.  
If the provider of the resident comment is visiting the site, we would appreciate an additional observation if possible.’”

“…They're watching.”

Yuto gave a wry smile.

“They know I'm here?”

Of course, they could tell through location information and log references.

That itself made sense.

“Then I'll write down the rough parts too.”

Standing at the edge of the parking lot, Yuto opened his notes app.

“Resident addendum: Impressions of the non-trial area at the suburban shopping center

- The cooler route and shaded benches in the trial area clearly make walking and waiting easier.  
  That deserves strong praise.

- On the other hand, the non-trial area — especially the far edge of the parking lot — still contains places where people do not know where they are allowed to stop and can do nothing but endure the heat.

- As a resident, I am happy that some places have improved, but at the same time, I feel a small sense that people remaining in the unimproved areas are being left behind.

- I understand that it is difficult to change everything at once, but even one small sign saying ‘You may rest here’ in the non-trial area would make the gap feel very different.”

As he wrote, the roughness in his chest began to settle into shape.

“This should be written as a hope, not a complaint.”

Yuto added one final sentence.

“- If the trial area is expanded in the future, I would be very glad, as a resident, if priority were given to creating the first small point where people in the non-trial area could feel relief.”

A quiet response arrived a few minutes after he sent it.

“Corporate design-team comment, excerpt:

‘Thank you for your impressions of the non-trial area.  
The sentence, “I am happy that some places have improved, but I also feel that people in unimproved areas are being left behind,” will be treated as an important perspective in future discussions about phased implementation.

We will also consider, during the next phase, the proposal to first create one small point of relief in the non-trial area.’”

“…They understood it.”

Even so, nothing would change immediately.

The non-trial area was still hot today.

Yuto walked once more from the edge of the parking lot to the entrance.

The cooler route.

The shortest sun-exposed route.

Both clearly existed.

“Just as you can't cool the whole world at once, you can't change an entire parking lot at once.”

He understood that, yet somewhere inside he still wished everything could change.

That contradiction became the roughness he felt.

On the bus back to town, Yuto organized his notes.

“Today's events:  
- Inspected the trial route at the suburban shopping center  
  → Visualized cooler route and repositioned shaded benches were effective even through direct experience  
  → Non-trial areas still contained places where people could only endure the heat  
- Cooling Commons trial log requested a resident observation of the non-trial area, so I added both the positive effects and my unease  
  → Corporate design team plans to use the sentence in discussions of phased implementation”

“Today's observations:  
- Trial introduction creates a difference between places that changed and places that have not yet changed  
- For residents, that difference produces happiness and a slight feeling of being left behind at the same time  
- Cooling Commons can contribute to phased improvement by preserving not only happiness, but also unease, as part of the log”

Finally, his usual single line.

“Today's line:  
- I am glad that my comment helped create a cooler route, but I also want to keep looking toward the places that are still hot.”

His cumulative Cooling Credit was rising slowly.

“Earned today: 0.027 CC  
Cumulative total: 0.478 CC”

“0.5 really is almost here.”

Along with the numerical milestone, he felt that he was nearing a point where he would need to decide how to handle the happiness and unease inside himself.

“Maybe my role is not to paint everything positively, but to write down both what feels good and what catches against me.”

Yuto closed the notebook and quietly exhaled.

The world was still hot.

But the cooler route, the shaded bench, and a comment containing both happiness and unease were beginning to add new layers to that heat.

Thank you for reading Episode 17.

This episode followed Yuto as he visited the trial routes at the suburban shopping center and experienced both the happiness of seeing his comment take physical form and the unease caused by the heat remaining in non-trial areas.

By asking for resident impressions of the non-trial area, the Cooling Commons trial log begins to show a system that records not only positive change, but also feelings of being left behind, and uses them for phased improvement.

As Yuto becomes conscious of his role in writing down not only positive outcomes but also points of discomfort, he gains a more honest foundation for future themes such as appropriation, return of value, and inequality.

I hope you will continue following the choices Yuto makes in this world where happiness and unease exist at the same time.
