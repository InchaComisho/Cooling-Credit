# Episode 8: Beyond the Living Zone

[← Top / README](../../README.md)

In Episode 8, Yuto follows WindArc's invitation and begins looking beyond his usual living zone, toward the boundary between individuals and organizations: small chain stores and semi-public spaces.

His own perspective and a slightly more professional viewpoint begin to intersect, and the difference between individual cooling and organizational cooling starts to appear.

---

Friday.

Soma Yuto checked his phone notifications over and over.

The message from WindArc the previous night:

“Would you like to look at a slightly larger place together someday?”

Yuto had replied:

“If the timing works, I would like to look together.”

That timing arrived sooner than he expected.

A little after nine in the morning, a new mission appeared in **ThermoBalance**.

“Cooperative Mission Notice:  
Target: Nearby players (Bronze and above)  
Location: Suburban chain store surroundings  
Content: Proposal to improve heat stagnation and customer flow around the store”

“…It came.”

Under the description, the sender's name appeared in small text.

“Proposer: WindArc (Bronze tier)”

“A suburban chain store…”

It was a place slightly outside Yuto's living zone.

Not the station front, and not the riverside.

A suburban store where many customers came by car.

A place he would not often visit on foot alone.

But one scene immediately came to Yuto's mind.

Along a large road near his apartment, there was an area where a family restaurant, a drugstore, and a supermarket stood side by side.

The parking lot was wide, and heat always accumulated around it.

“It's not completely outside my living zone either…”

He sometimes passed by after work.

He sometimes went shopping there.

“I'll go.”

Once he decided, Yuto moved immediately.

He had no convenience-store shift that morning.

He had time.

The heat was harsh, but that also meant the margin for cooling was large.

After passing the intersection along the main road, the area in question came into view.

A large family restaurant.

Next to it, a drugstore.

Behind them, a supermarket.

In front was a wide parking lot, its asphalt shining white in the sun.

“As always, it's a sea of heat…”

Yuto stood on the sidewalk in front of the family restaurant and opened **ThermoBalance**.

“Parking-lot surface temperature: +4.6°C compared with surroundings  
Exhaust-heat concentration: High (store rear side)  
Human stay rate: Medium to High (weekends and evenings)  
Cooling potential: Medium to High”

“When the potential shows high, it feels like pressure…”

This place was a little different from an individual's living zone.

The store side tried to keep the inside comfortable using air conditioning and intake and exhaust systems.

As a result, heat was pushed outside.

People moved back and forth between the cool interior and the hot parking lot.

Somewhere in that flow, there had to be room for cooling.

“Now… where should I start?”

As Yuto looked over the entire parking lot, a voice came from behind him.

“You came.”

He turned around and saw a young man in casual clothes, not running wear.

“WindArc.”

“Yes.”

He raised a hand lightly and smiled.

His clothes were different from those at the riverside yesterday.

But his eyes were the same.

“This is where I work part-time.”

“Your workplace?”

“The family restaurant.”

WindArc pointed at the sign.

“I work in the kitchen, and the exhaust heat behind the building is pretty severe.”

“The back side…”

In the passage behind the store, multiple outdoor units and ventilation outlets lined the wall.

Hot wind constantly blew from them.

“Customers don't notice it.”

“Well, it's the back.”

“But this heat wraps around to the parking lot side more than you'd think.”

WindArc walked a short distance to show him.

The corner of the store.

The edge of the parking lot.

An invisible line of heat passed through there.

“If you stand here, can you feel the wind is hot?”

“…You're right.”

Yuto narrowed his eyes as if checking the wind direction.

Heat blown out from the store rear covered part of the parking lot.

It mixed with car engine heat and became even heavier.

“In summer evenings, customers start saying this parking lot feels uncomfortable somehow.”

“Somehow.”

“They don't say it specifically, but they say it's hot and they don't want to stay long.”

That was something that could affect sales.

“If we make the air conditioning stronger, the electricity bill rises. If we cover the whole parking lot, it costs too much.  
So for now, everyone treats it as unavoidable.”

“Places written off as unavoidable are where the growth potential is.”

“Exactly.”

WindArc smiled.

“So this mission is about seeing how much two individual players can propose before it becomes a full organization-level matter.”

Yuto looked over the whole parking lot.

There was no large roof.

Almost no tree shade.

The only cooling element was the slight shade near the store entrance.

“Shade, wind, and flow line…”

The usual keywords lined up in his head.

But the scale here was different.

Placing a piece of paper under a tree would not be enough.

“Are we going to make an official proposal to the store?”

“Not immediately.”

WindArc shook his head slightly.

“First, we identify the range of things that could be done without spending much money, and organize it among ourselves.”

“Organize it…”

“It's like gathering materials so we can decide later whether to submit it as a proposal through the store's cooling management screen.”

Materials.

Logs.

Seeds of proposals.

Until now, what Yuto had done was to act directly in each place and take the numbers.

This time, he was looking at the whole place and organizing what could be possible.

It would not immediately become numbers.

But it might lead to larger numbers later.

“For now, let's divide the parking lot into three zones.”

WindArc began drawing a simple diagram on his phone.

“Near the entrance: a zone where people stay longer.  
Store rear side: a zone with strong exhaust heat.  
Far side: a zone with many cars entering and leaving.”

“Zoning.”

“I think what we can do differs for each.”

Yuto nodded.

“Near the entrance, shade and rest points.  
At the store rear, how to release exhaust heat.  
At the far side, organizing movement lines and dispersing heat.”

“Exactly.”

WindArc wrote Yuto's words directly into his notes.

“I want to include this in the second report. Something like ‘daily movement route perspective plus store perspective.’”

“The second report.”

“Your first report was really useful.”

It was an embarrassing compliment.

“I still don't think I wrote anything that impressive.”

“It is impressive. Fewer people than you'd think look at cooling places through daily movement routes.”

WindArc stood near the parking lot entrance and watched people passing through.

Before noon.

Parents with children.

Elderly people returning from shopping.

Office workers coming for lunch.

“I want to see where the walking people look most strained.”

“Most strained…”

Yuto stood in the same place and watched people's movements.

People walking along the shaded side of the store to avoid direct sunlight.

People cutting through between cars.

People stopping in front of the entrance to look at their phones.

Among them, his eyes went to the place where the burden seemed highest.

“Right in front of the entrance.”

“Exactly.”

The place where the store's shade and parking-lot heat collided.

A place everyone had to pass through to enter or leave.

“I think even a simple mechanism that increases shade and a marker for where to stand would make a difference.”

“Mechanism…”

“For example, changing the position of the banner flags.”

WindArc pointed to the banner flags beside the entrance.

Right now, they stood diagonally toward the parking lot.

If they were moved slightly closer to the store, they could increase the shade.

They could expand the visible cool zone.

“Using banners to increase shade is a good idea.”

“And it costs zero.”

Yuto immediately wrote down the idea.

“Parking lot entrance:  
- Adjust position and angle of banner flags  
- Move them closer to the store shade to expand shaded zone  
- Naturally guide people's standing positions toward shade”

“The store rear side…”

“Make the no-entry zone in front of the outdoor units clearer.”

WindArc led him to the rear passage.

There was only a simple chain stretched across part of it.

People passed near it without much concern.

“The exhaust heat here is so strong that staff get tired all at once when they pass through in summer.”

“Can't the route be changed?”

“It can, but the route gets a little longer, so everyone dislikes it.”

A short route and a long route.

A hot route and a less bad route.

Right now, the short and hot route was being chosen.

“This also becomes danger-avoidance guidance.”

“Yes. Similar to the warehouse aisle.”

Yuto scanned the chain position and movement lines.

“Rear passage exhaust-heat zone: Danger level Medium to High  
Danger-avoidance route: Not set”

“Even placing an arrow like ‘Hot area, this route recommended’ might change things.”

“I'll propose it to the store.”

WindArc nodded while taking notes.

“Things like this will make the report stronger if combined with your daily movement log.”

“Combined…”

Living-zone perspective and store perspective.

Individual footsteps and organizational rules.

They would line up in one report.

“What about the far side?”

“That involves car movement, so let's only look at it roughly today.”

The far side of the parking lot had many cars entering and leaving.

Engine heat.

Tire friction.

Idling time.

Changing that would probably require a formal discussion with the store side.

“For now, I want to summarize at least the entrance and the rear side from the perspective of individual players.”

“So today's work is material gathering.”

“Exactly.”

In the afternoon, Yuto returned to his apartment and sat at his desk.

He lined up his notebook and phone and organized what he had seen that day.

“Suburban chain store surrounding cooling memo (materials):  
- Parking lot entrance:  
  - Expand shaded zone by adjusting banner flag positions  
  - Guide people's standing positions toward shade  
- Store rear passage:  
  - Clear avoidance route around danger zone in front of outdoor units  
  - Guide people from the shorter route to a slightly longer but cooler route  
- Far-side parking zone:  
  - Organize stopping positions and entry/exit routes (requires store-side review)”

“This can go straight into the second report…”

Yuto opened the cooling report creation screen in **ThermoBalance**.

In the title field, he typed:

“Suburban Cooling Memo from Daily Route and Store Perspectives”

In the description field, he wrote briefly:

“Notes on a suburban chain store parking lot and rear passage, viewed from both daily movement routes and store-side perspectives.  
I summarized improvement ideas that individual players can still propose as materials.”

For visibility, he once again selected “Nearby players only.”

When he pressed submit, a small notification appeared immediately.

“Second cooling report published  
Visible to: Nearby players (Bronze and above)”

A few minutes later, another notification arrived.

“WindArc commented on your report.”

He opened it.

“The entrance and rear passage of the suburban chain store are much easier to organize now.  
May I use these materials as part of a proposal to the store?”

“Proposal…”

That was something Yuto had never created before.

Logs.

Memos.

Materials.

The moment they began to turn into a formal proposal.

“Reply:  
‘Of course. If you actually submit it to the store,  
I would be happy if you could tell me how the site responds.’”

When he sent it, another short notification came back.

“Preparation stage for cooperative proposal detected  
Expected cooperative bonus if proposal succeeds: Medium”

“This system really never stops showing expected values…”

With a wry smile, Yuto checked the Cooling Credit he had earned that day.

The guidance around the parking lot entrance and rear passage was not yet an official setting.

It was only personal observation and a little movement-line adjustment.

Even so, a small number had moved.

“Today earned: 0.029 CC  
Cumulative total: 0.318 CC”

“I passed 0.3…”

One third.

Still small.

But definitely continuing to increase.

At the end of his notebook, Yuto added one line.

“Today's observations:  
- Outside the living zone, there are still ranges an individual can observe  
- Combining store perspective and daily movement route perspective gives proposals more depth  
- Logs can become materials for proposals”

He had not yet submitted a formal proposal to a company.

But he had reached the step just before it.

Confirming that distance, Yuto quietly closed his notebook.

Thank you for reading Episode 8.

This time, prompted by WindArc's invitation, Yuto began looking beyond his living zone, toward the boundary between the individual and the organization: the surroundings of a suburban chain store.

A store-side perspective begins to overlap with his previous daily movement route perspective, and cooling logs begin to look like materials that can become proposals.

By taking this step, the story can naturally connect to later developments such as bringing ideas to companies, appropriation, return of value, and open-license-like structures in the latter half of Part 1 and into Part 2.

I hope you continue to enjoy seeing how Yuto's view expands and where he runs into walls.
