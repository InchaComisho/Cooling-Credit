# Episode 34: The Shadows Seen as Support Expands

[← Top / README](../../README.md)

Episode 34 is written as the episode where, while the expansion of support begins to become visible, the question of who that support is really for becomes even sharper.  
This time, as plans for workplace and out-of-home support become more concrete, Yuto realizes that the more support expands, the more the people still left out also become visible.

---

## Main Text

Tuesday.  
Soma Yuto sat down on a bench in the shopping street and stared at his smartphone screen.

Since yesterday, the next-stage plans for the Cooling Commons daily-life support link had slowly become a topic of discussion.  
Break support for workplaces.  
Temporary cooling spaces outside the home.  
Longer hours for cooling spots in the shopping street.

All of them were necessary.  
He believed that.  
But the more the discussion expanded, the more clearly he could see where the support would reach.

A message from WindArc had arrived.

“WindArc:  
‘The workplace support plan is moving in a pretty positive direction.  
But the more voices we hear from actual worksites, the more it also looks like support is only expanding to people who can work.’”

“...So that is the issue after all.”

Yuto muttered quietly.

The morning convenience store shift.  
Restaurant kitchens.  
Salespeople on the road.  
Logistics.  
Customer service.

Support for occupations where taking breaks was difficult was definitely needed.  
And yet, even then, the people able to stand at the entrance of that support were those who had a workplace, those who were working.

Then what about people who could not work?  
People who were unemployed?  
People who could not go out because of caregiving?  
People who could not move because of their health?

The more support expanded, the more its shadows became visible.

“The more we expand it,  
the more I start worrying about the people who still cannot get inside.”

At noon that day, Yuto met WindArc in front of the station.

A temporary misting device had been installed in the station plaza.  
It was cool.  
People gathered around it.  
But beyond it, a line of people waiting for the bus continued under the blazing sun.

“How far has the workplace support plan progressed?”

“As a proposal, it is fairly concrete.  
For example:  
- support for occupations where it is hard to take breaks,  
- securing spaces where workers can cool down during shifts,  
- adding points to facilities when cooling logs are submitted between shifts,  
that kind of direction.”

“...Additional points for submitting logs.”

Yuto felt a slight uneasiness.

“Recording the actual situation is important,  
but if only people who can write logs benefit, won’t that create another kind of inequality?”

“That is exactly the problem.”

WindArc nodded.

“People who can write. People who can organize information. People who are used to systems.  
Those people find it easier to access support.”

“If people need some kind of ability just to access support,  
then in the end we split people again into those who can use support and those who cannot use it easily.”

“Yes.  
That is why I want simplicity of procedure to be part of the support plan as well.”

Yuto looked at the misting device in the station plaza and thought.

Expanding support.  
Visible support.  
But the more support became visible, the more visible the procedural walls became too.

“For example,  
- support that is judged automatically,  
- break tickets that can be used on site,  
- temporary cooling spaces that require no application,  
things like that.  
Otherwise, people who need support but cannot use it will remain.”

“Exactly.”

WindArc lowered their voice slightly.

“And we also need support for people who are not working.  
Unemployment, childcare, caregiving, medical treatment.  
Workplace support alone is nowhere near enough.”

“And people who are between home and work too.”

“Yes.  
Stations, bus stops, parks, libraries, government offices.  
There are also proposals to expand support to places that can serve as ‘places to stay.’”

Yuto looked up at the nozzle of the misting device.

“The more support expands,  
the more important it becomes to ask what we even call support.”

“Yes.  
Not only electricity bill assistance, but places where people can cool down, places where they can sit, time when they can rest.  
We are finally starting to see all of that as support.”

On a station bench sat a parent with a child and an elderly person wiping away sweat.  
Someone stood up, and someone else sat down.  
Through that repetition, support was gradually spreading from money to place.

In the afternoon, Yuto returned to his apartment and wrote the day’s events in his notebook.

“Today’s events  
- Workplace and out-of-home support plans became more concrete.  
  → Support for occupations where it is hard to take breaks.  
  → Securing spaces where workers can cool down during shifts.  
  → Additional points for submitting cooling logs between shifts.  
- Talked with WindArc about the expansion of support and the people still left out.  
  → Support for people who are not working.  
  → Temporary cooling spaces that require no application.  
  → Automatic judgment, break tickets, and other ways to simplify procedures.  
- The meaning of support is expanding beyond electricity bill assistance to include places to stay and time to rest.”

“Today’s realizations  
- The more support expands, the more a new inequality appears: support that only people who can use it can use.  
- If log submission and application procedures become walls, the people who need support most are the ones most likely to be left behind.  
- As the meaning of support expands, the reach of the system changes as well, but the difficulty of drawing lines also increases.”

Finally, he added one line.

“Today’s line  
- The more support expands, the darker the shadows of places it still cannot reach become.”

When he checked his smartphone, his Cooling Credit had increased slightly.

“Earned today: 0.013 CC  
Total: 0.713 CC”

“So I passed 0.7.  
But today, the real feeling is not the number. It is the shadow of places support still cannot reach.”

The world was still hot.  
In that heat, support was trying to move beyond electricity bills toward people who worked, people who could not work, people who could not move, and people who had to stop and rest.  
But that expansion also made the outlines of new people being left out rise into view.

## Afterword

Thank you for reading Episode 34.

This episode depicts how, as workplace and out-of-home support plans become more concrete, a new structure appears: the more support expands, the more visible the people who still cannot enter it become.  
Yuto and WindArc recognize the need to expand break assistance and cooling spaces, while also confirming the importance of simplifying procedures so that only people who can submit logs or applications do not end up benefiting.

The major point is that the definition of support has expanded from electricity bill assistance to places to stay and time to rest.  
At the same time, the risk of leaving behind people who are not working, people who cannot move, and people who are not used to dealing with systems has become clear.

Part 3 will continue to deepen the question of how to fill the gap between the expansion of support and the shadows that remain beyond that expansion, and how companies and local governments handle those shadows.  
I hope you will continue following how Yuto records the shadows that become visible as support expands, and how he turns them into the next proposal.
