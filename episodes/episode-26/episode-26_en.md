# Episode 26: The Sound of a Support Notification Falling

[← Top / README](../../README.md)

Episode 26 is the first episode in Part 3 where the theme of support and inequality begins to take concrete shape.

The official version of Cooling Commons starts moving its daily-life support link, and the wording and timing of that notification create small misalignments and rough feelings among resident players.

---

## Main Text

Monday.

Soma Yuto tried to ignore the vibration of his phone while restocking plastic bottles in the back room of the convenience store.

The manager had told him that using a personal phone was fine as long as it did not bother customers.

But he was still on shift.

He heard the sound, but did not look at the screen.

He returned several bottles to their cases and felt the outside air through the gap in the shutter.

Heat, as always, seemed to be looking for somewhere to escape.

When his break finally came, Yuto took out his phone.

There was one unfamiliar notification on the screen.

> Notice regarding the Cooling Commons Daily-Life Support Link (trial):
>
> Target: resident players with Daily-Life Support Link Lv.1 or higher
>
> Details:
> - During the next predicted heat-wave peak period,
>   a trial will partially subsidize electricity charges for household cooling used between 1:00 p.m. and 4:00 p.m.
>
> - Detailed eligibility information will be sent separately.

“...So it came.”

The phrase he had seen in Episode 25, “under consideration,” had turned into a concrete time slot and a concrete measure.

“From one to four in the afternoon... That’s exactly the awkward time for people who need to move around during the day.”

Convenience-store shifts.

Walking through town.

Dropping off or picking up children at nursery school.

For many residents, midday was also a time when they had to be active.

A system that prioritized support for people who used cooling at home during that time looked rational at first glance. At the same time, it could also look as if people who did not move were the ones who benefited.

“Still, for elderly people who stay home during the day, or people working from home, it must be a big help.”

It half touched his own life, and half missed it.

That very incompleteness felt like the theme of Part 3.

Under the notification, there was a small note.

> Support slots are limited.
>
> Eligible users will be selected by comprehensively considering:
> - Cooling Commons contribution category
> - Daily-Life Support Link level
> - Residential environment, such as owned home, rental, or apartment building

“...This wording is the scariest part.”

Comprehensively considering.

Limited slots.

Selection.

Those three phrases were common in institutional documents. But from the viewpoint of residents, they also cast a shadow: who would not be selected?

As Yuto returned from the back room to the store, his phone vibrated again.

> Nearby player chat: new messages available

His break only had a few minutes left.

Standing in the shadow of the terminal beside the register, Yuto opened the screen just briefly.

> Bronze Player A:
> “The Daily-Life Support Link notification came, didn’t it?
> I’m Lv.0, so I guess I’m not eligible. It feels kind of complicated.”

> Bronze Player B:
> “I’m Lv.1, but I’m almost always outside during the day.
> So it feels helpful, but also kind of awkward...”

> Silver Player:
> “From what I hear, the institution is treating this as a ‘try it first and watch the reaction’ stage.
> Honestly, I think the support will hit unevenly.”

Yuto skimmed the log, and then his time was up.

He put the phone back in his pocket and returned to the register.

Afternoon.

When his shift ended, he stepped outside.

The heat had passed its peak slightly, but it was still heavy.

On his way toward the shopping street, he opened the chat again.

> WindArc:
> “You’re Lv.1 for the support link too, right, Soma?”

> Yuto:
> “Looks like it.
> But I’m usually outside during that time, so I’m not sure whether I can really use it.”

> WindArc:
> “Right.
> In my case, I’m in a restaurant kitchen, so household cooling isn’t really the issue in the first place...”

From the institutional side, this might be a reasonable first attempt at daily-life support.

But when compared against the lives of actual players, the way it landed varied a lot.

> Rank Hidden:
> “This Daily-Life Support Link is only the first test stone.
> The premise is to see who it reaches, what kinds of discomfort and gratitude appear, and then adjust little by little.”

> Bronze Player A:
> “I understand that, but if they clearly say, ‘You aren’t eligible because you haven’t contributed enough,’ it would honestly feel pretty rough.”

> Silver Player:
> “I think the institution will avoid saying it that way.
> But there will probably be situations where it looks that way in practice.”

Yuto sat down in the shade of the shopping street and let out a small breath.

“If support and contribution start looking like the score screen of a cooling game, that would be pretty bad...”

Numbers.

Levels.

Categories.

They were beginning to connect directly to who could spend time in a cool room and who had to endure the heat.

In the evening, when Yuto returned to his apartment, the room still held some of the heat from the day.

Before turning on the air conditioner, he opened ThermoBalance once.

> Current residence:
> Temperature: 30.8°C
> Heat stress index: medium to high
> Daily-Life Support Link status:
> - Trial slot candidate: provisionally registered
> Final support recipients will be determined together with other conditions.

“‘Provisionally registered’ is an amazingly unsettling phrase...”

He was a candidate, but not yet a recipient.

He might be selected. He might not.

Yuto turned on the air conditioner at his usual setting.

Whether he received support or not, he had no choice but to use it in this heat.

As the cool air began to push through the room, his phone vibrated once more.

> Message from the Cooling Commons Design Team (excerpt):
>
> Regarding the trial electricity subsidy for players with Daily-Life Support Link Lv.1,
> we have added a form for discomfort reports concerning bias in support slots.
>
> Feedback from both:
> - people who receive support
> - people who do not receive support
> will be treated as evaluation material.

“They’re starting support with a discomfort form attached from the beginning. That’s kind of impressive...”

The design assumed from the start that both gratitude and dissatisfaction would be gathered.

It could be called sincere. It could also be called the suffocating feeling of a world where everything becomes a log.

Yuto opened his notebook and began writing down the day.

> Today’s events
> - A concrete notice arrived for the Cooling Commons Daily-Life Support Link trial.
>   - A proposal to subsidize household cooling electricity fees between 1:00 p.m. and 4:00 p.m. during a heat-wave peak.
>   - Selection will comprehensively consider contribution category, support link level, residential environment, and other factors.
>
> - In the player chat:
>   - Lv.0 players felt the roughness of being “outside the target.”
>   - Some Lv.1 players felt that it was “helpful but awkward” depending on lifestyle.
>   - The design side explained it as a first test stone, assuming bias from the start.
>
> - My room became a provisional candidate for a support slot.
>   - I confirmed again that I have to use the air conditioner regardless of whether support actually arrives.

> Today’s realization
> - When support and contribution begin to connect, a game-like score display can easily enter daily life.
> - The first support trial is not only about collecting data on who was helped, but also on who felt left behind.
> - Support and inequality affect not only institutions but also the atmosphere inside the player community, so here too the question of lines matters: how far to connect things, and where to separate them.

Finally, he wrote his usual single line.

> Today’s line
> - The sound of a support notification falling brings both relief and roughness with it.
>   Watching and writing both of them down is becoming my role in Part 3.

When he checked his phone, today’s Cooling Credit had increased only a little.

> Earned today: 0.013 CC
> Total: 0.609 CC

“The cooling number is as usual.

But the number that says where support will fall is still invisible.”

Yuto closed his notebook and weakened the air conditioner fan slightly.

The world was still hot.

And inside that heat, the question of support, contribution, and inequality was quietly beginning to sit down at the table of daily life, in the form of notifications, chats, and discomfort forms.

## Afterword

Thank you for reading Episode 26, Part 3 Episode 2.

This episode showed:

- the official version of Cooling Commons sending a concrete electricity subsidy proposal through its Daily-Life Support Link,
- the way that support creates three feelings among players at once: gratitude, awkwardness, and the roughness of being outside the target,
- and the institution designing the support from the beginning as a test stone that assumes bias and discomfort.

In Part 3, the themes of support, contribution, and inequality will deepen through:

- where support actually lands,
- how people feel about where it lands,
- and how those voices feed back into the institution.

From the next episodes onward, the story will explore:

- how people who receive support and people who do not receive support experience changes and discomfort in their lives,
- how companies try to use support and contribution scores for marketing,
- and where Yuto himself chooses to stand: as someone who receives support, someone who speaks about support, or someone who proposes lines around support.
