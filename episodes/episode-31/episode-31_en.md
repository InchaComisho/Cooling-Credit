# Episode 31: The Meeting Room of How Things Are Used

[← Top / README](../../README.md)

Episode 31 shows the first time Yuto directly faces how support records are being used after companies have begun to make use of them.  
This time, he does not merely record his discomfort. He speaks a little more directly to the corporate representatives about exactly what feels wrong.

---

## Main Text

Sunday.

Soma Yuto stopped for a moment in front of a rental meeting room at the edge of the shopping street.

Today was a joint briefing session held by the regional corporate liaison council and the Cooling Commons design team.
Its stated purpose was “sharing support records and exchanging opinions toward next year’s regional cooling measures.”

In other words, it was a meeting about how far support records could be used by companies.

Yuto had been invited by WindArc.
WindArc had told him, “I think the voices of resident players are necessary, so please come if you can.”

“A meeting room, huh. It’s been a while...”

A simple reception desk stood in front of the door.
From inside, Yuto could hear the sound of presentation equipment and the dry hum of air conditioning.

When he sat down, large text was projected on the screen at the front.

“Cooling Commons Support Record Sharing Session  
Theme: Synergy Between Regional Value and Cooling Measures”

“Synergy, huh.”

Yuto frowned slightly at that word.

The meeting began.
A corporate representative began lining up the results from the support record area.

“After the introduction of the daily-life support link, average staying time in the station-front shopping street increased.”
“Recognition of the phrase ‘Take a Breath Here’ improved, and more passersby slowed down or stopped.”
“This initiative has also contributed to improving the regional brand, and visitor surveys have shown favorable responses.”

The slides were blue and white, easy to read.
The graphs were clean.
The numbers looked polished.

But Yuto immediately sensed that the “summer that did not receive support” was absent from them.

WindArc was quietly taking notes in the seat next to him.

Once the explanation reached a pause, the meeting moved into questions and comments.

“We would now like to hear opinions from resident players.”

The eyes in the room shifted slightly toward Yuto’s side.
Yuto slowly raised his hand.

“Yes. I’m Soma.”

The room grew a little quieter.

“The materials for today’s support record sharing were very easy to understand. But what bothered me was that only the positive results were emphasized: longer staying times, favorable responses, and so on. I could hardly see the feelings of people who did not receive support, or people who were outside the target range.”

Several people looked up.

“It is true that the lives of people who received support became a little easier. But at the same time, people who did not receive support remained in the same heat. That difference is hard to see from survey numbers alone.”

One of the corporate representatives at the front leaned forward slightly.

“Of course, we recognize that point. However, this sharing session is also a place to summarize positive outcomes...”

“I think it is fine to treat them as positive outcomes.”

Yuto continued without cutting off his own thought.

“But if only the positive outcomes are extracted, the emotions of people who did not receive support, and the feeling of being left outside the system, disappear. That is what bothers me the most.”

A slight stir moved through the room.
WindArc nodded quietly beside him.

Yuto continued.

“For example, the phrase ‘support record area’ does not only indicate a place where support happened. It is also a place where there were people who could not receive support. If that part is erased, it may become a brand, but the difference in daily life becomes invisible.”

For a moment, the screen at the front seemed to freeze.

The corporate representative thought for a moment before answering.

“Thank you for pointing that out. We intended to show the improvement of the region as a whole. But it is true that the perspective of who was left behind may have been insufficient.”

Yuto felt a little of the tension leave his shoulders.

“Thank you. There is one more thing.”

Everyone in the room looked at him.

“I do not think it is entirely wrong to use support records for next measures or even for publicity. But when you do that, please do not select only favorable voices. I want the uncomfortable voices and the emotions of people who did not receive support to be included as part of how support actually worked.”

The air in the meeting room hardened slightly.

But WindArc opened his mouth as if to bridge the gap.

“The design side has the same understanding. We believe the way results are shown and the differences that remain should not be separated.”

Several people nodded.
The representative also took notes and nodded.

After that, the meeting continued. They discussed how support records might be used, how they should be publicized, and how resident logs should be handled.
But inside Yuto, one feeling remained.

“I said it in a meeting room...”

In clear words.
To the people in front of him.
What was missing.

After the meeting ended, WindArc smiled faintly in the hallway outside.

“That worked quite well.”

“Did it?”

“Yes. I think the line about treating support records not as advertising, but as records that show differences in daily life, stayed with everyone.”

Yuto felt a little embarrassed and looked away.

“Well, I don’t think showing the results itself is wrong. It’s more about how the way you show them can make some things disappear.”

“That is exactly why saying it matters.”

Outside the meeting room, the afternoon heat was still strong.
Even so, the air felt a little lighter than it had inside.

On the way home, Yuto looked up at the poster in the shopping street.

“Support Record Area”

The words were still there.
But they looked a little different now.

“If only the differences could be written next to the achievements.”

Thinking that, he returned to his apartment.

When he opened his notebook, the events of the day came together naturally.

“Today’s Events
- Attended a joint briefing session by the regional corporate liaison council and the Cooling Commons design team
  → Confirmed the scene where support records were being treated as regional value
  → Pointed out that materials emphasizing outcomes hardly included the feelings of people who did not receive support or were outside the target range
  → Said that support records should be treated as records that show differences in daily life

- WindArc supported the meeting by adding context and organizing the discussion
  → The concern over selecting only favorable voices was also shared.”

“Today’s Realizations
- Support records become attractive material for companies
- But depending on how they are shown, the feelings and inequalities of those who did not receive support can disappear
- Putting what is missing into words in a meeting room is a cooling action different from filing a discomfort report.”

Finally, he added one line.

“Today’s Line
- The key point in Part 3 is not just praising the achievements of support, but whether the remaining differences can be spoken aloud in the meeting room.”

When he checked his phone, his Cooling Credit had increased a little.

“Earned today: 0.014 CC  
Total: 0.677 CC”

“More than the number, what matters today is that I managed to say it in the meeting room.”

The world was still hot.
But within that heat, Yuto had touched the way support was being used for the first time in an actual meeting room, and he had placed the differences that were about to be made invisible directly in front of the people there.

## Afterword

Thank you for reading Episode 31.

This episode showed Yuto attending a joint briefing session after support records had begun to be used by the regional corporate liaison council, and directly pointing out the differences hidden behind the results.
The important point here is that Yuto did not merely submit his discomfort as a report. He said, in the meeting room itself, that support records should be treated not as advertising, but as records that show differences in daily life.

With this, a new viewpoint emerges alongside Part 3’s existing themes of support and inequality, and systems and daily life: who can correct the way support itself is used, where, and how?
WindArc’s role in organizing the meeting and adding context also showed how the voice reaching the room was not just an individual feeling, but something closer to a community voice.

In the next episode, the story will follow how much of this meeting is reflected, and how the corporate side adjusts the way it presents the results.
Please continue to follow what Yuto notices after that one sentence he managed to say in the meeting room.
