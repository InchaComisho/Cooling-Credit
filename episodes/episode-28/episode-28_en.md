# Episode 28: An Afternoon with Support

[← Top / README](../../README.md)

Episode 28 shows Yuto actually spending the day on which support has been granted, and recording both the practical relief and the discomfort that comes with it.

Through Yuto's day, it depicts how the Cooling Commons daily-life support link touches the temperature of ordinary living, not merely as a notification, but as something felt in the room itself.

---

## Main Text

Thursday.

Soma Yuto had been slightly restless since morning.

From 1 p.m. to 4 p.m. today.

The test slot for the Cooling Commons daily-life support link would actually begin operating.

"The household cooling electricity subsidy will apply today from 13:00 to 16:00.

Eligible device: one air conditioner in the living room

Subsidy cap: a fixed percentage of the electricity consumed during the eligible time period today"

He had already looked at the notification, which had arrived that morning, more times than he wanted to admit.

"It is not like anything suddenly changes just because there is support."

Even so, the psychological catch he usually felt about turning on the air conditioner in the afternoon was a little weaker.

After finishing his morning shift and returning home, the room was holding a little heat.

The heat-blocking sheet by the window was working in its usual slow, subtle way.

He picked up the air conditioner remote.

Exactly 1 p.m.

He turned it on.

Cool air began to flow, slowly pushing back the heat that had gathered along the wall.

"Support application start confirmed.

Today's cooling usage will be recorded."

"Yes, yes. Recorded."

Yuto said it with a half-smile and turned on the fan.

It carried the cool air deeper into the room.

He did not close the window completely, leaving a very small route for ventilation.

"Not because there is support, I am going to make it stronger.

Just as an extension of the usual way, I use it with a little more peace of mind."

That feeling mattered to him.

He sat at the desk and opened his notebook.

He organized today's usage in the same format as the preliminary log.

"13:00-14:00

- Rest after the morning shift
- Indoor temperature decreased from around 31°C to around 28°C
- Used together with a fan
- Direct sunlight blocking at the window maintained

14:00-15:00

- Drafted a report
- Rehydrated
- Cooling load remained stable

15:00-16:00

- Took a light nap
- Physically, it felt much easier
- Because support was available, I felt I did not have to keep enduring any more"

His hand stopped slightly after writing the final line.

"I did not have to keep enduring any more, huh."

That was not only about the effect of the air conditioner.

It meant that, because there was support, the part of him that had unconsciously been pushing himself too hard had loosened a little.

But at the same time—

"Discomfort while support was applied today:

- I am able to use cooling, but people who have not received support are in the same heat
- This sense of relief also feels as if it stands on top of a sensory gap with someone else
- Being inside a cooled room makes the heat outside feel even more vivid in my imagination"

After writing that much, Yuto let out a breath.

"That part really cannot be erased."

Receiving support had made things easier.

That fact was true.

But if that ease made the people who had not received support invisible, then something was wrong.

A little after 2 p.m.

His smartphone vibrated.

"Notice for post-support log for support recipients:

Please summarize today's support experience in three points:

- What was helpful
- What felt uncomfortable
- What you would like next time"

"Three points, huh."

It was almost exactly what he had already been thinking about.

In the cooled room of the afternoon, Yuto quietly began shaping his words.

"What was helpful:

- Rest after the morning shift became much easier
- The psychological hesitation around using cooling decreased
- I was able to concentrate on notebook work

Discomfort:

- While the support was being applied, I felt as if only I had become a little lighter
- When that lightness is placed beside the heat of people outside the support target, it feels a little rough
- I am grateful that support connects to daily-life security, but the feeling of inequality does not disappear

What I would like next time:

- I want the voices of people who were outside the support target to be treated with the same weight
- It would be good to have separate support not only for household cooling, but also for heat countermeasures at workplaces and while out of the home
- It would be good to have a mechanism that prevents people who receive support from overlooking the feelings of those who did not"

By the time he finished writing, the room temperature had settled into a comfortable range.

Compared with an ordinary summer day, it was just a little easier to breathe.

In the evening, before his next shift, he went out to the shopping street.

It was still hot as ever.

But today, that heat looked a little different.

Support had reached his room.

But there were places in the city where support had not reached.

When he walked while knowing that difference, the scenery changed.

The stair landing at the station.

The round sticker that said, "Take a breath here."

Beneath it, a student who looked like a junior high schooler was retying a shoelace.

Yuto passed by without saying anything.

He simply slowed his pace a little.

"Today's total daily-life cooling:

- Indoor cooling support applied
- Stop-and-rest guidance during outing
- Increased number of rests in my own room"

At night, after returning to his apartment, he officially submitted the post-support log.

"What was helpful:

- Effective for recovery after the morning shift
- Made it easier to decide to use cooling
- Improved work efficiency

Discomfort:

- The feeling that only I had become lighter
- The sensory difference from people who did not receive support
- The fact that support does not end with simply being 'nice'

What I would like next time:

- Separate support for people outside the current support target
- Expansion of support to workplaces and places outside the home
- A mechanism that allows support recipients to put invisible inequality into words and leave it behind"

After sending it, a little tension left his shoulders.

"Well, I guess writing this properly is my role in Part 3."

A short receipt notification came back on his smartphone.

"Post-support log received.

The support experience and discomfort will be treated as reference material for the next design stage."

Yuto added one final line to his notebook.

"Today's line:

When support reaches you, life becomes a little easier.

But how you return that ease in words is also part of support."

When he checked the smartphone display, his Cooling Credit had barely changed.

"Earned today: 0.010 CC

Total: 0.636 CC"

"The numbers did not move much.

But the felt temperature moved a lot today."

The world was still hot.

But by actually spending an afternoon with support, Yuto had become able to put into words, even if only a little, the feeling that gratitude and the roughness of inequality could coexist in the same room.

## Afterword

Thank you for reading Episode 28.

This episode focused on how Yuto spent the afternoon when the daily-life support link test slot actually began operating and support reached him.

What mattered here was that he honestly accepted the support as something helpful, while also properly leaving behind the feeling that only he had become lighter and the sensory difference from people the support had not reached.

Through this episode, the central theme of Part 3, support and inequality, becomes concrete not as an abstract institutional issue, but as the temperature of a room between 1 p.m. and 4 p.m., the ease of resting, and the contrast with the heat outside.

Also, by proposing in the post-support log that recipients of support should be able to put invisible inequality into words and leave it behind, Yuto strengthens his position not only as someone who receives support, but also as someone who returns his voice to the design of support.

From here, Part 3 will dig further into the discomfort of those who did not receive support, the ways companies use that support, and how distortions in the system become visible.

I hope you will continue following how Yuto puts both gratitude and roughness into words without erasing either one.
