# Episode 15: Wind from the Meeting Room

[← Top / README](../../README.md)

In Episode 15, the results of the COOL STREET design meeting attended by WindArc begin to return gradually to Yuto's town.

At the same time, fragments of institutional uncertainty over how Cooling Commons should be formally positioned reach Yuto through logs and messages, and he begins to vaguely consider how such questions might ultimately be settled.

---

Friday.

Soma Yuto ate toast while using the morning news as background noise.

The television displayed messages such as, “Heatstroke transport cases remain high this month.”

The tone made it sound like someone else's problem.

But the actual heat was not someone else's problem.

The phone on the table vibrated softly.

“The first COOL STREET design meeting has concluded.  
A summary will be shared with nearby players.”

“That must be the meeting WindArc mentioned yesterday.”

An institutional meeting.

A place where site staff, municipal officials, the shopping street association, and cooling-system representatives gathered.

One of Yuto's sentences had been carried into that room.

“My town became a test field for the system.”

He could not imagine what expression those words had worn as they moved around the meeting room.

He opened the notification details.

“COOL STREET Design Meeting No. 1 — Summary Excerpt:

- Based on existing resident logs, the following provisional guideline draft was created:  
  1. Shade zones and bench positions should be decided by comparing them with daily-life logs.  
  2. Informal signs such as ‘It is a little cooler here’ should coexist with official signs whenever possible.  
  3. In shopping-street route design, priority should be given to making ‘easier routes’ visible rather than merely showing shortcuts.

- Regarding the Cooling Commons trial framework:  
  - A proposal was made to explicitly credit phrases quoted from resident reports in project documents. However, concerns were raised about how much personal identification should be shown and what level of return should be provided.  
  - Discussion will continue in future meetings.”

“…They're actually using the guideline draft.”

The first point was exactly what he had been doing.

Reviewing the relationship between shade and bench positions through resident logs.

The second point was strangely specific.

“‘Informal signs should coexist’ is completely about our handwritten papers.”

“It is a little cooler here.”

“Sitting zone.”

“On hot days, please rest in the shade.”

Yuto had imagined that when institutions entered, such things would be the first to be removed.

But the proposed guideline explicitly moved in the direction of allowing them to coexist.

“So they really discussed things like this in the meeting room.”

Then came the third point.

Making easier routes visible instead of shortcuts.

It was an extension of what Yuto had done on school routes and in rear passages.

“So even from the system side, showing an easier route has meaning.”

His eyes moved to the section about Cooling Commons.

A proposal for crediting phrases.

Concerns.

Continued discussion.

“Of course it isn't going to be decided that easily…”

How much of a person's name should be shown?

At what level should value be returned?

Those questions could not be settled simply through goodwill.

Responsibility.

Expectations.

Disputes.

Many elements were entangled.

“But the fact that it remained on the agenda is already pretty significant.”

If no one had said anything, the issue would never even have reached the table.

ShiroReflect's concerns, WindArc's comments, and Yuto's own discomfort had overlapped until the topic finally became something that could be discussed.

He still had a little time before his convenience-store shift, so Yuto headed to the shopping street.

Street trees.

The bench.

The COOL STREET poster.

The combination had not changed much since yesterday.

But some details were different.

A small, unobtrusive plate had been added near the foot of the bench.

“This bench was positioned with reference to resident logs.  
(COOL STREET trial in progress)”

“…Seriously?”

It felt as though the words once written at the edge of the blueprint had appeared at the edge of the town itself.

Resident logs.

Placement.

Trial.

Yuto reached a hand into the bench's shade.

The shadow fell at a position that covered roughly up to a seated person's knees.

His phone vibrated.

“COOL STREET provisional guideline: Bench placement rule (trial)  
This location was arranged with reference to the following logs:  
- Shopping-street tree rest-point log (Soma Yuto)  
- Cooperative bench-position adjustment log (anonymous player)”

His name appeared in small letters on the screen.

“So it actually says, ‘We used this as a reference.’”

It was a tiny credit line that no one in the world would notice unless they approached and looked into the screen.

But Yuto could see it.

He could confirm that the town's bench had been placed using his daily-life log as material.

“For now, this level of credit might be just right.”

His name did not need to be printed in huge letters on a sign.

But inside the system, the connection remained visible.

An elderly man sitting beside him leaned toward the plate.

“Resident log…?”

He read the words quietly.

“I don't really know what it means, but if the bench is easier to sit on now, that's fine.”

He crossed his legs again.

That one sentence made Yuto feel strangely relieved.

“Right.  
People don't need to understand everything.”

Who wrote the log.

What happened in the meeting.

What Cooling Commons meant.

People did not need to know every detail.

For some, it was enough that the place was easier to sit in and cooler.

Even so, the relationships behind it remained within the system instead of disappearing.

When Yuto headed to the riverside entrance, he found another small change.

A tiny QR code had been added to the edge of the sign.

“This tree-shade usage idea is part of the Cooling Commons trial framework.”

“So they finally started naming it…”

Yuto scanned the QR code.

A browser-like screen opened, displaying a Cooling Idea Sharing Test Page.

“Cooling Commons (Beta)  
Case: Tree-shade guidance at the riverside entrance

Overview:  
- The case began with a tree-shade guidance log created by a resident player.  
- It developed through cooperative actions by multiple players and the installation of a municipal sign.  
- It has now reached its current trial-operation stage.

Sources:  
- Daily-life cooling log, ‘Riverside Entrance Tree-Shade Guidance’ (Soma Yuto)  
- Cooperative log, ‘Sitting Zone’ (WindArc and others)

Return of value:  
- At present, a very small portion of cooling results is being returned experimentally to the source logs.  
*Formal return rules remain under discussion.”

“…So this is it.”

Words, names, and the handling of returns.

All of them had been gathered onto one page.

It was not perfect.

The phrase “under discussion” directly reflected the uncertainty on the institutional side.

But his name appeared as part of an official document.

“Probably only a small number of people will ever view this page.”

People willing to scan the QR code.

People interested in Cooling Commons.

Institutional participants.

Even so, the page existed.

Its existence felt like a small promise.

Yuto returned to his apartment and sat at his desk.

He opened his notebook and wrote down the day's events.

“Today's events:  
- Summary of the first COOL STREET design meeting  
  → Resident-log references explicitly included in the bench-placement rule  
  → Coexistence with informal signs included in the guideline draft  
  → Visibility of ‘easier routes’ adopted as an official design term  
- Cooling Commons (Beta) page published for the riverside entrance  
  → My logs and WindArc's logs listed as part of the case  
  → Return rules still ‘under discussion’”

“Under discussion…”

The phrase was frustrating, but also reassuring.

Because “not yet decided” also meant “still possible to change.”

“Today's observations:  
- Decisions made in meeting rooms descend into the town as plates and QR codes  
- Resident logs and words are beginning to appear not only in blueprints, but also in explanations for citizens  
- The ‘under discussion’ state of Cooling Commons is also space where both discomfort and expectation can enter”

He stopped his pen once, then added another line.

“Today's line:  
- The wind from the meeting room is surprisingly slow, but it really does reach the town.”

He checked his cumulative Cooling Credit.

“Earned today: 0.022 CC  
Cumulative total: 0.427 CC”

“A little closer to 0.5.”

Numbers, systems, and towns did not change immediately.

But they were certainly moving one step at a time.

“I still don't know where I'll be when Cooling Commons becomes formal…”

Yuto smiled slightly as he closed the notebook.

“But at least I want to keep watching how the wind from the meeting room reaches the town.”

The world was still hot.

Yet the bench's shade, a small plate, and a single QR code had mixed a little new wind into that heat.
