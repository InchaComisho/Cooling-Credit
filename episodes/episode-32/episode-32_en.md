# Episode 32: The Day the Presentation Changed

[← Top / README](../../README.md)

Episode 32 shows how the one sentence Yuto managed to say in the meeting room begins to be reflected in the next design of support.  
This time, the corporate side’s adjustment of how the results are presented and the resident side’s effort to put the remaining gaps into words both begin to move within the same week.

---

## Main Text

Monday.  
After finishing his morning shift at the convenience store, Soma Yuto walked through the shopping street as usual.

The words he had spoken in the meeting room yesterday still remained in his mind.

“Support records should not be treated as advertising, but as records that show differences in people’s lives.”

How far had those words reached?  
He would have been lying if he said he was not curious.

The poster on the bulletin board in front of the station looked a little different from the day before.

“Cooling Commons Support Record Area  
In this shopping street, the trial operation of the daily-life support link has confirmed:  
・Improved cooling use during heat-wave peak hours  
・Longer bench-stay times  
・Expanded recognition of the ‘Take a breath here’ slogan.”

The heading was the same.  
But a small note had been added at the bottom.

“*These results are records that include the voices of both residents who received support and residents who were outside the support target.  
The differences created by support are still under continued review.”

“...So it got in.”

Yuto let out a small breath.

Honestly, it was only one line.  
Even so, the words “residents who were outside the support target” being included mattered.

A new QR code had also been added beside the poster.

“Design background of the support record area”  
“Voices of those who received support and those who did not”  
“Key points from Cooling Commons discomfort reports”

“...They separated it.”

The presentation had changed.  
It was no longer an advertisement made only of results. It had become a structure where the background, the gaps, and the discomfort could be read side by side.

That was probably proof that the point made in the meeting had worked at least a little.

Then a voice came from behind him.

“Did you see it?”

It was WindArc.

“I saw it.  
They added the note.”

“Yes.  
It seems the corporate side decided once to change how they present it.”

“That was quick.”

“It was.  
If they are going to use it in business materials, showing that they are properly looking at the gaps makes a better impression now than bluntly showing only the results.”

Yuto gave a small wry smile at that phrasing.

“So in the end, it’s still about presentation...”

“But even a change in presentation can sometimes make gaps that remained there easier to see.”

Yuto looked up at the poster.

“Including the voices of residents who were outside the support target.”

Just that one line changed how the whole poster looked.  
At the very least, it no longer said, “Everything is going well.”

“This is still better.”

“Yes.  
But the people outside the support still do not become more comfortable just because of this, so the real work begins from here.”

“The real work...”

WindArc took out a phone and showed him the screen.

“Regional Corporate Liaison Memo (Updated)  
・Adjust how support records are presented  
・Use transparency that includes the voices of people outside the target as an appeal point  
・In the next public messaging, consider touching not only on the benefits of support but also on design issues.”

“‘Transparency as an appeal point,’ huh...”

“Well, they are companies.”

Yuto was a little exasperated, but he understood.

“Still, if they are willing to appeal transparency, it becomes easier to talk than before.”

“That is true.  
At least they are not pretending the discomfort reports never existed.”

They left the shopping street and sat on a bench near the station.  
There was shade, but heat still rose from the ground.

“But changing how it is shown does not make people who did not receive support feel better that same day, does it?”

“No.  
That is exactly why the next stage of support is necessary.”

“The next stage?”

“For example:  
・Cooling support for workplaces and places people go outside their homes  
・Alternative support for those who were outside the target  
・Support by place of stay, not only by household cooling systems.”

“...That becomes a much bigger issue.”

“Yes.  
But otherwise it stops at ‘only the people who received support became a little more comfortable.’”

Yuto folded his arms on the bench.

“Changing the presentation of support is the first step.  
But unless the range of support actually expands, the rough feeling of inequality remains.”

“Exactly.”

In the afternoon, Yuto walked farther through the shopping street.  
He scanned the newly added QR code and opened the design background page.

The page contained the following text.

“About the expression policy for support record areas  
・Adjusting from a structure that places only results at the front to a structure that also makes residents’ differences and institutional issues visible  
・Explicitly addressing ‘insufficient return’ and ‘the emotions of those outside the target’ pointed out in discomfort reports  
・Using transparency not as a justification for support, but as a step toward the next institutional improvement.”

“...They went this far?”

It was considerably more honest than before.  
At the very least, the direction was no longer to make everything look clean only.

Yuto felt slightly relieved.

But behind that relief, another question remained.

“Even if they write this much,  
it does not erase the heat felt by people who did not receive support.”

When he returned to his apartment, he opened his notebook on the desk.

“Today’s events  
・A note was added to the shopping street support record poster stating that it includes the voices of residents who were outside the support target  
・The QR code now allows people to read the design background of the support records and the key points of the discomfort reports  
・The corporate side adjusted the presentation and began using transparency as an appeal point  

・From WindArc, shared the need for the next stage of support:  
　→ Cooling support for workplaces and places outside the home  
　→ Alternative support for those who were outside the target  
　→ Place-based support.”

“Today’s realization  
・When the way something is presented changes, gaps that had become invisible can become visible again  
・However, stronger transparency alone does not eliminate inequality itself  
・In Part 3, it is necessary to think separately about how support is shown and how support is expanded.”

Finally, he wrote one line.

“Today’s line  
・Even if changing the presentation makes it look like a step forward, the summer of those who did not receive support is not over yet.”

His Cooling Credit had increased only slightly.

“Earned today: 0.011 CC  
Total: 0.688 CC”

“The numbers are as usual.  
But today was definitely the day the presentation changed.”

The world was still hot.  
In the middle of that heat, the support record poster had become just a little more honest, and as Yuto watched that change, he began to feel that what was needed next was not transparency, but expansion.

## Afterword

Thank you for reading Episode 32.

This episode showed how the words spoken in the previous meeting were reflected in a small way, as a note was added to the support record poster stating that it includes the voices of residents who were outside the support target.  
The focus here was the corporate side adjusting its presentation and shifting slightly from an advertisement made only of results toward an explanation that also shows gaps and issues.

However, the important point is that this change is an improvement in presentation, not a resolution of inequality.  
After seeing that gap, Yuto and WindArc recognize that what is needed next is support by workplace, outside destination, and place of stay.

In Part 3, the story will continue digging into what remains insufficient after transparency increases.  
I hope you will continue following what Yuto records without overlooking, inside a city whose presentation has begun to change.
