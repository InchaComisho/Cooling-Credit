# Episode 24: Going to Check the Effect of Words

[← Top / README](../../README.md)

Episode 24 takes place immediately after Yuto has turned his discomfort into official words, and shows how the effects and side effects of those words slowly return to his daily life.

Yuto learns that his report has begun to be handled by the design side, and then goes back into the city to check what has changed and what still has not.

---

## Main Text

Saturday evening.  
Soma Yuto stared at the notebook left open on the desk in his apartment.

“I understand that it was not stolen, and I admit that it is still not enough.  
Putting both of those things into words and handing them over was one kind of cooling I could do right now.”

It was the line he had written yesterday.  
It was his own summary after submitting a discomfort report to Cooling Commons.

“...Then I should probably go see how far those words I handed over are working.”

He could not settle down after simply writing and sending them.  
Just like cooling that had been turned into numbers, “cooling through words” would not feel real unless he checked it on-site.

Yuto picked up his phone and opened ThermoBalance.

“Total Cooling Credit: 0.580 CC”

“I don’t need to move around heavily today either.  
Let’s call it a day to check the effect.”

He drank some barley tea and went outside.

The alley leading to the shopping street was as hot as usual.  
Still, compared with before, the intention to cool the city had become a little more visible.

Street trees.  
Benches.  
Signs.  
Posters.

He stopped in front of one of them, the poster that read “Take a Breath Here.”

The slogan was the same as yesterday.  
The credit line said, “Cooperation: COOL STREET Executive Committee.”

“Visually, nothing has changed yet.”

Submitting a discomfort report did not mean that personal names would appear the next day.  
It did not mean credit points would suddenly turn into life support.

Yuto watched the people passing in front of the poster.

A parent and child returning from shopping.  
An elderly person carrying bags.  
A student walking while looking at a phone.

Among them, a few people traced the words “Take a Breath Here” with their eyes and slowed their pace slightly.

They did not completely stop.  
But for one moment, their movements seemed to say, “Oh, it’s okay to rest here.”

His phone vibrated.

“Behavioral change from viewing the shopping street poster ‘Take a Breath Here’ (trial measurement):  
・Passersby who slowed down after seeing the poster: approximately 12% of total  
・Bench and shade usage: slight increase”

“The numbers are small...  
But it is working.”

Yuto felt both the roughness in his chest and the movement in front of him at the same time.

“For these people, it doesn’t matter who said it.”

What mattered was that the words “Take a Breath Here” were written there.  
What mattered was that, in the heat, people could see those words and let their feet slow down.

When he saw that effect, the discomfort over the absence of names drifted a little farther away.

But it did not disappear completely.

“The words work.  
But you still can’t see whose life they came from.  
I guess I have no choice but to remember both at the same time.”

Yuto passed through the shopping street and headed toward the station.

On the landing of the stairs, his round sticker was still there.

The small handwritten “Take a Breath Here” was completely unofficial, unlike the poster.

Not everyone noticed it.  
But people tying their shoelaces or adjusting their bags had started to pause in that spot more often.

His phone vibrated again.

“Station stair landing log updated:  
・People who stopped on the landing after seeing the round sticker: +9% compared with the previous week  
・Average dwell time: +1.7 seconds”

“This place has traces with a name.”

The poster had no name.  
The round sticker had the handwriting that only he could recognize as his own.

“The outside face is anonymous contribution.  
The inside face is a small trace of myself.  
For now, all I can do is look at that two-layer structure.”

At that moment, a new message arrived from the system.

“Notice from the Cooling Commons design team:  
The main points of your discomfort report, ‘On the nameless Take a Breath Here poster,’ have been reflected in design meeting materials.

Reflected points (excerpt):  
・The need to add not only ‘whether something is appropriation,’ but also ‘whether sufficient return is being provided’ as an evaluation axis  
・The possibility that resident players may become exhausted if anonymous contribution continues  
・The proposal that internal credit should be examined on the premise of linkage with life support”

“...It really made it into the meeting.”

Yuto stared at the screen.

“It is not appropriation.  
But it is not enough.  
That expression is becoming language on the design side as it is.”

It would not change the city’s scenery right away.  
But it would slightly change the frame of what criteria the system was built on.

Before taking the bus from the station area to the riverbank, Yuto operated his phone for a moment.

There had been a small change on the internal credit page.

“Word Contribution Points:  
・Contribution to the ‘Take a Breath Here’ slogan: +3  
・Contribution through discomfort report: +2

Total: +5  
※ Life support link under review”

“Just because the points increased doesn’t mean I get anything right now...”

Even so, at least a mark remained showing that the act of writing the discomfort itself had been evaluated.

“It’s not ‘complaining gives you points.’  
It’s more like ‘putting necessary discomfort into words increases the system’s judgment axes.’”

When he reached the riverbank entrance, he saw the COOL WATER sign and the words, “Please rest in the shade of the trees.”

A similar air to “Take a Breath Here” drifted there too.

In the shade, a runner returning from a run and someone out for a walk were sitting down.  
The three-layer structure of sign, paper, and tree shade remained unchanged.

Yuto watched the scene and traced the inside of his chest with words.

“The effect is there.  
The roughness is there too.  
I have no choice but to log both as they are.”

He returned to his apartment and opened his notebook that night.

“Today’s events  
・Confirmed that the shopping street poster ‘Take a Breath Here’ is causing small changes in walking speed and bench use  
・Confirmed that the round sticker on the station stair landing is also slowly influencing stopping behavior  
・The main points of the discomfort report were reflected in design meeting materials, and the ‘return’ evaluation axis became a formal subject of review”

“Today’s realizations  
・Slogans and words can certainly change resident behavior even when no individual name appears  
・At the same time, the discomfort of not being able to see whose life the words came from is necessary as an axis for system design  
・By putting discomfort into writing and handing it over, a state that is ‘not appropriation, but still lacks return’ is beginning to gain an official name and evaluation axis”

Finally, one line.

“Today’s line  
・The fact that words made someone a little more comfortable, and the fact that not enough has yet returned to me.  
Going to check both of them is how I walk through cooling right now.”

When he checked his phone, the day’s number was small again.

“Earned today: 0.014 CC  
Total: 0.594 CC”

“The numbers are moving little by little.  
But the words moved a bit more today.”

The world was still hot.  
But in the middle of that heat, Yuto’s discomfort report had entered the design team’s materials, and the word “return” was gradually beginning to take shape as an official evaluation axis within Cooling Commons.

## Afterword

Thank you for reading Episode 24.

In this episode, Yuto walks through the city the day after submitting his discomfort report, checking the effect of those words.  
Through the shopping street poster, the round sticker on the station stairs, and the sign at the riverbank, he goes to see both the fact that words are making someone a little more comfortable and the fact that not enough has yet returned to him.

What matters is that Yuto does not say, “The words work, so names are unnecessary,” nor does he say, “There are no names, so everything is wrong.” Instead, he continues to log the space between those two positions — the state of “not appropriation, but still lacking return.”  
That stance influences the process by which “return” is added as an evaluation axis in Cooling Commons, and will connect to future life support links and the balance between larger corporate and municipal projects.

Please continue following Yuto as he carries both the effect of words and the insufficiency of return, and as he chooses how to keep walking.
