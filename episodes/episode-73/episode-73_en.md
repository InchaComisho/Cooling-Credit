# Preface

Episode 73 overlaps two moments: the result of the priority proposal contest at Point A, the Third Worker Rest Station, and an emergency cooling request from the northern cargo-handling area.
Will Yuto's relay-rest route be recognized? And before the official wide-area mission even begins, how will he protect the port's work that simply cannot be stopped? This episode keeps the pace high by dropping the next crisis immediately after the winner is decided.

# Episode 73 — The Winner's Next Site

“Priority Proposal Contest — Ended”
“Calculating results”

In front of the Third Worker Rest Station, Yuto stood frozen, still gripping his phone.

Beside him, HEATWAVE-3 silently watched his own terminal.

The roll-call tent was already being cleared away. The relay-rest setup at the safety inspection booth was also nearing the end of its short-lived role, because the next vehicle inspection window was approaching.

Inside the Third Rest Station, workers with leg and back problems were still resting.

Cooling output: 55%.

Only a short while ago, cutting it to 40% had looked like the more rational choice.

But now, people who could move were heading to the Second Rest Station with a short rest stop in between, while those who struggled to move stayed at the Third.

Instead of cutting one group off, the operation had been divided by use.

“The result is taking a while,” Yuto said.

“Wide-area decisions always take a while,” HEATWAVE-3 replied. “They don't decide from one number.”

“You're the one saying that?”

“I judge by numbers. I never said there was only one number.”

That surprised Yuto a little.

HEATWAVE-3 had pushed for the 40% reduction. He had looked like someone who prioritized efficiency.

But he had never proposed a full shutdown. He had prepared an alternate rest site. And even now, he was still watching the workers' movement patterns.

“Yuto.”

HEATWAVE-3 looked at him.

“Your plan is troublesome.”

“Is that a compliment?”

“Half of one.”

He smiled faintly.

“You separate people. You separate spaces. You separate time. You create a place to stop once in the middle. It takes more people to make your plan work than it does to move a single piece of equipment.”

“But there were people we couldn't preserve without doing that.”

“I know.”

A short silence followed.

Then Yuto's phone vibrated.

“Priority Proposal Contest — Result Notice”

The atmosphere around them seemed to stop.

Yuto opened the result screen.

“Comparison Results”

“HEATWAVE-3 Proposal
Cooling-load reduction: High
Route simplicity: High
Reproducibility: High”

“Soma Yuto Proposal
Cooling-load reduction: Equivalent
Continued use by mobility-limited users: High
Route congestion reduction: High
Return of local reserve branch: High
On-site adaptability: High”

The screen went dark for a moment.

Then the verdict appeared.

“Winner: Soma Yuto”

“…I won?”

His voice came out smaller than expected.

“Winner granted discretionary official participation status for North Pier Seven.”

“Official participation registration complete.”

“Pre-observation reward: +0.080 CC”
“Alternative operation adoption reward: +0.250 CC”
“Priority proposal contest reward: +0.420 CC”

“Today's earnings: +0.750 CC”

The total jumped.

“Total Cooling Credit: 2.091 CC”

“Two point… zero nine one.”

Yuto stared at the screen again and again.

His total had been 1.261 CC. Now it had crossed 2.0 in one leap.

He was still outside the metropolitan rankings.

But he was moving forward.

Haru's voice came over comms.

“You won.”

“I won.”

“Then be happy about it. At least for a minute.”

Soma's voice cut in next.

“That reward is huge! How many days of our cooperative-operation pool is that?”

“Don't calculate it.”

“Of course I'm calculating it. This is a story about climbing up, isn't it?”

Nagi joined the channel.

“Congratulations. But returning L-4 instead of keeping it at Point A mattered. They even evaluated the fact that you preserved reserve capacity for Point C and the north-side passage.”

“That was because everyone gave me information.”

“That counts too,” Nagi said. “You collaborated, then put the final proposal out under your own name. That's your strength.”

Yuto straightened just a little.

He had won.

Against HEATWAVE-3, a top-ranked player.

In a thirty-minute contest over one small rest station.

Then the port-wide alarm sounded.

BWOOOOON.

“Northern Cargo-Handling Area — Emergency Cooling Request”

“Wide-Area Cooling Reserve: 6.9%”

The moment of celebration vanished instantly.

HEATWAVE-3 closed his terminal.

“Congratulations, winner.”

“Thank you.”

“But now the real thing starts.”

Yuto looked at the northern cargo-handling zone blinking red on the map.

“What happened?”

“High-temperature containers came in ahead of schedule.”

HEATWAVE-3 started running as he spoke.

“The northern handling area doesn't deal only with ordinary cargo. There are precision components vulnerable to heat, packaging materials that require temperature control, and workers who will keep moving until night.”

“Logistics and people again.”

“This time, the scale is different.”

◇

The northern cargo-handling area felt nothing like the rest stations and refuge spaces Yuto had seen so far.

Huge containers.
Forklifts.
Cargo cranes.
Massive conveyor systems.

Workers moved constantly between them.

There was little shade.

Heat reflected off metal container walls and asphalt.

It felt like there was another summer trapped inside the port.

“It's hot…” Yuto muttered.

Numbers flooded his phone.

“Northern Cargo-Handling Area — Surface temperature: 56.8°C”
“Work-zone apparent temperature: 44.1°C”
“Cooling reserve: 6.9%”
“Workers under high-temperature caution: 38”
“High-temperature containers: 12”

“Twelve?” Yuto asked.

“More than normal,” HEATWAVE-3 replied. “There was a transshipment delay on the west side of the port. The overflow got redirected north.”

One of the large containers had a red warning light on it.

“Temperature-Control Priority Cargo”

“What's inside?”

“We don't get the details. But it's cargo that can't remain exposed to high heat.”

“Then can't we just send more cooling here?”

“We can't.”

HEATWAVE-3 opened the northern piping map.

“The main cooling tower is in protection mode. Reserve is 6.9%. If we send full output here, one of A, B, C, or D will drop.”

“Even the Third Rest Station?”

“Possibly.”

Yuto thought of Point A, the place he had just fought to preserve.

Workers with bad backs and legs were resting there.

If he cut that cooling, people would struggle again.

“So what do we do?”

“Deciding that is your first job as a discretionary official participant.”

HEATWAVE-3 sent data to Yuto's terminal.

“North Pier Seven — Official Wide-Area Mission”
“Phase 1: Northern Cargo-Handling Area”

“Base reward: 5.000 CC”
“Additional completion conditions: Multiple”

“Discretionary authority: Submit and test local operating proposals”

Yuto's heart pounded.

5.000 CC.

The number that had seemed so distant.

Now he was standing at its entrance.

But another warning appeared at the same time.

“Wide-Area Mission Start Condition:
Secure at least 2.0% total cooling reserve from the existing four points.”

A.
B.
C.
D.

The very places Yuto had been trying to preserve.

To send enough cooling to the northern handling area, he now had to free up a combined 2.0% from them.

“…So in the end, we still have to cut something.”

FROST-9 arrived a little later.

“We do.”

White jacket. Silver control unit.

“But you can propose where, when, and by how much.”

“Me?”

“You earned discretionary official participation status.”

FROST-9 looked at him steadily.

“From this point on, your proposal will move part of North Pier Seven.”

Yuto looked down at the map.

Point A: Third Worker Rest Station.
Point B: Waiting-Vehicle Water Station.
Point C: Harbor Perimeter Shade-Bench Zone.
Point D: Neighborhood Emergency Refuge Space.

Simply preserving all four was no longer enough.

There were thirty-eight workers in the northern handling area and twelve high-temperature containers.

He had to create at least 2.0% of cooling reserve for them.

“What's the time limit?” Yuto asked.

FROST-9 checked her terminal.

“Twenty-five minutes.”

“Phase 1 begins in: 00:24:59”

“Twenty-five minutes…”

Then voices rose from deeper in the cargo area.

“Open the rest area again!”

“It's too hot by the containers!”

“We're short on water!”

Several locations were calling out at the same time.

Yuto's phone vibrated.

“Official Wide-Area Mission — Initial Proposal”

“Required cooling reserve: 2.0% or more”

“Proposer: Soma Yuto”

At the bottom of the screen were four output sliders.

A.
B.
C.
D.

And the Northern Cargo-Handling Area.

The 5.000 CC that could push him upward.

Its first step began with taking a little cooling away from someone else.

# Afterword

Episode 73 reveals the result of the priority proposal contest at Point A, where Yuto defeats HEATWAVE-3.

Key points this time:

- HEATWAVE-3's plan was strong because of its high load reduction and simple route design using a 40% reduction.
- Yuto kept the Third Rest Station at 55%, protected users with mobility limitations, and created a relay-rest route through the safety inspection booth for short-stay users.
- Returning the L-4 auxiliary branch from Point A and preserving reserve capacity for Point C and the north-side passage was also evaluated positively.
- Yuto earned +0.080 CC for observation completion, +0.250 CC for adoption of his alternative-operation proposal, and +0.420 CC for the priority proposal contest, bringing his total Cooling Credit to 2.091 CC.
- He obtained discretionary official participation status for North Pier Seven and formally qualified for the wide-area mission with a minimum reward of 5.000 CC.
- Immediately afterward, an emergency cooling request appeared in the northern cargo-handling area, where 12 high-temperature containers and 38 workers required protection.
- To proceed with the wide-area mission, Yuto must secure at least 2.0% total cooling reserve from existing Points A through D.

Yuto has now won his first direct contest against a top-ranked player and crossed 2.0 CC. But to move toward the truly large 5.000 CC reward, he must now gather cooling reserve from the same A-through-D locations he spent so much effort preserving.

Next episode will focus on his initial proposal: how much to reduce, from which points, and during which time windows, all within twenty-five minutes. The real question is whether Yuto can create a new distribution that cools the northern cargo-handling area without simply abandoning the places he fought to keep alive.
