# Episode 10: The Gap Between Names and Credit

[← Top / README](../../README.md)

In Episode 10, Yuto speaks with a hidden-rank player and begins thinking about how success stories treat ideas, and about the gap between names and credit.

At the same time, the feeling of wanting ideas to remain open and shareable without simply being taken for free begins to take shape in words.

---

Sunday.

Soma Yuto woke to a quieter morning than usual.

No part-time shift.

No plans.

Even so, yesterday's discomfort remained in his mind.

The cooling proposal for the suburban chain store had been adopted.

The shade at the entrance had increased, and the danger zone in the rear passage had been clearly marked.

That itself had certainly been good.

But when the store summarized it as a success case, the names placed at the front would be WindArc and the store alone.

Yuto's report would remain only as a reference source inside the system.

“…I guess that's normal in this world.”

Yuto picked up the phone beside his pillow and opened **ThermoBalance**.

“Cumulative Cooling Credit: 0.318 CC”

The number continued to rise little by little.

That itself was not bad.

But another notification still remained at the edge of his vision.

“Message from hidden-rank player  
‘Would you like to talk sometime about the relationship between success cases and report references?’”

“I'll go hear them out.”

Yuto exhaled, got ready, and went outside.

The meeting place was a small café a little distance from the station.

A glass entrance.

A modest sunshade and potted plants outside.

From a cooling perspective, it looked like a properly considered store — neither excessive nor insufficient.

When he entered, a comfortable coolness wrapped around him.

It was not overcooled.

But no heat remained either.

“This place is well designed…”

As Yuto looked around, someone at a table in the back raised a hand.

“You are Soma Yuto, correct?”

“Yes.”

The speaker was a woman with a calm presence.

She looked around thirty.

A simple shirt and trousers.

Her eyes were sharp, but not cold.

A laptop, a smartphone, and a **ThermoBalance** terminal were arranged on the table.

“Nice to meet you.  
My handle is ShiroReflect.”

“…I've seen that name near the top of the rankings.”

Yuto froze for a moment.

One of the highest-ranked individual players in the world.

A player known for cooling through urban reflective design.

“You don't need to react so dramatically.”

ShiroReflect laughed lightly.

“Today, I didn't come to talk about the whole world. I came to talk about your town and your logs.”

“That's still a pretty big conversation.”

“True.”

She gave Yuto a small nod.

“First, I saw yesterday's success-case log.”

“The store one?”

“Yes.  
WindArc's proposal — the entrance shade and the rear-passage route — was good.  
Your report was referenced too.”

“Inside the system, apparently.”

“But your name does not appear in the externally published case material.”

“That's exactly it.”

At last, it felt like they had reached the real subject.

“I don't mind my report being used as material.  
I'm actually happy about that.  
But when it goes out as a ‘success case,’ everything after that carries only the proposer's name…”

“It leaves a slight discomfort.”

“Yes.”

ShiroReflect nodded and operated the terminal.

A summary of the store's cooling success case appeared on the screen.

“Case title:  
Cooling improvements for a suburban chain-store parking lot and rear passage

Proposer:  
WindArc (store staff)

Contents:  
Expansion of the shaded zone near the entrance / establishment of a route avoiding the danger zone in the rear passage, and related measures”

Below it, in small text, were only the words: “Referenced report IDs: Multiple.”

“One of those report IDs is your ‘Small Cooling Logs from Daily Life.’”

“So in the world of numbers, the connection is properly recorded.”

“Yes.  
But when this case is shared with other stores or companies, many people will not look as far as the referenced report IDs.”

“…Right.”

Busy staff members.

People who skim a case and decide to try something similar.

They are not interested in who produced the original idea.

They look only at the result and the method.

“In that state, credit on the idea side tends to become thin.”

“Credit…”

“It can mean a name, or it can mean a share of Cooling Credit.  
Either way, return to the original idea can become too small.”

ShiroReflect continued in an even tone.

“That is where your discomfort lies.”

“I think so.”

Yuto straightened his posture slightly.

“I don't particularly want to sell my name or become famous.  
But if there is no system that properly returns something to the person who produced the idea,  
won't it eventually become nothing more than having ideas taken for free?”

“That concern is valid.”

ShiroReflect took a sip of coffee.

“The current system is quite strict about distributing credit for the actual amount of cooling.  
How much the temperature fell. How much danger was reduced.  
Those results are almost impossible to fake.”

“I think that's good.”

“But distribution of credit for the cooling idea itself is still experimental.”

“Experimental…”

“Report-ID references are one part of it.”

She displayed another item on the screen.

“Cooling Idea Trace (Beta)  
- Cooling report ID  
- Number of references  
- Cooling results derived from those references  
- Automatic return rate to the idea provider (trial)”

“Your report is active here too.”

“Automatic return…?”

“At present, there is a mechanism that sends a very small amount of Cooling Credit back to the referenced report.  
But it is still a beta function, and only limited cases qualify.”

“Limited.”

“The suburban chain-store case is one of them.”

Yuto looked at the values displayed.

“Cooling Idea Trace:  
Referenced report: Small Cooling Logs from Daily Life  
Reference count: 3  
Cumulative return: +0.006 CC (trial value)”

“…It's there.”

Small.

But the number had undeniably moved.

“That 0.006 CC is an experimental return to the idea side.”

“Experimental…”

“At present, only a very small portion of the total Cooling Credit goes to the idea side.  
Most of it goes to the implementation side — WindArc and the store.”

“That makes sense.  
They're the ones actually moving things and changing the store.”

“But the people working on-site have also begun saying that the original idea may deserve a little more return.”

“They have?”

“WindArc is one of them.”

ShiroReflect scrolled and showed him one log.

“WindArc comment log:  
‘The report based on daily movement routes helped me.  
Since my credit increased through the work I did on-site,  
I think there should be a system that automatically returns a little more to the referenced report as well.’”

“…He said that?”

Yuto laughed without meaning to.

“It isn't displayed in a way that would normally reach you directly.”

“But I'm glad.”

“I imagine so.”

ShiroReflect's expression softened slightly.

“The problem is what form the return should take.”

“What form…”

“A name alone?  
Cooling Credit?  
Evaluation points?  
Or some combination of them?”

Yuto thought for a moment.

“If it's only a name, being seen might feel good, but it doesn't change your life.”

“Correct.”

“If it's only Cooling Credit, money could move without anyone knowing where the idea came from.”

“That concern is also valid.”

“And if it's only evaluation points, maybe you just become someone vaguely considered important.”

“You observe these things well.”

ShiroReflect nodded with mild admiration.

“Players like you, who view institutions from the perspective of an ordinary resident, are valuable.”

“I'm not saying anything that important.”

“You are.”

She continued seriously.

“Designers of the cooling system are currently considering a mechanism that can combine open idea sharing with fair return of credit.”

“Open sharing and fair return…”

“Do you want to close your ideas?”

“Close them…?”

“In other words, do you want to keep them entirely to yourself?”

Yuto tilted his head slightly.

“Honestly, no.  
If it cools the town, I think anyone should be allowed to use them.”

“I thought so.”

“But I do think that if they are used, something should come back.”

“That is natural too.”

“I don't want to claim that everything belongs entirely to me.  
If someone sees one of my ideas, improves it, and uses it somewhere else, I would rather welcome that.”

After saying it, he surprised himself a little.

“Oh. So what I mean is, I want it to stay open, but I don't want everything to disappear.”

“Exactly.”

ShiroReflect smiled.

“That feeling is already beginning to be put into words in several places.”

“What do they call it?”

“Cooling Commons.”

“Cooling… Commons.”

“Cooling ideas are opened as shared resources.  
Anyone can use them.  
Anyone can improve them.  
But when they are used, credit and a certain return to the original designer are generated automatically.”

It was almost exactly the organized form of what he had just said.

“Open, but protected.”

“Yes.”

“And credit also means leaving the name attached.”

“We are considering a structure that combines both meanings.”

ShiroReflect glanced outside the café window, then looked back at Yuto.

“For example, suppose you post a cooling idea on social media or a dedicated platform under a Cooling Commons license.”

“A license…”

“Anyone can view it.  
Copy it.  
Improve it.  
But if the implementation produces cooling results above a certain level, credit automatically flows to the source-idea ID.”

“So that's Cooling Commons…”

“Still only an experimental framework.”

She laughed.

“Your discomfort is one case demonstrating why such a framework is necessary.”

“Necessary…”

“Yes.”

Yuto drew a small breath.

“I thought I was only cooling things inside my living zone.”

“That is precisely why it matters.”

ShiroReflect became serious.

“Institutional discussions easily become theories detached from reality when they lack the viewpoint of ordinary residents.  
When a player like you properly puts discomfort into words, it can become the trigger that closes a hole in the system.”

“…I don't feel like I'm doing anything that important.”

“You are doing something important inside daily life.”

The words carried some weight.

But it was not an unpleasant weight.

“A formal version of Cooling Commons will not appear immediately.  
But from Part 2 onward — somewhat further ahead — this framework may begin operating in earnest.”

“Part 2…?”

“Of your story.”

ShiroReflect said it in a slightly joking tone.

“Right now, it is still the final part of Part 1.  
Cooling actions within the living zone. The accumulation of small proposals.  
The birth of discomfort.  
This is the phase where you need to experience those things properly.”

“Experience them…”

“Yes.  
If the story jumped to Cooling Commons before you knew this discomfort, it would feel like a system imposed from above.  
For you to feel it as something created because it was needed, this stage is necessary.”

The explanation made strange sense to him.

“Then what I can do now is…”

“Continue cooling within your living zone.”

She answered simply.

“Write logs.  
Record your discomfort.  
When it feels necessary, publish something small.  
That is enough.”

“Enough…”

“That becomes the foundation of Cooling Commons.”

Yuto looked into his coffee cup.

Daily life.

Cooling.

Logs.

Discomfort.

All of them were connected to institutional design.

“Then for now, my role is to properly remember the discomfort.”

“That is one way to describe it.”

ShiroReflect smiled.

“And one more thing.”

“One more?”

“Value your cooperation with WindArc.”

“Of course.”

“He is both a resident and an on-site player.  
Like you, he carries not only a top-down institutional perspective, but also the lived sense that comes from below.  
When Cooling Commons begins moving, it will help to have players like that near the center of the system.”

“The center…”

Yuto narrowed his eyes slightly.

“I don't know whether either of us can get that far.”

“You don't need to know.”

ShiroReflect shrugged.

“For now, it is enough to remember the discomfort and keep cooling your living zone.”

That ended the conversation.

When he left the café, the air outside felt slightly heavy.

The temperature was high.

But inside him remained a different kind of heat from usual.

“Names and credit…  
Openness and return…”

Yuto murmured as he walked.

“I need to make sure I don't forget this discomfort.”

After returning to his apartment, he opened a new page in his notebook.

“Today's observations:  
- In externally published success cases, the name attached to the original idea easily disappears  
- Inside the system, the source is recorded, and there is a trial function that returns part of the Cooling Credit  
- The feeling of wanting ideas to remain openly shareable without being taken for free can become the seed of Cooling Commons”

After writing, Yuto put down his pen.

“Today's role:  
- Properly remember the discomfort  
- Continue cooling inside the living zone”

“With this, most of the foundation for Part 1 is in place…”

The story had not entered Part 2 yet.

Cooling Commons had not formally begun either.

But its existence had started to appear in the distance.

Keeping that distance intact, Yuto quietly closed his notebook.

Thank you for reading Episode 10.

Through Yuto's conversation with the hidden-rank, institution-oriented player ShiroReflect, this episode began putting into words the treatment of success cases and ideas, the gap between names and credit, and the feeling of wanting to share openly without simply having ideas taken for free.

This discomfort and clarification create a natural foundation for connecting the end of Part 1 to Cooling Commons, an open-license-like framework that will become important in Part 2.

The next episode returns to cooling within the living zone while occasionally sensing the presence of institutions, creating a slightly unusual balance between daily life and the larger system.
