# Episode 70 — Beneath the Shaded Bench

## Preface

Episode 70 investigates Point C, the last unobserved site at North Pier Seven—the shaded bench zone along the port perimeter.
An unregistered small-scale cooling branch exists here, absent from the official blueprints. If Yuto can identify what it really is, it may become a way to fill small “cooling gaps” like the north passage. At the same time, the presence of a competing player interested in the equipment draws closer.

---

## Main Story

“Point C — Port Perimeter Shaded Bench Zone”
“Unregistered equipment detected”

“Small-scale cooling branch: response detected”

Yuto ran along the perimeter road of North Pier Seven, heading toward the seaside walkway.

The noise of the container yard faded slightly behind him, replaced by the stronger smell of salt in the air.

But it was still hot.

There was a sea breeze.
Yet even the wind itself felt warm.

The shaded bench zone along the road sat in the shadow of a long warehouse wall.

Six benches.
An old water-supply sign.
A small roadside tree.
And piping rising from the ground beside the wall.

“So this is it…”

Until moments ago, Point C had been running at 18% output.

To redirect cooling toward Point D, they had reduced it to 10%.

There had been only two users.

One had moved toward the north passage, where Haru and the others had responded.
The other had discovered an old cooling box beneath one of the benches.

When Yuto arrived, a Point C guide staff member and an elderly man wearing a cap were waiting.

“Are you Soma?” the staff member asked.

“Yes. I heard you found a cooling box.”

“It’s here.”

The staff member pointed beneath the bench.

An old metal box was embedded there, almost hidden by the shadow of the seat.

Its paint had peeled away, and nearly all of its lettering had faded.

Only one label was still barely readable.

“Port Perimeter
Auxiliary Cooling Terminal”

“Auxiliary cooling terminal…”

Yuto crouched down.

A small vent sat on the side of the box.

A faint stream of cold air leaked from it.

“It’s still alive,” Yuto said.

“Probably,” the elderly man replied.

“I was sitting here, and only my feet felt a little cool. I wondered why, so I looked underneath.”

“Thank you for telling us.”

“No need. I used to work around here years ago.”

The man looked down the row of benches.

“This place used to be busier. Cargo workers would stop here before heading home. Drivers waiting for their turn would sit here. People heading back toward the residential district used it too.”

“Was the auxiliary cooling terminal installed for that?”

“Who knows. But there are midsummer days when the shadow of this wall isn’t enough. I feel like air used to come up from around your feet.”

Yuto held up his smartphone.

“Point C — Unregistered Equipment”
“Terminal response: low-output standby”

“Air from beneath the feet…”

He connected to Nagi.

“Nagi, I’m at Point C. The auxiliary cooling terminal is in low-output standby.”

“Send me video.”

Yuto aimed the camera.

The metal box.
Thin piping.
The vent.
A small panel beneath the bench.

“…I’ve never seen this format before. It may be old port-specific equipment.”

“Can we use it?”

“Don’t touch it yet. If the piping is connected to the main cooling tower, it could be dangerous right now. But if it’s an independent auxiliary route, we may be able to use it for the north passage.”

“To fill the cooling gap up north?”

“Possibly. But it looks like activation requires authentication.”

On the front of the box was a small panel.

“Auxiliary Terminal L-4”
“Normal output: stopped”
“Emergency output: awaiting authentication”

“L-4…”

Yuto wrote down the code.

Then footsteps approached.

“So it was here after all.”

A voice he did not recognize.

He turned and saw a woman in a thin black coat.

Sunglasses covered most of her eyes.
In one hand she held a slim silver terminal.

Its display showed a purple player ID.

“MIST CROW”

“…MIST CROW?” Yuto said.

“That’s right.”

MIST CROW looked at the box beneath the bench.

“I picked up a response from unregistered equipment. This explains why Point C’s local temperature stayed strangely stable even after output was reduced.”

“You knew about this place?”

“No.”

“Then why did you come?”

“To inspect it. And, if necessary, seal it off.”

Yuto’s expression changed.

“Seal it off?”

“Equipment that isn’t on the blueprints is a risk in a wide-area operation. We don’t know its maximum output, connection destinations, or maintenance history.”

“But if it can be used—”

“That ‘if’ is exactly what makes it dangerous.”

MIST CROW showed him the readings on her terminal.

“Auxiliary Terminal L-4
Estimated consumption: 0.6% of wide-area cooling reserve
Connection: unknown
Maintenance record: none”

“0.6% looks small. But North Pier Seven only has 7.6% reserve right now. There is no reason to activate an unverified 0.6% and risk destabilizing the main cooling tower again.”

Yuto could not argue with that.

He had already seen how frightening wide-area adjustment could be.

To help one person, they had redirected water from Point B.
To make Point D more bearable, they had reduced output at Point C.
A small change in one place could affect someone somewhere else.

Unregistered equipment was even more dangerous.

“Let me inspect it before you seal it,” Yuto said.

“Only the connections and output. I’m not saying we’ll use it.”

“That still takes time.”

“The observation pass has a deadline.”

Yuto checked his screen.

“Point C observation deadline: 00:11:23”

“Eleven minutes. If we inspect this, it might become a small, permanent support option for the north passage.”

“Might.”

“But there’s a chance we can preserve it in a smaller form instead of shutting it down completely or moving a major line.”

MIST CROW looked at Yuto through her sunglasses.

“Do you intend to preserve everything?”

“No.”

Yuto shook his head.

“If it’s dangerous, I’ll stop it. But I don’t want to stop something just because we don’t understand it. I want to verify it first.”

MIST CROW went silent for a moment.

Then she operated her terminal.

“Point C — Auxiliary Terminal L-4
Limited diagnostic access granted”

“Ten minutes,” she said.

Yuto’s phone chimed.

“Diagnostic access: started”
“Operation prohibited. Read-only access permitted.”

“Thank you.”

“Don’t thank me. If the diagnostics show danger, agree to an immediate seal.”

“Understood.”

◇

Nagi began reading the terminal remotely.

“L-4’s internal temperature is low. But it isn’t being supplied directly from the main cooling tower.”

“Then from where?”

“…Wait. The piping flow goes in two directions.”

Yuto looked at the box under the bench.

“Two directions?”

“One route serves Point C beneath the benches. The other goes to the old water post in the north passage… K-17.”

“K-17?”

The water post where Haru and Soma had delivered the release key in the previous incident.

The place where cold water had helped a man on the verge of heat stress.

“L-4 and K-17 are connected?”

“More precisely, they share an old auxiliary cooling-and-water line. If L-4 runs at low output, it may be able to lower the water temperature on the K-17 side.”

“So we could send colder water to the north passage.”

Yuto nodded.

“Even without local cooling there, cold water could provide emergency support.”

“That alone has value,” MIST CROW said.

“But L-4 has another unknown connection.”

Nagi went quiet for a moment.

“Found it. L-4 isn’t connected only to the perimeter benches and the water post.”

“What else?”

“The residential side. A thin auxiliary pipe extends to the temporary refuge space at Point D.”

“All the way to Point D?”

Yuto opened the map.

Point C.
The north passage.
Point D.

A small line linked all three points, separate from the large main cooling tower network.

“A small-scale cooling branch…” Yuto murmured.

“Maybe Point C can support both the north passage and Point D.”

MIST CROW checked her terminal.

“But if all three points run simultaneously, it won’t stay at 0.6%.”

“Estimated maximum load: 1.8%”

The figure appeared.

1.8% out of a wide-area reserve of 7.6%.

That was large.

“We can’t use it now,” Yuto said.

“It’s too dangerous while the main cooling tower is unstable.”

MIST CROW looked slightly surprised.

“So you agree to seal it?”

“I don’t agree to continuous operation,” Yuto replied.

“But I don’t want it sealed either. I want to keep it in standby and use low output for only one of the three points when necessary. For example, if someone gets stranded in the north passage again. Or if Point D suddenly gets an influx of people.”

“Each time, you spend 0.6%.”

“In exchange, we may avoid having to move the entire wide-area line.”

MIST CROW thought for a moment.

“A reserve branch for local support.”

“Yes.”

“Who decides when it activates?”

Yuto did not answer immediately.

That question had come up before, during the night of the shared cooling line.

Who decides priorities?

Does one person hold everything?
Or do multiple people watch together?

“I won’t decide alone,” Yuto said.

“The Point C staff, the north-passage lead, the Point D management side—and Nagi, who can read the equipment. At minimum, two on-site parties should confirm before activation.”

“Collaborative operation again.”

“That’s how I work.”

Yuto’s phone vibrated.

“Point C — Unregistered Equipment Diagnostics”

“Safety: provisionally acceptable”
“Wide-area impact: restriction required”
“Local support value: high”

“Submit alternative operating plan?”

“I will.”

Yuto opened the proposal screen.

“Proposal: Do not keep L-4 in continuous operation.
Preserve it as a low-output reserve branch for local emergency support.
Activation conditions:
• Heat-stress warning in the north passage
• Sudden user increase at Point D
• Emergency-use demand at Point C
When one of these occurs, and only after dual on-site confirmation plus equipment-log confirmation,
allocate a maximum of 0.6% to only one of the three points.”

He sent it.

“Observation deadline: 00:02:11”

MIST CROW read Yuto’s proposal.

“Not bad.”

Yuto looked up.

“Accepted?”

“The evaluation bureau still has to rule on it.”

“Of course.”

“But…”

MIST CROW lowered her sunglasses slightly.

Her eyes looked less cold than quiet.

“This is not ‘reduction in order to preserve.’ It is unused capacity held in reserve for the next crisis.”

“Unused capacity.”

“Top players see reserve as a number. You see reserve as something that can still be used when someone gets into trouble.”

At that moment, Yuto’s phone chimed.

“Pre-observation pass: Point C observation complete”

“Observation completion reward: +0.080 CC”

“Alternative operating plan: submitted to evaluation bureau”

“Formal participation eligibility: under review”

Yuto let out a small breath.

At least the observation was complete.

But he still had not earned entry into the 5.000 CC wide-area assignment.

Then FROST-9 called by voice.

“Yuto, can you hear me?”

“Yes.”

“I saw the discovery log for L-4. I also read your alternative plan.”

“What do you think?”

A brief pause.

“North Pier Seven’s reserve has recovered from 7.6% to 8.4%. The main cooling tower’s protective operation has stabilized.”

“Then can we test L-4?”

“Still too soon. But a test operation may be possible.”

Yuto looked down at L-4 beneath the bench.

“However, there’s a problem before that,” FROST-9 continued.

“A problem?”

“HEATWAVE-3 has announced that Point A’s Third Worker Rest Building will be reduced to 40% earlier than planned.”

“But the Second Rest Building had just gone down.”

“It’s back online. His position is that since the alternate destination has recovered, the original plan should proceed.”

“But there are people who can’t easily move there.”

“Exactly. So your formal participation review will be decided by the next case.”

A new display appeared on Yuto’s phone.

“North Pier Seven — Priority Proposal Rights Contest”

“Target: Point A — Third Worker Rest Building”
“Begins in: 00:29:59”

“Contestants:
Soma Yuto
HEATWAVE-3”

“Victory condition:
Within 30 minutes, demonstrate an alternative operation that outperforms the 40% reduction plan.”

Yuto stared at the screen.

The small cooling branch at Point C.

The water in the north passage.

The refuge space at Point D.

Immediately after proposing ways to preserve those functions, he was now being placed directly against HEATWAVE-3.

And his opponent was ranked third in the metropolitan area.

Red text appeared on Yuto’s screen.

“Winner receives North Pier Seven formal participation status with local discretion.”

In the heat of the port,
Yuto finally stepped into a true head-to-head battle with a top-ranked player.

## Afterword

Episode 70 investigates the unregistered “Auxiliary Cooling Terminal L-4” discovered beneath the shaded benches at Point C, revealing the existence of a small auxiliary line hidden within North Pier Seven.

Key points in this episode:

- L-4 was an old auxiliary cooling terminal beneath the Point C benches, still alive in low-output standby.
- It shared a small cooling-and-water line with the K-17 water post in the north passage and the temporary refuge area at Point D.
- Running all three points simultaneously could consume up to 1.8% of the wide-area reserve, making continuous operation dangerous while the main cooling tower remained unstable.
- Rather than sealing L-4, Yuto proposed preserving it as a low-output emergency reserve branch that can allocate up to 0.6% to only one of the three points at a time.
- Activation would require confirmation from two on-site parties plus equipment-log confirmation, avoiding dependence on Yuto alone.
- Completing Point C observation earned Yuto +0.080 CC.
- Meanwhile, HEATWAVE-3 moved to accelerate the planned 40% reduction at Point A’s Third Worker Rest Building, triggering a direct contest for priority proposal rights against Yuto.

In Episode 70, Yuto treats the unregistered equipment not as “a tool to activate immediately for profit,” but as reserve capacity that might quietly support people during the next emergency.
That judgment differs from the efficiency-first instincts of many top-ranked players. FROST-9 nevertheless recognizes the value in his approach and gives him the chance to face HEATWAVE-3 directly.

Next episode moves to a 30-minute demonstration at Point A’s Third Worker Rest Building.
HEATWAVE-3’s 40% reduction looks rational now that the Second Rest Building has recovered. Yuto must prove, within 30 minutes, an alternative that preserves access for workers who have difficulty moving while still protecting the wide-area reserve. For the first time, only one winner will receive formal participation status with local discretion.
