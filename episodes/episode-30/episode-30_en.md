# Episode 30: How Support Records Are Used

[← Top / README](../../README.md)

Episode 30 is the point where the story of support has gone through one full cycle, and the question of how companies see that support begins to show itself.  
After seeing both the summer that received support and the summer that did not, Yuto begins to notice how the record of support is about to be used.

---

## Main Text

Saturday.  
Soma Yuto finished his convenience store shift in the morning and wandered through the shopping street.

The sky was clear.  
The heat was merciless, but the memories of the afternoon with support and the summer without support were still fresh in Yuto's mind.

He stopped when he noticed a new poster on the bulletin board in front of the station.

"Cooling Commons Support Record Area  
In this shopping street, the trial operation of the daily-life support link has confirmed:  
- improved cooling use during heatwave peak hours  
- longer bench stay times  
- expanded recognition of the phrase 'Take a breath here'"

"...Support Record Area, huh."

The wording was clean.  
But when Yuto saw the small line below it, "Cooperation: Regional Business Liaison Council," his eyes narrowed slightly.

"Companies are starting to use it."

The support had improved cooling in the town.  
That result was certainly real.  
But that fact was being turned into advertising material: this town is strong on cooling, this area is easy to live in.

As he looked up at the poster, a voice came from behind him.

"You saw that?"

He turned around and found WindArc standing there.

"I saw it.  
...It already feels like they're selling the support record."

"Yes.  
Very quickly, too."

WindArc gave a wry smile.

"Apparently the Regional Business Liaison Council has started using it in business proposal materials, saying,  
'This area has produced cooling results through the introduction of the daily-life support link.'"

"Business proposal materials..."

Yuto frowned slightly.

"Support becomes advertising?"

"It does.  
And cooling results look good.  
They make it easy to present companies as working on heat countermeasures or contributing to the community."

Yuto fell silent for a while.

Support had made someone's life a little easier.  
That much was fine.  
But when companies absorbed it as regional value or brand power, the story began to shift little by little.

"It feels pretty uncomfortable when companies look at support and start talking like it's their achievement."

"Exactly.  
And when they present the effect of support as an improvement for the whole area,  
it becomes harder to see who was left behind."

Yuto nodded deeply at that.

"The people who received support had their lives made a little easier.  
But the people who didn't receive it are still there.  
Companies are probably looking only at the former."

"Yes.  
And once a place has a support record, companies will find it easier to make the next proposal."

"What kind of proposal?"

"For example:  
- store renovations that follow this shopping street's cooling routes  
- attracting customers with 'Support Record Area' signs  
- events linked to Cooling Commons  
Things like that."

"...None of those are bad, exactly.  
But it feels like something is missing."

"Yes.  
There is something missing."

WindArc sat down on a bench and showed Yuto his phone.

"Regional Business Liaison Council memo, excerpt:  
- raise recognition as a support record area  
- form an image as a region enthusiastic about heat countermeasures  
- use the Cooling Commons case as an explanation for introduction in other regions  
- select resident player voices that are as favorable as possible"

"...Select."

"Yes.  
That is the part that worries me most."

"Only favorable ones?"

"Yes.  
Discomfort from people who did not receive support,  
or the heat felt by people support did not reach,  
will probably be treated as unsuitable for business proposals."

Yuto looked at the shadow of the bench and thought.

"Showing the support results is fine.  
But selling only the convenient parts  
is just another kind of cutting out, isn't it?"

"Exactly."

After a short pause, WindArc continued.

"That is why discomfort reports are necessary.  
They are needed to record that the support record is real, but also what is becoming invisible behind it."

"Should I write another one?"

"Yes.  
Especially these two points:  
- support is turning into corporate brand material  
- the voices of people who did not receive support are being selected out  
Both should be formally recorded."

Yuto nodded, gazing slightly into the distance.

"Then I'll write it."

That evening, Yuto sat on a bench in the shopping street and began writing in his notebook.

"Discomfort Report, Draft  
Title: Support records beginning to be used in corporate advertising

Content:  
- It is true that the trial introduction of the daily-life support link improved the shopping street's cooling environment and bench stay times.  
  In that sense, the existence of support records can be evaluated positively.

- On the other hand, I feel discomfort that the Regional Business Liaison Council has begun using these records in business materials and advertisements as proof of a town with cooling results or an easy-to-live-in area.

- Support did not only make the lives of those who received it a little easier.  
  At the same time, it produced the heat of those who did not receive it, and the feeling of being placed outside the system.  
  In advertisements and materials, those latter emotions tend to be selected out and become invisible.

- I do not completely reject converting support records into corporate value.  
  However, when doing so, I feel it is necessary to preserve both  
  'who was helped, and by how much'  
  and  
  'who was left behind, and by how much.'"

As he wrote, the rough feeling inside Yuto gradually took shape.

"I understand that support gets used as a record.  
But if the summer that did not receive support disappears from it, that's pretty bad."

Before sending it, he added one final line.

"- Support records should be treated not as convenient advertising material, but as records that show differences in daily life."

After he sent it, no reply came immediately.

At night, only a short message arrived from WindArc.

"WindArc:  
That sentence is really important.  
I'll pass it to the design team too."

On his way back to his apartment, Yuto looked up at the station poster once more.

"Support Record Area."

The phrase was certainly positive.  
But behind it, there was still the shadow of people who had not been included in the record.

Back in his room, he opened his notebook.

"Today's Events  
- A 'Support Record Area' poster was put up in the shopping street  
  → Confirmed that the Regional Business Liaison Council has begun using support records for business proposals and attracting customers  
- WindArc shared that companies tend to select only favorable voices  
- Drafted a discomfort report  
  → discomfort about support records becoming advertising  
  → concern that the emotions of people who did not receive support will become invisible  
  → proposal that support records should remain records that show differences in daily life"

"Today's Realization  
- Support is a relief for those who receive it, while also easily becoming brand material for companies  
- At that moment, what companies want to see is the improvement result, not the rough feelings that remain  
- Discomfort reports are necessary to record those remaining rough feelings"

Finally, one line.

"Today's Line  
- Before support records are sold as clean achievements, they should record who was left in the heat as well."

Cooling Credit had increased slightly again today.

"Earned today: 0.015 CC  
Total: 0.663 CC"

"It feels like companies are moving faster than the numbers."

The world was still hot.  
And support had certainly changed the town a little, but how companies would cut out that change had now clearly become the next problem.

## Afterword

Thank you for reading Episode 30.

This time, I depicted how the records of the daily-life support link begin to be used by the Regional Business Liaison Council in advertising and business proposal materials.  
What matters here is that support does not end as simply something good. For companies, it can quickly be converted into brand material and an appeal of regional value.

Through Yuto's conversation with WindArc, the problems of selecting only favorable voices and erasing the summer that did not receive support from advertising were put into words as a discomfort report.  
This episode introduces the next stage of Part 3's theme of support and inequality: the more correctly support appears, the more things can become invisible.

In the next episode, as companies try to use these support records even more actively, the story will show from what position Yuto responds, and how he tries to protect records that reveal differences in daily life.
