# Episode 12: A Milestone and the Next Step

[← Top / README](../../README.md)

In Episode 12, Yuto looks back on cooling within his living zone, his companions, and the presence of systems, then gives himself a personal milestone.

At the same time, a small next step toward Cooling Commons and a larger stage begins to appear.

---

Tuesday.

As usual, Soma Yuto opened **ThermoBalance**.

“Cumulative Cooling Credit: 0.352 CC”

“…0.5 is still far away, but now that I've passed 0.3, maybe this is a good time to mark one milestone.”

The convenience-store back room.

The bus stop in front of the station.

The street trees in the shopping street.

The riverside entrance.

The suburban chain store.

The space in front of outdoor units in a residential area.

The nursery entrance.

Over the past few weeks, he had seen quite a number of heat distortions inside his living zone.

“At first, the world was just hot.”

The news saying it was within the normal range.

Thirty-eight-degree asphalt.

Cooling Credit, where temperature became currency.

Now, all of it looked like something to be solved.

Yuto flipped through the notebook on his desk.

On the first page, the ways to cool things were listed like a child's diary.

“Sprinkle water  
Make things white  
Increase shade  
Let wind through  
Release heat  
Guide people to cooler places”

“I came this far from these scribbles…”

The bench shade.

The bus-stop waiting line.

The convenience-store back room.

The bakery mist.

The riverside entrance tree shade.

The suburban chain store parking lot.

The stickers in front of the nursery.

All of them extended from that first note.

“Then today will be a day to walk and look back.”

Yuto put his phone in his pocket and went outside.

First, he headed to the street tree in the shopping street.

At its root were his own paper saying, “It is a little cooler here,” and someone else's paper saying, “+ If the bench is shifted slightly, it becomes even cooler.”

And now, the bench itself had been moved closer to the shaded side.

Before noon.

A student sat there looking at a phone, and an elderly person sat with shopping bags at their feet.

Yuto watched from a short distance away.

“When I first put paper here, I thought maybe no one would see it.”

Now, it was being used normally.

His phone vibrated.

“Street-tree shade usage rate: Stable  
Cooperative action log: Continuing”

“Continuing…”

Even when he did nothing, this place continued to cool itself.

That made him strangely happy.

Next, he went to the riverside entrance.

In the tree shade of the plaza, his paper, WindArc's paper, and someone else's paper overlapped.

“This is tree shade  
Sitting zone  
On hot days, please rest on the shaded side without pushing yourself”

Today too, someone returning from a run was sitting in the shade.

Someone walking a dog was also standing on the shaded side.

His phone vibrated.

“Riverside entrance shade usage rate: High  
Time spent in dangerous environment: Low”

“This isn't just my place anymore.”

When he first scanned the shade, it had been a realization made by him alone.

Now, logs and papers from multiple players overlapped there.

Cooperation.

Layering.

Chains.

“This is probably what Part 1 has achieved.”

Yuto did not go down the riverside stairs. He turned around there.

Next, he headed to the suburban chain store.

The family restaurant entrance.

The shadows of the banner flags.

The danger zone in the rear passage.

At the entrance, someone was waiting on the shaded side.

In the rear passage, staff members were walking while avoiding the front of the outdoor units.

WindArc's proposal.

His own report.

The store management screen.

“From here on, this is already the territory of the site and the company.”

Yuto looked over the family restaurant parking lot from a little distance.

There would probably be fewer chances for him to directly intervene.

Instead, WindArc, the store staff, and system-oriented players would move.

“It's not my town, but it's a place slightly connected to my town.”

Saying that, he checked his phone.

“Suburban chain store cooling results log:  
Proposal contribution: WindArc 70% / Yuto 30% (provisional)”

“This ratio will probably change someday too.”

Cooling Commons.

Open licensing.

Return of value to ideas.

If those formally began operating, the rules for contribution ratios might also change.

“For now, being the 30% resident side is enough for me.”

Yuto left the parking lot behind.

Finally, he stopped by the nursery in the residential area.

Small animal stickers were attached near the entrance.

They quietly indicated, “Please wait here,” on the shaded side under the roof.

It was just after noon.

Not peak drop-off or pick-up time, but several parents and children passed near the entrance.

A parent saw the sticker and said to a child, “Shall we wait here?”

The child stood there.

Their feet were in the shade.

His phone vibrated.

“Nursery entrance shaded stay position: Stable  
Heat-stress index (estimated): Slight decrease”

“This is properly cooling too.”

It did not dramatically lower the temperature of the entire city.

But for each person's life, the burden decreased a little.

“I don't think my town has to include saving the whole world.”

Yuto sat on a park bench a short distance from the nursery.

Bench shade.

A fountain.

A sandbox.

Trees.

This too was one of the places he had touched a little near the beginning.

“Cooling the whole world is the work of projects and systems.  
Cooling inside daily life is the work of myself and someone nearby.”

Both kinds of work existed.

His own role was the latter.

“Then the end of Part 1 should be about recognizing my own range of responsibility.”

Yuto opened his notebook and slowly moved his pen.

“Part 1 summary (for myself):  
- Cooling Credit is a currency in a world where value is created by actual cooling results  
- Inside the living zone, there are many seeds and distortions of cooling  
- Convenience store, station front, shopping street, riverside, suburban store, residential area, nursery  
  → It is okay to see all of them as my town  
- Through cooperation with other players (WindArc, hidden-rank player, ShiroReflect),  
  I gained a sense that individual cooling can connect slightly to systems  
- I felt the gap between names and credit, and the discomfort between openness and return of value  
  → I understood the need for Cooling Commons through lived feeling”

“…When I summarize it like this, it really feels like one season.”

Twelve episodes.

His living zone and the basics of cooling.

Discomfort and beginnings.

“The question is how far I step in during Part 2.”

ShiroReflect's words came back to him.

“Right now, it is still the final part of Part 1.  
Cooling actions within the living zone, accumulated small proposals, and the birth of discomfort.  
This is the phase where you need to properly experience them.”

“I experienced them, didn't I?”

Yuto looked up at the sky over the residential area.

A hazy white sun.

Heat.

Shade.

Wind.

That scenery had not changed yet.

The temperature would not drop immediately.

“Even so, I can say that small parts of my town have certainly changed.”

It was not an achievement grand enough to save the world.

But for his own life and the lives of people nearby, it was enough.

In the evening, when he returned to his apartment, a new notification appeared in **ThermoBalance**.

“Season 1 (Living Zone Cooling Arc) experience has reached a certain value.  
New mission category unlocked:  
System and Project Cooperation”

“…It came.”

Living Zone Cooling Arc.

Season 1.

A new mission category.

“So this is where Part 2 begins.”

Yuto looked at the screen and smiled slightly.

Formal proposals to companies.

City projects.

Concrete design of Cooling Commons.

Those were still ahead.

But he had certainly accumulated enough experience to stand at the entrance.

“Then the end of Part 1 will be about properly acknowledging the self that made it this far.”

On the final line of the notebook, he wrote:

“Final line of Part 1:  
- I cooled my town a little.  
  That is enough reason to move on.”

He put down the pen and closed the notebook.

His cumulative Cooling Credit remained quietly displayed.

“Cumulative Cooling Credit: 0.365 CC”

“Now, on to Part 2.”

He murmured to no one and placed the phone face down.

The world was still hot.

But he was no longer just a resident who complained about the heat.

From here on, the stage will expand to companies, municipalities, and open-license-like frameworks, while the core of the story will be how Yuto keeps his own perspective.
