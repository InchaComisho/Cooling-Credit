# Episode 19: If I Were to Give 0.5 a Name

[← Top / README](../../README.md)

Episode 19 is the day Yuto finally reaches 0.5 CC, and the moment begins to carry meaning beyond being a simple number.

At the same time, a quiet question begins to emerge: how should he use or define that 0.5 from here on?

---

Tuesday.

Soma Yuto woke a little more slowly than usual.

The air conditioner was running in a gentle fan mode.

The air in the room felt just slightly lighter than the day before.

After staring at the ceiling, he reached for the phone beside his pillow.

He opened **ThermoBalance**.

“Cumulative Cooling Credit: 0.497 CC”

“…I'll definitely pass it today.”

His convenience-store shift was in the afternoon.

The morning was free.

Now that he had organized the cooling of his own room, he had enough room to move a little on the outside again.

“But today doesn't need to be a day where I go out and earn aggressively.”

Reaching 0.5 itself was already almost certain.

What concerned Yuto more was how he would meet that moment.

He ate some bread, drank barley tea, got ready, and went outside.

The air was still hot.

But simply knowing that he had a few more places to escape the heat than before made him feel different.

He passed through the shopping street.

The shade of the street trees.

The COOL STREET posters.

The small plates on the benches.

All of them were already becoming familiar scenery.

On the way toward the riverside, his phone vibrated briefly.

“Daily cooling log update recommendation:  
Would you like to record one small cooling action within your living zone today?  
Because you are close to reaching 0.5 CC, this is recommended as a milestone point for organizing your logs.”

“…So it even gives milestone prompts.”

It was not being treated as a mere number.

It was as though the system were saying that organizing his logs here would make the next stage easier.

“Then what should today's one small action be?”

After thinking for a moment, Yuto headed toward the station instead of the riverside.

The bus stop in front of the station.

The shade beneath the roof.

The waiting line.

It was the place where he had once tested a cool waiting line using a banner and standing-position markers.

Before noon, several students and older adults were waiting there.

The line stretched along the shaded side, while almost no one stood in direct sunlight.

“This has already become a place that doesn't need me to interfere anymore.”

And that was fine.

The fact that more places could keep working without him was an achievement he could be proud of.

It would be faster to look for a place that still needed his eyes and hands.

When he shifted his gaze, he noticed a small staircase at the exit on the opposite side of the station.

A short staircase.

No roof.

The kind of steps that absorbed heat across their entire surface by midday.

At the top, a man in a suit stopped briefly to retie his shoelace.

Sweat on his face.

Heat across his back.

“…Could this be made even slightly easier?”

Yuto scanned the area around the staircase.

“Stair surface temperature: 3.9°C above surroundings  
Stay duration: Short, but used by a consistent number of people each day  
Cooling potential: Low to medium”

“The potential is low, but it's still a place someone always uses.”

No one stayed there for long.

But a steady number of people passed through every day.

They tied shoelaces.

Adjusted their bags.

“Even rewriting it in people's minds as a place where it's okay to stop for a moment might make a difference.”

Yuto moved to the small landing at the top of the stairs, where a narrow patch of shade fell.

Standing there, he wrote a short note.

“Opposite-side station stair landing  
- Short stays / little shade / hot steps  
→ Mark a standing position on the shaded side to strengthen the feeling that it is okay to stop here and retie something.”

“The action itself is simple.”

Before his shift, he bought some tape and round stickers like the ones used for store displays.

He placed a small round sticker on the shaded side of the landing.

On it, he wrote in small letters: “Take a breath here.”

It was not a municipal project.

It was not a corporate project.

It was entirely an unofficial personal mark.

In the early afternoon, before his shift, he actually tried it.

A small circle on the shaded side of the stair landing.

He had no idea how many people would change where they stood because of it.

But when he stood there himself, it was undeniably easier.

His phone vibrated.

“Daily-life cooling log: Opposite-side station stair landing  
Stay comfort: Very slight improvement  
Unofficial sign reading ‘Take a breath here’ detected.  
+0.023 CC”

“…It reached it.”

The number crossed the narrow line.

“Cumulative Cooling Credit: 0.521 CC”

“So I passed 0.5.”

At that moment, a slow realization spread through his chest.

There was no dramatic effect.

Only the number 0.521 on the screen.

Yet that number recolored the past several weeks slightly.

The convenience-store back room.

The station benches and bus stop.

The shopping street and riverside.

The suburban chain store and shopping center.

The nursery school.

His own room.

All of it was inside that 0.521.

“If I were to give 0.5 a name…”

Yuto thought in the shade of the stair landing.

“Maybe it would be one full circuit of my living zone.”

Not a great victory.

But one full circuit of his town.

The amount represented cooling along daily routes, writing logs, speaking with companions, sensing the presence of institutions and companies, and arranging his own room.

That was what 0.5 contained.

After finishing his convenience-store shift and returning to his apartment, **ThermoBalance** quietly displayed something new.

“Cumulative Cooling Credit has exceeded 0.5.

Living-Zone Cooling Experience: Level 2  
New recommended categories:  
- Review level of participation in Cooling Commons  
- Reset sharing range of living-zone logs  
- Consider how to engage with medium-scale projects”

“…A review.”

It did not suddenly unlock a dramatic set of new functions.

Instead, it quietly suggested that he review his existing logs and his way of participating.

Cooling Commons.

Daily-life cooling logs.

Comments for corporate projects.

Municipal projects.

Apparently he could reset how much of each would be shared and what would remain only with him.

“So 0.5 is the line where the system starts saying, ‘From here on, choose a little for yourself.’”

Yuto opened his notebook.

“Personal memo upon reaching 0.5 CC

- This number represents one full circuit of my living zone.  
- Convenience store / station / shopping street / riverside / suburban store / shopping center / nursery school / my own room.  
  → All of them are inside the 0.5.

- From here on, cooling enters a phase where I choose for myself  
  how much to share with others and how much to place within institutional or corporate systems.”

“Choosing…”

Until now, he had been fully occupied simply trying to cool everything he could see.

From here on, he would also need to accept the existence of areas he intentionally did not touch.

His room.

Parts of his daily life.

Routes he used on exhausted days.

None of them had to be placed entirely into Cooling Commons.

He could share them, or not.

“Then this is probably the right point in Part 2 to draw a line between what I share and what I don't.”

Yuto created two sections on a new page.

“Things I will share  
- Movement-route logs around town, such as bus stops, station stairs, the shopping street, riverside, and suburban facilities  
- Cooling ideas connected to institutional and corporate projects  
- Reports and phrases written with the assumption that they may be placed in Cooling Commons”

“Things I will not share  
- Detailed layout and daily patterns inside my own room  
- Detailed logs from days when my physical condition was near the limit  
- Places that function as small private escape zones for me alone”

“This line isn't perfect, but it's enough for now.”

Not sharing something did not mean he would not cool it.

It meant only recognizing that it did not need to appear on the blueprint of the entire world.

“If I were to give 0.5 another name, maybe it would be the point where I learned to draw my own boundary.”

With that thought, the small round sticker on the stair landing took on another meaning.

“Take a breath here.”

One point in the city.

At the same time, one point in his own life.

“Cooling for others, for myself, and for institutions or companies.  
It's impossible to cool for everything at once.”

That was why he needed to begin choosing, little by little, who each act of cooling was for.

His phone vibrated one final time.

“Today's daily-life cooling total: 0.044 CC  
Cumulative total: 0.521 CC  
Memo:  
‘On the day you reached 0.5, you cooled your own room and a station staircase a little.’”

“…That sounds incredibly ordinary when phrased like that.”

But that was fine.

An ordinary day could become a milestone.

Yuto added one final line to his notebook.

“Today's line:  
- Inside 0.5 are cooling for someone else and cooling for myself, properly mixed together.”

He put down the pen and exhaled quietly.

The world was still hot.

But within that heat, learning to choose how far he would cool and what he would leave untouched was a change larger than the number 0.5 itself.

---

Thank you for reading Episode 19.

This episode treated reaching 0.5 CC not as a simple game-like milestone, but as one full circuit of Yuto's living zone and the point where he begins choosing what to share and what not to share.

By combining the small “Take a breath here” sign at the station staircase with the cooling of his own room, the episode shows cooling for others and cooling for himself existing on the same day.

Drawing a personal line between what will and will not be shared creates a foundation for Yuto to engage with larger corporate projects, the formal version of Cooling Commons, appropriation and return, and inequality without wearing down his own life and emotional state too severely.

I hope you will continue following how Yuto redraws those boundaries in the world beyond 0.5.
