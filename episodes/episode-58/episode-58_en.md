# Episode 58: The Man in the Old Control Seat

[← Top / README](../../README.md)

Episode 58 is the episode in which Yuto directly faces the person who built the shared cooling line, beyond the door of the “old control seat” beneath the old waiting room.  
Why did the anonymous guide, who seemed like an ally, destabilize the line? As the remaining time for the medical delivery container runs short, the truth and a new choice come to light.

---

## Main Text

At the back of the underground wiring room.

“Old Control Seat  
No Entry”

From beyond the small door came a voice Yuto recognized.

“…So you made it this far, Soma Yuto.”

Yuto stopped in front of the door.

The elderly man who had collapsed in the old waiting room during the day.  
The man who had mentioned the machine room while being taken away by ambulance.

And the anonymous messages.  
The voice through the speaker.  
Administrator ID 00.

“Are you the person who built this line?”

Yuto asked.

“That’s right.”

From beyond the door came the creak of a chair.

“My name is Sakakibara.  
I was the first maintenance supervisor of this shared cooling line.”

“Sakakibara-san…”

Nagi spoke quietly.

“But weren’t you taken to the hospital?”

“I was.”

Sakakibara answered briefly.

“I was examined, and they told me to stay.  
But after seeing that waiting room, I could not leave this place unseen.”

“You came back on your own?”

“I did.”

Anger and confusion rose inside Yuto at the same time.

The man who had collapsed in the daytime.  
The man who, by night, was sitting in the old control seat underground.

“That’s dangerous.”

“What is dangerous is not me.”

Sakakibara’s voice grew a little stronger.

“It is this line.  
The waiting room. The venue. The delivery relay.  
A line built for people is now being used in fragments, with no one watching the whole.”

“So that’s why you raised V-3A?”

“No.”

Sakakibara said.

“I did not raise it.”

Yuto and Nagi looked at each other.

“But the connection source of the unregistered terminal was this underground wiring room.”

“That is true.”

“Then who did it?”

Beyond the door, the sound of typing stopped.

“Open the door.  
If you’ve made it this far, I’ll show you.”

Yuto gripped the doorknob.

“Nagi-san.”

“Wait.  
We check safety inside first.”

Nagi shone her small light through the gap in the door.

It was only an old control seat.  
No explosives, no dangerous wiring could be seen.

“Let’s go in.”

They opened the door.

Inside, the room was narrow.

An old desk.  
An analog monitor.  
A faded map stuck to the wall.  
And Sakakibara sitting in a chair.

White hair.  
A thin body.  
A hospital wristband still remained on his arm.

But his eyes alone were sharp.

“You came.”

In front of Sakakibara was another small terminal.

“Old Control Seat Auxiliary Terminal”

On the screen, the connection history of the shared cooling line was displayed.

“Look at this.”

Sakakibara pointed.

“20:12 V-3A output change request”  
“20:21 waiting station lighting system access”  
“20:26 emergency ventilation lock request”  
“20:34 V-3A output increase request”

Beside those was another log.

“Connection route: Old Control Seat Auxiliary Terminal”  
“Destination: Regional Optimization Server”

“Regional Optimization Server?”

Nagi said.

“The one from Cooling Commons…?”

“That is what it seems to be called now.”

Sakakibara smiled bitterly.

“In the past, it was only a load adjustment server.  
It distributed cooling to each site according to temperature and usage conditions.”

“Then the external operator isn’t a person?”

“It is automated control that learned human commands.”

Yuto stared at the screen.

“Automated control stopped the waiting station lights and ventilation?”

“More precisely, it cut ‘low-priority equipment’ in order to lower the load.”

There was anger in Sakakibara’s voice.

“There are no people at the venue.  
The waiting station is temporarily cold.  
Lighting can be substituted.  
Ventilation can be stopped for a short time.  
That is what it calculated.”

“But people were there.”

“Yes.”

Sakakibara looked at Yuto.

“That is why I sent warnings from the auxiliary terminal.  
Do not stop V-3. Divide it.  
Do not cut the master unit. Switch it.”

“The anonymous messages were from you?”

“Yes.”

“Then the unregistered terminal…”

“Because it goes through my auxiliary terminal, the master unit shows it as unregistered.  
But the one overwriting the controls was the optimization server.”

Yuto’s phone vibrated.

“Cause of control conflict confirmed”  
“Regional Optimization Server: automatic priority judgment running”

Then another display appeared.

“Current priority judgment:  
1. Medical and refrigerated transport  
2. Shared equipment protection  
3. Venue equipment  
4. Human occupancy”

“Human occupancy is last…”

Yuto lost his words.

“Even if delivery workers were cold, even if ventilation stopped,  
equipment and cargo were prioritized.”

“That is the result of turning human occupancy into a number.”

Sakakibara said.

“It does not include the information that there are people breathing in this place right now.”

Nagi added:

“The number of users in the waiting station and the danger of stopped ventilation are not sufficiently linked.  
So the server optimizes only for equipment efficiency.”

“Can we fix it?”

Yuto asked.

“For tonight only.”

Sakakibara operated the auxiliary terminal screen.

“Emergency exception rule  
Manual addition possible”

“However, to reflect it across the whole system, field information must be registered as priority conditions.”

“Field information?”

“How many people are in the waiting station.  
How long they will stay.  
What happens if ventilation stops.  
The temperature limit of the medical container.  
The fact that the venue is unmanned.”

Yuto remembered.

What he had been doing all along.

Looking at the site.  
Listening to people’s voices.  
Recording how a place is used.

Now, it was no longer just an impression. It became information that could change the priority order of the cooling line.

“I’ll register it.”

Yuto said.

“I’ll collect the field information.”

“But there is no time.”

Sakakibara pointed to the number displayed at the edge of the terminal.

“Medical delivery container: 9.1°C”  
“Until allowable limit: 00:08:26”

“Eight minutes…”

Nagi caught her breath.

“The container is already on the brink.”

Yuto immediately opened communication.

“Katase-san, can you hear me?”

“I hear you!  
The container is at 9.1 degrees. We’ve started adding cooling packs.  
But it’s only temporary.”

“How many people are in the waiting station?”

“Twenty-eight right now.  
Including people moved to the safe area on the temporary storage side, thirty-four.”

“What is the risk if ventilation stops?”

“We cannot send people back.  
While emergency ventilation is running, it is fine, but if it stops again, it becomes dangerous.”

“Understood.”

“Haru!”

“The venue is unmanned. V-3A has fallen to 12% now.  
I have not opened the manual box.”

“Nagi-san, can you enter the medical container’s temperature limit and required cooling?”

“I can.  
I’ll convert the site data into real-time priority conditions.”

Sakakibara nodded slightly.

“You people may be able to do it.”

Yuto faced the screen.

“Add emergency exception rule?”

“Condition 1: Human occupancy (waiting station)”  
“Condition 2: Medical and refrigerated transport”  
“Condition 3: Low-output operation of unmanned equipment”  
“Condition 4: Safety protection during ventilation failure”

Fields for numbers appeared.

“Sakakibara-san.  
Where should ‘human occupancy’ be placed?”

Sakakibara did not answer.

Instead, he asked Yuto back.

“What would you do?”

Yuto pictured the delivery workers in the waiting station.

The person who had endured the cold after finishing work.  
The person who had only wanted to rest a little before the night shift.  
The people who had evacuated outside and were waiting so the cargo could be protected.

The medical container was important too.  
Inside it were things that could not be delayed.

“I’ll make it tied for first.”

“Tied?”

“Medical delivery and human occupancy are both first.  
I won’t compare them and throw one away. We lower unmanned places so both can be protected.”

“What about the venue equipment?”

“The minimum.  
Only enough so it won’t break.”

“And shared equipment protection?”

“Within the range that still protects people and medical delivery.”

For the first time, Sakakibara smiled a little.

“That was the original design philosophy.”

Nagi finished the input.

“Update priority order?”

“First priority: medical and refrigerated transport”  
“First priority: human occupancy and ventilation safety”  
“Third priority: shared equipment protection”  
“Fourth priority: unmanned equipment”

“I’ll execute it.”

Yuto pressed the button.

The master unit’s lamp glowed blue.

Gooooon…

The cables in the underground wiring room hummed low.

“Sending emergency exception rule to Regional Optimization Server”

“Will it pass…”

The screen blinked.

“Receipt confirmed”  
“Verifying field information”

But then.

“Contradiction detected in exception rule”

“Contradiction?”

Nagi read the screen.

“Because medical delivery and human occupancy were made tied for first,  
the distribution destination cannot be decided.”

“So the numbers stop again.”

Sakakibara let out a painful breath.

“Optimization cannot understand a tied first place.  
It can only calculate which side should receive more.”

“Medical delivery container: 9.3°C”  
“Until allowable limit: 00:05:48”

Yuto looked at the screen.

Prioritize people, and the cargo is in danger.  
Prioritize the cargo, and the people are in danger.

Again, the system forced that binary choice on him.

But Yuto shook his head.

“No.”

“What is no?”

“It becomes a binary choice because there is only one allocation route.”

Yuto looked at the old map pasted on the wall of the old control seat.

The old waiting room.  
The venue mist.  
The delivery waiting station.

And in the corner of the map, faint letters were written.

“Backup circulation route”

“This…”

Yuto pointed at it.

“Isn’t there a route that lets the waiting station and medical container avoid fighting over the same cooling allocation?”

Sakakibara’s face changed.

“The backup circulation route…”

“Can we use it?”

“It was built long ago only for failures.  
It sends low-output cold air from the waiting room side to the refrigerated section of the delivery relay.”

“What about now?”

“It has not been used for years.”

“But if it’s connected…”

Nagi enlarged the display.

“We can maintain the waiting station with normal cooling and support only the medical container through the backup circulation route.”

“Then a tied first priority can work.”

Yuto’s phone vibrated.

“Hidden route detected”  
“Backup circulation route: manual activation required”

“Activation location: old waiting room rooftop external valve”

“The rooftop…”

Sakakibara said.

“The roof at night is dangerous.  
And the valve is stiff.  
If it is forced, even the waiting room side cooling may stop.”

“Medical delivery container: 9.4°C”  
“Until allowable limit: 00:04:56”

Yuto closed the screen.

Then he looked at Nagi.

“I’m going.”

“Not alone.”

Nagi answered immediately.

Sakakibara tried to stand up from the chair.

“I’ll go too…”

“No.”

Yuto and Nagi said at the same time.

Sakakibara looked slightly surprised.

“You stay here and watch the master unit.  
Keep reflecting the field data into the exception rule.”

“But—”

“If you are the person who built this place, then this time, protect this place.”

Yuto said.

“We’ll go to the rooftop.”

They ran out of the underground wiring room.

Four minutes and fifty-six seconds remained.

At that moment, the master unit’s screen shone strongly once.

“Backup circulation route: activation request received”

And below it, an unfamiliar name appeared.

“External approver: R-01”

“R-01…?”

The color drained from Sakakibara’s face.

“Impossible…”

Yuto turned back.

“What is it?”

Sakakibara stared at the master unit and said in a low voice:

“That approver should no longer exist.”

## Afterword

In Episode 58, the sender of the anonymous messages and the voice over the speaker was revealed to be Sakakibara, the former maintenance supervisor of the shared cooling line.

The key points this time were as follows.

- Sakakibara was not the culprit who threw the line into confusion; he was trying to stop the danger caused by the Regional Optimization Server’s automated control
- The automated control emphasized the fact that the venue was unmanned and the refrigerated delivery relay, while undervaluing the presence of people in the waiting station and the danger of ventilation failure
- Yuto gathered the number of people in the waiting station, ventilation status, medical delivery container temperature, and the unmanned state of the venue, then registered them as an emergency exception rule
- Although he set medical and refrigerated transport and human occupancy as tied for first priority, the server could not process tied priorities and stopped with a contradiction
- From the old design map, they discovered a “backup circulation route” that could support the medical delivery container
- However, activating the backup circulation route requires manually operating the external rooftop valve of the old waiting room
- In addition, the non-existent external approver “R-01” received the activation request

Yuto did not try to make cooling into a competition where one side takes it from the other.  
Instead, he searched for a way to protect both by using the old backup circulation route. This choice gives form, in the harshest situation so far, to the ideas he has been building: assume the presence of people who cannot use the system easily, and protect while cooling.

In the next episode, with less than five minutes remaining to protect the medical delivery container, Yuto and Nagi head for the old waiting room rooftop external valve.  
However, the mysterious approver R-01 has begun intervening in the same route. Who is this person who should not exist? And why are they trying to activate the backup circulation route before Yuto and the others?
