# Episode 20: A Consultation That Does Not Go Too Far

[← Top / README](../../README.md)

Episode 20 takes place immediately after Yuto crosses 0.5 CC and draws a line for himself, only to have that line tested at once.

Just as he tries to preserve the distance of an ordinary resident, a slightly deeper consultation arrives.

---

Tuesday night.

Soma Yuto had just closed the notebook on his desk.

“Today's line:  
- Inside that 0.5 are cooling for someone else, cooling for myself, and both of them properly mixed together.”

The line he had drawn between what he would share and what he would not.

Writing it down had quieted some of the restlessness in his head.

“…Drawing a line doesn't suddenly make everything easier.”

The world was still hot.

The system was still being built.

The corporate case also had a continuation waiting somewhere ahead.

Even so, deciding not to carry everything had begun to become a small support inside him.

Then his phone vibrated.

“Message from nearby player  
Sender: WindArc”

“I thought nothing else would come tonight…”

Yuto opened the message.

“WindArc:  
‘Can I ask you something?  
There will soon be a COOL STREET call for cooling ideas from individual players,  
and I'm unsure how much of the resident logs should be submitted.’”

“…Perfect timing.”

He had only just drawn the line between what he would and would not share.

Immediately afterward came a question asking how much should be exposed.

“I guess that's how it goes.”

Yuto exhaled briefly and continued reading.

“WindArc:  
‘The main categories are public-facing places such as:  
- routes through shopping streets  
- entrances to suburban stores  
- waiting lines near stations  
But it seems some resident players are considering submitting their rooms or highly personal routes too…’”

“…So that's where the instability is.”

He was not the only person unsure whether to draw a line.

Other players were also struggling with how much of themselves to reveal.

“WindArc:  
‘The institutional side seems to want as much as possible,  
but I feel uneasy that some people may exhaust themselves by sharing too much.  
How much do you plan to submit, Soma?’”

“How much…”

Yuto remembered the page he had written earlier.

“Things to share:  
- route logs from the town  
- cooling ideas connected to institutional and corporate projects  
- reports and phrases created with Cooling Commons in mind

Things not to share:  
- details of my room  
- detailed logs from days when my condition was near its limit  
- places that serve as my own refuge”

“At this point, this is the only answer I can give.”

Yuto wrote a short reply.

“Reply:  
‘I plan to limit my submissions to places people already use on the assumption that they are shared.  
Shopping streets, station areas, riverbanks, suburban facilities, things like that.

On the other hand, I think it is better not to submit my room or routes I only use on exhausted days — places that function as my own refuge.’”

A reply came from WindArc several dozen seconds later.

“WindArc:  
‘A refuge…  
That is an extremely important word.’”

“It isn't that dramatic.”

Yuto shrugged.

But for players, a refuge was as important as cooling itself.

They walked through the city to earn Cooling Credit.

They became involved with institutions and corporate cases.

Without somewhere in between where they could remain without thinking about anything, their minds would not last.

“WindArc:  
‘Then in my own proposal, I'll write that the call should remain limited to public-facing places,  
and that private rooms and refuge logs should be excluded.  
It would also reduce the burden on resident players.’”

“He's turning it into a proposal.”

Yuto felt his posture straighten slightly.

The line he had drawn for himself was becoming part of a proposal to the system.

“This is probably how a consultation that doesn't go too far should be answered.”

At that moment, another notification appeared in the corner of the screen.

“Notice from the institutional side:  
‘As part of consideration for the formal version of Cooling Commons,  
we are collecting player opinions on how far foundational-life logs should be included.’”

“…Here it is.”

The formalization of Cooling Commons he had first heard about in Episode 10.

The beta version that began appearing in Episodes 15 through 17.

The realization in Episodes 18 and 19 that his own life foundation could also become a log.

And now he was being asked how far the system should reach.

“I just drew the line, so right now I can answer without hesitation.”

Yuto opened the institutional feedback form.

The field requested a concise statement.

“Question:  
How far should the logs covered by Cooling Commons extend?  
In particular, please state whether life foundations such as private homes should be included.”

“Keep it short, but make it clear.”

Yuto began typing.

“Response:

‘I believe Cooling Commons should be limited to places people use on the assumption that they are shared.

Cooling logs for shopping streets, station areas, parks, riverbanks, suburban facilities, and other places used as public space may reasonably be shared openly and included in the return system.

However, I believe a healthier approach would be to exclude personal life foundations themselves, such as home layouts or detailed logs of private escape routes.

The reasons are:
- resident players may become exhausted by exposing too much of their daily lives;
- such information may directly affect personal safety and privacy;
- removing all refuge spaces may eventually damage the sustainability of cooling activity itself.’”

When he read it again, it sounded more serious than he had expected.

“Well, it's fine to be this serious.”

He pressed send, and a quiet notification returned.

“Your opinion has been received.  
It will be used as reference material in consideration of the formal Cooling Commons framework.”

“When you call Part 2 the ‘institution arc,’ it sounds rigid…  
but in the end, it's really about drawing lines like this.”

Cooling the town.

Building a system.

Taking part in corporate projects.

All of them were also questions of how far to become involved and how much to reveal.

That night, Yuto summarized the day in his notebook.

“Today's events:  
- Consultation from WindArc about the call for cooling ideas from individual players  
  → Replied that submissions should be limited to public-facing places and that life-foundation logs should be excluded  
- Submitted an opinion to the formal Cooling Commons review requesting that life-foundation logs remain outside its scope”

“Today's observations:  
- Situations that test a boundary can arise immediately after it is drawn  
- Keeping life foundations such as homes and refuge spaces outside Cooling Commons is important for the health and sustainability of resident players  
- A response to a consultation that avoids going too far can itself become material for institutional design”

Finally, one line.

“Today's line:  
- Deciding not to reveal everything is also part of designing the rules of cooling.”

He checked his phone. The day's number was modest.

“Earned today: 0.013 CC  
Cumulative total: 0.534 CC”

“A quiet day in terms of numbers…”

Yuto smiled slightly as he closed the notebook.

“But the numbers on the rule side moved quite a lot.”

The world was still hot.

But part of the boundary of Cooling Commons had begun to carry the words of a resident: do not go too far.

---

Thank you for reading Episode 20.

Immediately after Yuto reached 0.5 CC, the questions of how much to reveal and whether life foundations should be included in Cooling Commons returned to him as a concrete consultation and an institutional call for opinions.

The core of this episode is the word “refuge” emerging in his exchange with WindArc and then being converted directly into a proposal to the institutional side.

The boundary of not revealing everything and not carrying everything is presented not merely as self-defense, but as a condition for continuing cooling activity over the long term.

This perspective gives Yuto room to participate in rulemaking without overburdening himself as the story moves into heavier themes such as appropriation, return, and inequality.
