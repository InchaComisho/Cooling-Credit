# Episode 25: The Day the Official Version Begins

[← Top / README](../../README.md)

Episode 25, the first episode of Part 3, shows Yuto witnessing the launch of Cooling Commons as it begins to move as an official version.

It is the day when the daily-life logs, institutional reports, and discomfort reports he has built up are evaluated together. At the same time, it is an opening episode in which the first signs appear that the way those evaluations are presented may already begin to distort.

---

## Main Text

Sunday.

Soma Yuto woke up a little earlier than usual.

The air conditioner was running gently.
The air in the room was being kept at a temperature that was just barely tolerable for this season.
Beside his pillow, his smartphone gave a quiet notification sound.

ThermoBalance was showing a display he had never seen before.

“Notice:
The official version of Cooling Commons will be introduced on a trial basis in this area starting today.

- Daily-life cooling logs
- Institutional reports
- Discomfort reports

All previous records will be evaluated as initial data for the official version.”

“...So it finally starts.”

Yuto slowly sat up.

In Part 1, he had begun cooling his town.
In Part 2, he had connected with institutions and companies, and turned his discomfort into words.
And today, at the beginning of Part 3, all of that was being read in as the foundation of the official version.

“So if it’s the official version, that means this is the real thing.”

Beta version.
Trial operation.
Under discussion.

Those labels were beginning to come off, little by little.

Another notification was lined up on the screen.

“Your level of participation in Cooling Commons will be reevaluated.
Based on your daily-life cooling logs, reports, and discomfort reports,
provisional contribution categories and return categories will be assigned.”

“Contribution categories and return categories...”

Just the combination of words gave him a feeling that was both cold and faintly reassuring.

“I understand contribution, but what form does ‘return’ take...?”

While eating some bread, Yuto scrolled down the page.

A simple explanation appeared.

“Contribution categories, examples:
- Daily-life cooling log contribution
- Institutional report contribution
- Discomfort report contribution
- Corporate project cooperation contribution

Return categories, examples:
- Cooling Credit preference
- Daily-life support link, such as electricity bill assistance
- Priority access to equipment
- Invitations to learning and work opportunities”

“Electricity bill assistance... That’s just ordinary life.”

The “connection to daily-life support” he had briefly heard about in Part 2 had now become official wording.

It might still be under consideration.
But the fact that it appeared on the screen meant it was no longer a zero-probability topic.

“Part 3 feels like the arc where daily life and systems really start mixing together.”

Yuto finished his barley tea, got ready quickly, and went outside.

The sunlight was as harsh as ever.
But now, instead of merely enduring it, he had a viewpoint: how should he go look at this heat today?

On the way to the shopping street, his phone vibrated.

“Cooling Commons official version: initial evaluation results, excerpt

Contribution categories:
Daily-life cooling log contribution: medium
Institutional report contribution: small to medium
Discomfort report contribution: small

Return categories:
Cooling Credit preference: slight
Daily-life support link: registered for consideration
Priority access to equipment: not eligible at present”

“...So this is how it looks.”

His daily-life logs and reports had become labels such as “medium,” “small,” and “slight.”

Numbers were listed as well.

“Word contribution points: +5
Cooling log reference points: +12
Institutional report reference points: +7
Discomfort report reference points: +2

Total: +26
Daily-life support link consideration level: Lv.1”

“Level 1, huh...”

His electricity bill would not immediately go down.
But it meant he was no longer someone who would not be considered at all.

“I’m glad, but it still feels abstract.”

Yuto headed toward the shopping street.

Under the street trees, the “Take a Breath Here” poster was still there.
The small plate at the foot of the bench.
The COOL STREET sign.

He noticed that a small QR code had been added to the edge of each one.

He moved closer and scanned it.

“This poster’s slogan and cooling layout refer to the initial data of the official version of Cooling Commons.
This design is based on resident players’ logs, reports, and discomfort reports.”

“They even wrote ‘discomfort reports’...”

His name was not there.
But the phrase “resident players’ logs, reports, and discomfort reports” made the origin of the design one step more visible than before.

His phone vibrated.

“Message from a nearby player
Sender: WindArc”

“WindArc:
The official version has started.
I just heard from the design team that discomfort report items will be made one of the official evaluation axes.”

“An official evaluation axis...”

Yuto felt both a little relieved and a little afraid.

“If even discomfort gets evaluated, that means the responsibility to speak up increases too.”

He kept reading.

“WindArc:
Also, for people with Daily-life Support Link Lv.1,
there seems to be a proposal to preferentially consider electricity bill assistance for certain hours during the next summer peak.
It has not been decided yet, though.”

“Electricity bill assistance...”

His room’s air conditioner.
The heat-insulating sheet.
The furniture arrangement.

Those were things he had repositioned in Part 2 as his “life foundation.”
Now a thin pipe from the institutional side was beginning to reach toward that foundation.

“So the official version really is about daily life.”

After passing through the shopping street, he headed toward the small nursery school a little way from the station.

It was the small nursery school he had once been involved with in Part 2.
He wanted to know whether that cooling log had been read into the official version’s evaluation data.

Near the gate, in the shade of a tree, a modest plate had been added.

“This shaded space refers to daily-life cooling logs from the official version of Cooling Commons.
This is a trial layout to protect a ‘Take a Breath Here’ space for children and guardians.”

“...That phrase is exactly what I wrote.”

Take a breath here.
Children.
Guardians.

In the depth of his chest, happiness and a little fear mixed together.

“My words are entering actual places where people live.”

At the same time, the more signs and plates using “Take a Breath Here” increased, the more unavoidable the question of return, which he had pointed out in his discomfort report, became.

On the way back to his apartment, his phone vibrated again.

“Message from the Cooling Commons design team, excerpt:

With the initial introduction of the official version,
we inform you that discomfort reports concerning anonymous contribution and insufficient return have been incorporated into the evaluation axes.

At present,
for resident players with Daily-life Support Link Lv.1 or higher,
a trial framework proposal for electricity bill assistance during summer peak hours has been placed on the discussion table.

However,
further discussion is still needed regarding:
- how to select support recipients
- the relationship between support and contribution.”

“How to select support recipients...”

Yuto instinctively became wary of those words.

“If they do something like supporting people in order of how much they contributed, it will definitely distort somewhere.”

Cooling Commons.
An open space.
Resident players.

If “support slots” and “amount of contribution” became too tightly connected, a different problem could appear: not only the risk that people who submit more lose more, but also the risk that people who cannot submit are left behind.

“Part 3 is probably where the issue of inequality starts coming in...”

Yuto returned to his room and turned on the air conditioner.
He set the temperature a little high.
While the fan circulated the air, he sat at his desk.

He opened his notebook and wrote down what had happened today.

“Today’s events
- Cooling Commons official version was introduced on a trial basis in this area
  → Previous daily-life cooling logs, institutional reports, and discomfort reports were read in as initial evaluation data
  → Contribution categories and return categories were provisionally assigned
  → Registered as Daily-life Support Link Lv.1

- Wording was added to the shopping street ‘Take a Breath Here’ poster and the nursery school shaded-space plate stating that they refer to resident logs, reports, and discomfort reports

- The evaluation axis concerning ‘not stolen, but insufficiently returned’ was officially incorporated into design meeting materials
  → A trial electricity bill assistance framework during summer peak hours was placed on the discussion table”

“Today’s realization
- The official version has begun to operate not only as a system for cooling the town, but also as a system for supporting daily life
- There is both relief and fear in seeing words and logs handled through labels such as contribution categories and return categories
- Depending on how support recipients are selected, Cooling Commons could also become a place of support and inequality, so from here on, where lines are drawn and how far things are connected will become even more important”

Finally, he wrote the usual line.

“Today’s line
- On the day the official version began, my words moved a little closer to daily life, and at the same time, the shadow of support and inequality moved a little closer too.”

When he checked his phone, his Cooling Credit had increased slightly.

“Earned today: 0.016 CC
Total: 0.596 CC”

“The numbers are the same as always.
But the labels increased all at once today.”

Yuto closed his notebook and quietly breathed out.

The world was still hot.
But within that heat, the official version of Cooling Commons had begun to move, and the questions of daily life, support, and inequality were slowly being placed on the same table.

## Afterword

Thank you for reading Episode 25, Part 3 Episode 1.

This episode depicts the entrance to Part 3:

- Cooling Commons begins operating as the official version.
- The daily-life cooling logs, institutional reports, and discomfort reports built up so far are read in as initial evaluation data.
- Labels such as contribution categories, return categories, and daily-life support links begin to approach Yuto’s own life.

The key points are:

- The discomfort of “not stolen, but insufficiently returned” begins to be incorporated as an official evaluation axis on the institutional side.
- Daily-life support, such as electricity bill assistance, advances one step from “under consideration” to “on the discussion table.”
- At the same time, the questions of how support recipients are chosen and how contribution is connected to support begin to appear as possible seeds of future inequality and distortion.

In Part 3, the themes of the official version and daily-life support, contribution and return, and support and inequality will gradually be deepened from the perspectives of institutions, companies, and residents.

From the next episodes onward, small misunderstandings around support slots and more explicit corporate ways of “taking contribution” will begin to appear, while Yuto’s position within them becomes clearer.
