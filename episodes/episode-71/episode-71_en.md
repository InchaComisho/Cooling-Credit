# Episode 71 — Thirty Minutes, Head-On

## Foreword

Episode 71 takes place at Point A in North Pier Seven, the Third Workers’ Rest Shelter, where Yuto faces the city-area No. 3 player, HEATWAVE-3, directly.
HEATWAVE-3’s plan to reduce output to 40% is rational. Yuto cannot simply oppose it. Within thirty minutes, he must prove an alternative operation that preserves wide-area cooling capacity while still leaving a usable place for workers who have difficulty relocating.

---

## Main Story

“North Pier Seven — Priority Proposal Rights Contest”

“Target: Point A — Third Workers’ Rest Shelter”

“Candidates:
 Yuto Soma
 HEATWAVE-3”

“Winning condition:
 Demonstrate, within 30 minutes, an alternative operation superior to the 40% reduction plan.”

8:30 a.m.

More people than usual had gathered in front of the Third Workers’ Rest Shelter.

Workers.
Port-management staff.
Observers from the Metropolitan Cooling Evaluation Bureau.
A monitoring drone circling overhead.

And beside the shelter entrance, two proposals were displayed.

“HEATWAVE-3 Proposal
 Cooling output: 100% → 40%
 Alternative destination: Second Rest Shelter”

“Yuto Soma Proposal
 Alternative operation: Not yet submitted”

“‘Not yet submitted’ puts me at quite a disadvantage,” Yuto muttered.

Beside him, HEATWAVE-3 adjusted the collar of his black cooling vest.

“Because you haven’t submitted anything.”

“I’m submitting one now.”

“Then do it. But thirty minutes from now, what matters won’t be a nice story.”

HEATWAVE-3 looked toward the Third Rest Shelter.

“How much cooling capacity you preserved. Whether the people here could rest without strain. Whether the route to the Second Rest Shelter kept moving. If you can’t show those in numbers, you lose.”

“I know.”

Yuto looked inside the shelter.

Benches.
A water dispenser.
An air vent near the entrance.
A cooling unit along the wall.

The morning users were already increasing.

A short break before work.
A pause between cargo-handling tasks.
A few minutes to sit down, drink water, and take off a helmet.

Average stay: roughly eight minutes.

But some people could not return to the worksite without those eight minutes.

“Let me confirm the rules.”

Yuto opened his terminal.

“Demonstration time: 30 minutes”

“Evaluation items:
 1. Reduction of wide-area cooling load
 2. Continued use by people with mobility difficulty
 3. Safe guidance to the Second Rest Shelter
 4. Emergency departure rate
 5. Reproducibility”

“Reproducibility...”

A one-time trick would not win this contest.

HEATWAVE-3 operated his own terminal.

“HEATWAVE-3 Proposal — Demonstration Start”

The shelter’s cooling display changed.

“Third Rest Shelter: 100% → 40%”

The cool airflow gradually weakened.

“You’re lowering it already?” Yuto asked.

“The side that demonstrates first gets results first,” HEATWAVE-3 replied.

“The Second Rest Shelter is back online. Anyone who can move will be guided there.”

A staff member took position at the entrance.

“Second Rest Shelter this way
 4-minute walk
 Cooling output: 100%”

Several workers looked at the sign and stood up.

“The Second is back?”

“Then let’s go.”

Those who could walk began moving.

But others remained almost immediately.

A man dragging one leg.
A woman wearing a lower-back support belt.
A pale young worker.

“Is it hard for you to get to the Second Rest Shelter?” Yuto asked.

The woman with the support belt answered.

“I can walk. But our breaks are short. If I go there and back, I won’t have time left to sit.”

“If I stay here, I can get back to my work zone right away,” said the man with the limp.

“Walking gets me before the heat does.”

Yuto entered what they had told him.

“Users with mobility or time constraints: 6”

Then Haru’s voice came over the comms.

“I measured the route from Point A to the Second Rest Shelter. The four-minute estimate assumes you’re carrying nothing. With work helmets, safety boots, and congested walkways, it’s six and a half minutes one way.”

“Thirteen minutes round trip,” Yuto said.

“From a place where the average rest itself is eight minutes.”

“That’s why I’m leaving the Third Shelter at 40%,” HEATWAVE-3 said.

“I’m not shutting it down completely.”

“Is 40% enough for people who can’t relocate easily?”

“That’s what I’m proving.”

HEATWAVE-3 addressed the workers.

“If you’re in poor condition, remain in the Third Rest Shelter. Everyone else will be guided to the Second in sequence.”

It was rational.

Fast.

Narrow the group that stays. Disperse the people who can move.

“Third Rest Shelter users: 18 → 10”

“Cooling output: 40%”

“Wide-area load reduction: +0.8%”

The monitoring drone circled lower.

An evaluation appeared on Yuto’s terminal as well.

“HEATWAVE-3 Proposal
 Load reduction: Good
 User dispersal: Good
 Stay of mobility-limited users: Under continued observation”

“Not bad,” Yuto admitted.

HEATWAVE-3 did not answer.

He only watched the display.

“But my plan is different.”

“I’m listening.”

Yuto looked around.

The Third Rest Shelter.
The small awning beside the entrance.
The neighboring materials area.
And a short distance away, an unused temporary roll-call tent.

The tent appeared to be for port safety checks.

No one was using it now.

“I’m splitting the Third Shelter into two.”

“Two?”

“Indoors will be only for people with mobility difficulties or those whose condition needs priority. Short-stay users move to the roll-call tent beside the entrance.”

“There’s no cooling in that tent.”

“There is.”

Yuto opened the map on his phone.

Auxiliary Cooling Terminal L-4 at Point C.

It was not meant for continuous operation.
But the main cooling tower had recovered some reserve.

“North Pier Seven — Wide-area cooling reserve: 8.4%”

“You intend to use L-4?” HEATWAVE-3’s gaze sharpened.

“I’ll route up to 0.6% of the auxiliary branch to Point A.”

“L-4 is the support line for Point C, the north passage, and Point D.”

“I want to verify whether it can connect here too.”

Yuto contacted Nagi.

“Nagi, is there a route from L-4 to the Point A roll-call tent?”

“Not directly. But it connects to the old water line at Point A. We can’t drive an air unit with it, but there’s a chance we can supply chilled water.”

“Chilled water...”

“For simple cooling towels and maybe foot-level misting,” Nagi said.

“But L-4 can use up to 0.6% output. If an emergency happens at Point C, Point D, or the north passage, keep it ready to return immediately.”

“Understood.”

HEATWAVE-3 asked, “Then how far will you reduce the Third Shelter itself?”

“I want to keep it at 55%, not 40%.”

“What?”

“The indoor area, where the mobility-limited users remain, stays at 55%. Short-stay users move to the tent. The tent uses low-output chilled water from L-4 and sea breeze.”

“That only cuts the Third Shelter by 45%.”

“Yes. But it reduces the number of people sent to the Second Rest Shelter, which lowers walkway congestion and guidance load.”

“What about wide-area reserve?”

“Even after using 0.6% from L-4, I think we can bring the total close to your plan.”

“Nothing but ‘I think.’”

“So I’m demonstrating it now.”

Yuto operated his terminal.

“Yuto Soma Proposal — Provisional Registration”

“Third Rest Shelter: maintain 55%”
“Roll-call tent: convert to short-stay rest use”
“L-4 auxiliary branch: request up to 0.6%”
“Second Rest Shelter: guide only those who wish to relocate”

“Begin demonstration?”

“Begin.”

FROST-9 immediately came over the comms.

“L-4 use approved. Automatic shutdown the moment you exceed 0.6%.”

“Thank you.”

“Repay me with results.”

◇

Folding chairs were arranged inside the roll-call tent.

Haru opened the side curtain just enough for the sea breeze to pass through.

“The wind comes from here. Open the whole thing and the paperwork will fly. Stop at half.”

“Got it.”

Soma called out to workers at the entrance.

“Anyone going back quickly, over here! You can sit, drink water, and cool just your neck!”

“Not indoors?”

“Indoor seats are for people who have trouble moving! This side is for five-minute breaks!”

At first, no one moved.

That was natural.

No one wanted to leave the cooled interior voluntarily for a tent.

But once Yuto confirmed the chilled-water connection, slightly cool water began flowing from an old tap beside the tent.

Nagi’s voice came over the comms.

“L-4 stable at 0.4%. Water flow established through Point A’s old line.”

“The cold water is live!”

Yuto soaked a simple towel.

“You can wet this and cool your neck or arms! If you don’t want your clothes wet, just cool your feet!”

One worker stepped inside the tent.

“If it’s only five minutes, this is fine.”

Then another.

“Better to free the indoor seats for people with bad backs.”

Little by little, people shifted.

“Third Rest Shelter users: 10 → 7”
“Roll-call tent users: 0 → 5”

Yuto checked the indoor cooling display.

“Third Rest Shelter: 55%”
“Indoor temperature: 25.4°C”

Under HEATWAVE-3’s 40% plan, the room temperature had begun to exceed 27°C.

“How is it inside?” Yuto asked those who remained.

The woman with the back support smiled faintly.

“Easier than before. This is enough.”

The man with the limp exhaled deeply.

“It helps not having to go all the way to the Second.”

Yuto’s terminal chimed.

“Mobility-limited stay: Stable”
“Second Rest Shelter guidance: Congestion reduced”
“L-4 auxiliary branch: 0.5%”

“0.5... we still have margin.”

But the next moment, Nagi’s tone changed.

“Yuto, wait. User volume is increasing around L-4 at Point C.”

“Point C?”

“Drivers are gathering at the K-17 water post in the north passage. It may be because Point B’s water supply is temporarily congested.”

“Another chain reaction...”

Yuto looked at his terminal.

“L-4 auxiliary branch: 0.5%”
“Point C emergency-demand forecast”

“If we keep using L-4 here, we won’t be able to send it back when Point C or the north passage needs it,” HEATWAVE-3 said.

“That’s why I told you. A plan that keeps borrowing unregistered reserve whenever convenient has poor reproducibility.”

“It’s not over yet.”

“How much time?”

Yuto looked at the screen.

“Priority Proposal Rights Contest — Time remaining: 00:08:17”

Eight minutes.

Protect the Point A demonstration.
Return L-4 to Point C.
And somehow still beat HEATWAVE-3’s 40% plan.

Then a strong gust hit the roll-call tent.

Whap!

The side curtain snapped violently.

Wet towels flew, and the hose at the old water tap nearly came loose.

“Not good!”

The short-stay users jumped to their feet in surprise.

If the chilled water stopped, even the people who needed to remain indoors could overflow back into the main shelter.

And if L-4 remained committed to Point A, the new demand in the north passage could not be met.

Yuto’s phone vibrated hard.

“Emergency branch — Select an option”

“A: Keep L-4 at Point A and re-secure the roll-call tent”
“B: Return L-4 to Point C and reduce the Third Rest Shelter to 40%”
“C: Create a different short-stay route between here and the Second Rest Shelter”

Eight minutes remained.

The real contest had only just begun.

## Afterword

Episode 71 begins the thirty-minute demonstration contest between Yuto and HEATWAVE-3 at Point A, the Third Workers’ Rest Shelter.

Key points in this episode:

- HEATWAVE-3 executes a rational plan: reduce the Third Rest Shelter from 100% to 40% and guide workers who can move to the Second Rest Shelter.
- However, the real measured travel time to the Second Rest Shelter is about six and a half minutes one way once safety boots, helmets, and congested routes are included—roughly thirteen minutes round trip. That is a heavy burden for workers whose average rest is only eight minutes, especially those with mobility difficulties.
- Yuto proposes keeping the Third Rest Shelter at 55% while separating short-stay users into the nearby roll-call tent.
- He uses Auxiliary Cooling Terminal L-4 from Point C within its maximum 0.6% limit, combining chilled water, wet towels, and sea breeze in the tent.
- Mobility-limited users can continue resting indoors at around 25.4°C, while short-stay users begin moving to the tent.
- However, L-4 was originally intended for emergencies at Point C, the north passage, and Point D, and new demand begins appearing at Point C.
- Strong wind destabilizes the roll-call tent curtain and water hose, leaving Yuto with eight minutes and a new emergency choice.

Yuto’s plan does more than preserve output. It separates users who can relocate from those for whom relocation is difficult, then gives each group a different way to rest.
At the same time, HEATWAVE-3 is right about one weakness: a valuable reserve branch like L-4 cannot be kept tied up at a single site forever. And now real-world weather—the wind itself—is beginning to undermine the tent operation.

Next time, the eight-minute emergency branch begins.
Will Yuto keep L-4 at Point A, return it to Point C, or create an entirely new short-stay route to the Second Rest Shelter? Against the city-area No. 3 player HEATWAVE-3, Yuto must prove that his “way to keep something alive” can hold together until the very end.
