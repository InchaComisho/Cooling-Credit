# Episode 41: Beyond the Numbers

[← Top / README](../../README.md)

Episode 41 is written as a mid-Part 4 episode in which the review needed to sustain support begins from both the numbers and the field.  
This time, in response to uneven usage patterns and small operational burdens, Yuto deepens the adjustments needed to keep the support going.

---

## Main Text

Wednesday.  
Soma Yuto was looking at the usage records up to the previous day in the station-front plaza that morning.

On the surface, things were going well.  
Place-based support continued to be used.  
Voice guidance had taken root, and more people were speaking to the guidance staff.

But beyond those numbers, he could see a few concerning imbalances.

“Usage concentrated in the morning.”  
“Crowding after 3 p.m.”  
“Longer stays on the shopping street side, shorter use on the station-front side.”  
“High ratio of elderly users in the city office lobby.”  
“Concentrated use in front of the nursery school during drop-off and pickup times.”

“…The way it’s used is completely different depending on the place.”

Yuto closed the memo.

Even if it was the same support, the timing needed at the station front, shopping street, library, city office, and nursery school was different.  
In other words, simply spreading the same format unchanged would put strain somewhere.

WindArc arrived.

“Good morning.  
Did you see the usage imbalance?”

“I did.  
The numbers look good, but each place has a different rhythm.”

“Yes.  
That is why we are now adjusting not by ‘average,’ but by location.”

“That makes sense. Averages make things easy to miss.”

“Exactly.  
For example, at the station front, short turnover is important,  
but in the shopping street, being able to sit a little longer matters.  
At the city office, the role is absorbing waiting time.  
In front of the nursery school, it is a momentary rest.  
Even the same bench has a different role.”

Yuto nodded in understanding.

The more the numbers rise,  
the more dangerous it becomes to stop at “many people are using it.”  
Unless they looked at where, who, and how people were using it,  
the misalignments in support would disappear from view.

In the morning, a wave of crowding arrived at the station-front plaza.  
Commuters used the benches, while a little farther away, parents with children cooled off.

The guidance staff also looked slightly busy.

“The staff are under a little pressure, aren’t they?”

“Yes.  
Because spoken guidance has increased, there are times during peaks when they cannot keep up.”

“That should be improved.”

“Yes.  
There is a proposal to divide staff between morning and afternoon,  
and another to automate simple guidance only during times when usage concentrates.”

“Automate?”

“A shortened version of the voice guidance.  
It would repeat only, ‘You can rest here. It is free. No application is required,’ at fixed intervals.”

Yuto thought for a while, then nodded.

“Long explanations may not be necessary, but short repetition might lead to reassurance.”

“Yes.  
Repeating that ‘you may use it now’ has the effect of reducing hesitation.”

Before noon, in the city office lobby, one elderly person was speaking to a staff member.

“Mornings are all right, but in the afternoon there are times when I can’t sit.”

“I’m sorry.  
Because that is a concentrated usage time, we are working on adding seats.”

“I’m not blaming you.  
It’s just that waiting a long time makes me tired.”

Yuto’s brows tightened slightly at those words.

Support only for “people who can wait” has little meaning.  
What matters is how to reach people who cannot wait, people with little strength, and people who cannot move during crowded times.

WindArc took notes on the spot.

“I’ll take this voice back immediately.”

“Please.  
Even if the numbers look good, if the fatigue of waiting people remains, it still isn’t enough.”

In the afternoon, Yuto went around to the shopping street.  
There, seats that allowed longer stays were well received, but because stay times had grown, there were sometimes not enough seats elsewhere.

A shopkeeper said with a wry smile,

“It’s a happy problem, though.  
A lot of people say, ‘If I can sit here, I’ll rest here.’”

“That’s also proof that the support is taking root.”

“Sure.  
But when more resting places appear, you also need to distribute people.”

“That’s true.”

Support is not simply better the more it increases.  
It is necessary to adjust the role of each location and divide the flow.

In the evening, WindArc sent an additional update.

“WindArc:  
We will begin location-specific operational adjustments.  
The station front will be oriented toward short use, the shopping street toward medium-length stays, the city office toward absorbing waiting time, and the nursery school front toward temporary rest. We will slightly change the wording and guidance for each.”

“That sounds right.”

“WindArc:  
Also, we will strengthen simple voice guidance only during peak times.  
We will try adding the phrase, ‘You can sit now.’”

“You can sit now…  
That makes sense. If people cannot see the timing, they hesitate.”

“WindArc:  
Yes.  
Support depends not only on quantity, but on how timing is communicated.”

That night, Yuto opened his notebook.

“Today’s events  
・Confirmed that usage patterns show location-specific imbalances and time-of-day waves  
・Adjustments began to divide roles among the station front, shopping street, city office, and nursery school front  
・A proposal appeared to strengthen simple voice guidance in order to reduce staff burden during peak times  
・Confirmed again that design must reach not ‘people who can wait,’ but ‘people who need it now.’”

“Today’s realization  
・Even if the numbers are good, adjustment is needed if people get tired differently depending on the place  
・Support should be seen not through averages, but through the rhythm of the field  
・Part 4 has entered the stage of finely arranging support that has already spread.”

Finally, one line.

“Today’s line  
・The more support is used, the more important it becomes to see where and how it is being used.”

As usual, his phone showed a small increase in Cooling Credit.

“Earned today: 0.009 CC  
Total: 0.761 CC”

“Beyond the numbers, huh…”

Yuto looked out the window.  
What he could see was not only the increasing number of users.  
It was where, when, and how each one of them had sat down.  
Only when they could see that much could they say that support was truly reaching people.

## Afterword

Episode 41 depicted how, even when support usage data looks good at first glance, there are imbalances by location and time period, and adjustments must be made to match them.  
As Part 4 progresses, Yuto is shifting from the role of “expanding the system” to the role of “seeing the fatigue that exists beyond the numbers.”

The key points this time were as follows.  
- Usage differs among the station front, shopping street, city office, and nursery school front
- Concentrated usage times lead to staff burden and fatigue from waiting
- What matters is reaching the “people who need it now,” which averages cannot show

Support is not completed just because its initial introduction succeeds.  
Looking at imbalances in how it is used and fine-tuning the role of each place has become one of the major themes of Part 4.  
In the next episode, we will follow how this adjustment appears more directly as a feeling in the town.
