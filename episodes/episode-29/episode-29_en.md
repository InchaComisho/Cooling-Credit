# Episode 29: The Summer That Did Not Receive Support

[← Top / README](../../README.md)

Episode 29 is the episode in which, after an afternoon spent by someone who did receive support, the way things look to those who did not receive it becomes clearer.

Yuto himself was on the side that received support, but when he compares that fact with the people around him, the bias of the system begins to appear not as an abstract rule, but as emotion.

---

## Main Text

Friday.

Soma Yuto stood at the convenience store register, handling the lunchtime customers.

The air conditioning was working reasonably well.

But every time the entrance opened, the heat outside flowed in and slowly overwrote the air inside the store.

"It's hot today."

A middle-aged regular customer said so in front of the register.

"It really is. Going outside is brutal."

Yuto replied the way he always did.

A short exchange.

Even so, today the weight of those words sounded a little different.

Yesterday, the trial frame of the Cooling Commons daily-life support link had actually been applied.

His own room had become a little easier to endure.

But within the same heat wave, there were people who had not received support.

After knowing that, the backs of the people leaving the store bothered him just a little more.

During his lunch break, Yuto opened his phone in the back room.

In the player chat, messages were flowing from people who had seen the support experience logs from the day before.

'Bronze Player A:

"I read the support log.

I think it was good that it honestly said the support was helpful."'

'Bronze Player B:

"But from my side, since I wasn't eligible,

it's hard to describe the feeling of only the rooms of people who received support becoming a little cooler."'

'WindArc:

"Yesterday, the kitchen at my workplace was unbearably hot,

so while reading the household air-conditioning support log, I had mixed feelings.

I understand it as the first form of the system,

but the feeling that only the people who got support became a little more comfortable is still there."'

Yuto let out a small breath.

"The gratitude on the side that received support,

and the rough feeling left on the side that did not,

are not separate stories."

The system was moving as a first attempt.

But bias in support immediately appeared as emotion.

At that moment, a direct message arrived from WindArc.

'WindArc:

"Do you have a little time to talk today between work?

After seeing yesterday's support log,

I feel like we should also summarize the voices from the side of the summer that did not receive support."'

Yuto thought for a moment, then replied, "Sure."

In the evening, after his shift ended, he met WindArc on a bench at the edge of the shopping street.

There was shade.

But the backrest of the bench still held a little heat.

"Were you able to receive support yesterday?"

"Yes.

I was selected for the electricity bill support for the air conditioner in my room."

"I thought so.

...How was it?"

"I was grateful.

But at the same time, I became strongly aware that only I had become a little more comfortable."

WindArc nodded.

"That's the point.

When support reaches someone, it should simply be a form of help.

But it also makes it visible that someone else was left out."

"The people who did not receive it are even less visible, aren't they?"

"Yes.

People who were outside the target group even hesitate a little over whether they have the right to talk about the system."

"I understand that."

"And this time, because there was a condition of daily-life support link Level 1 or higher,

people at Level 0 felt as if they had been placed outside the conversation from the beginning."

Yuto thought while feeling the heat of the bench.

"As a system, since this was the first trial frame, there are probably parts that couldn't be helped.

But feeling like you're outside the conversation must be pretty hard."

"Exactly.

That's why what I want to summarize today is not only the impressions of people who received support,

but also how the people who did not receive it felt."

WindArc took out a phone and showed him a memo screen.

'The summer that received support / the summer that did not receive support

- Those who received support were able to use air conditioning with a little more peace of mind.
- Those who did not receive support became conscious, within the same heat, of being outside the system.
- Those outside the target group found it easy to hesitate even to join the topic.
- As a first support trial, it has meaning,
  but it leaves behind a difference in felt experience between the side that receives support and the side that does not.'

"...This can almost be used as it is."

"Yes.

But I want to reword it just a little."

WindArc continued.

"The summer that did not receive support does not simply mean a summer without support.

I want to include the nuance of a summer in which, amid the heat,

you are made to feel that you were not included as a target."

"I see..."

When Yuto heard that expression, something caught slightly in his chest.

It was not only the fact that support had not arrived.

Being made to feel that one had not been included gradually took effect.

"In that case, maybe it should be summarized as the discomfort of those who did not receive support."

"I think so.

And I also think we should avoid turning the gratitude of those who did receive support into a simple heartwarming story."

"...Right."

"Behind the gratitude,

there is the awareness that this did not reach everyone.

I think it is important to leave both of those together."

Yuto slowly nodded.

"So Part 3 is about leaving both sides behind, isn't it?"

"Yes.

Once support begins,

logs tend to split into logs that write only joy and logs that write only dissatisfaction.

But in reality, there are many emotions in between."

A little evening wind passed through the shopping street.

Feeling it, Yuto said,

"Then today, I'll properly write about the side of the summer that did not receive support too."

"Please do."

That night, Yuto returned to his apartment and opened his notebook.

'Today's events

- Shared my own experience as a support recipient within the player community.
  → Reconfirmed that gratitude and rough discomfort can coexist.
- Met with WindArc and organized the impressions from the side of the summer that did not receive support.
  → The feeling among those outside the target group of being placed outside the system.
  → The feeling that Level 0 players had difficulty joining the topic.
  → The need to prevent the gratitude of those who received support from walking on its own as a simple good story.'

'Today's realizations

- Support makes the lives of those who receive it a little easier, while inevitably leaving behind emotions among those who do not.
- The phrase "the summer that did not receive support" does not simply mean a lack of support. It expresses the feeling itself of not being included within the system.
- Recording gratitude and rough discomfort at the same time, without leaning entirely toward either one, is becoming the role of Cooling Commons logs in Part 3.'

Finally, one line.

'Today's line

- The summer that received support and the summer that did not are not separate seasons. They stand side by side inside the same heat.'

When he checked his phone, his Cooling Credit had increased only slightly.

'Today's earnings: 0.012 CC

Total: 0.648 CC'

"The number is small.

But giving shape to the voices from the side that did not receive support is a big thing."

The world was still hot.

And within that heat, the benefits of support and the emotions of those who had slipped through the support frame were gradually being put into words as things standing side by side in the same summer.

## Afterword

Thank you for reading Episode 29.

This episode organized, through Yuto's conversation with WindArc, the emotional gap between those who received support and those who did not after the support system actually began moving.

In particular, by using the expression "the summer that did not receive support," the episode brings to the front not merely the absence of support, but the feeling of being made aware that one was not included within the system.

The important point of this episode is not to turn the experience of support into a simple heartwarming story.

The gratitude is real, but behind it are people who were outside the target group, people who could not easily join the conversation, and people who became conscious of being outside the system.

Leaving both of those in the same notebook is becoming an increasingly strong part of Yuto's role in Part 3.

In the next episode, we will dig further into how these emotions from the side that did not receive support spread into discomfort reports for the system and into the atmosphere of the player community.

I hope you will continue following Yuto's viewpoint as he looks at both gratitude and rough discomfort at the same time.
