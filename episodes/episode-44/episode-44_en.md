# Episode 44: The Prior Visitor at the Edge of Town

[← Top / README](../../README.md)

Episode 44 is the episode where Yuto steps for the first time into the “town outskirts on the weekend” shown in Episode 43.  
Unlike the station front or shopping street so far, this is a small contest in a place where support is hard to reach. It also depicts Yuto’s encounter with an unexpected “prior visitor.”

---

## Main Text

Saturday, four in the afternoon.

Soma Yuto left the station and walked in the opposite direction from the shopping street he usually visited.

The words that had appeared in the notification stayed in his mind.

“Next candidate: town outskirts on the weekend.”

Even if it was called the edge of town, it was not a completely empty place.

About twenty minutes on foot from the station.  
An old factory site.  
Large warehouses.  
A logistics center.  
Secondhand shops along the main road.  
And a flea market venue where people gathered only on weekends.

“…This really is different from the support areas so far.”

At the station front, there were benches.  
In the shopping street, there were shops.  
At the library and city office, there were indoor places to rest.

But the outskirts had none of that.

What existed there were wide roads and parking lots with harsh reflected heat.  
There were few places to stop.  
There were few places to escape the heat.

And today was the weekend.

More people than expected had gathered at the flea market venue.  
Old clothes.  
Small goods.  
Used home appliances.  
Food stalls.  
Prize games for children.

It was lively.  
But at the edge of the venue, people with tired faces were drinking while standing.

“There’s nowhere to sit…”

Yuto looked around the venue.

There were a few simple tents.  
But all of them were for vendors.  
There were few chairs for resting.  
And almost all of the shade was already taken.

His phone vibrated.

“Priority condition:  
Discovery and improvement of ‘places where people can stop’  
in the town outskirts on the weekend.”

“Discovery and improvement…”

Yuto read the notification again.

“So rather than building something new here… first I need to find a place that can be used.”

That was when it happened.

“You’re in the way.”

A low voice reached him.

When Yuto turned around, a young man with a large backpack was standing there.  
A black cap.  
A cooling towel around his neck.  
In one hand, he held a worn small terminal.

“…Sorry.”

Yuto moved aside, and the man seemed about to walk past him.

But after a few steps, he stopped and glanced at Yuto’s phone screen.

“You can see the priority condition?”

Yuto grew slightly guarded.

“You too?”

“More or less.”

The man adjusted the towel around his neck.

“I’m Haru.  
I’ve been watching this area since last week.”

“I’m Soma Yuto.  
Since last week? Have you already started improving it?”

“No.  
I can’t.”

Haru pointed toward the back of the flea market venue.

“Over there.  
There’s an unused loading space at the edge of the venue.  
It has a roof, and the wind passes through.  
But management says they can’t open it as a resting place.”

“Why not?”

“It’s the route vendors use to bring things in.  
If people gather there, they could obstruct traffic.”

“…I see.”

It was not enough just to make a place where people could rest.  
At the edge of town, logistics and vehicle movement were close by.  
If done poorly, it could become dangerous.

“Then we have to look for somewhere else, don’t we?”

“I looked.  
But there’s nowhere with better conditions than that.”

Haru sounded irritated.

“So people are standing around again today, while nothing changes.”

Yuto looked toward the loading space.

It did indeed have a roof.  
Unused pallets were stacked along the wall, and several carts sat farther inside.  
It seemed likely to overlap with people’s path, and it was definitely risky.

But it did not look completely unusable.

“…What if we divided it by time?”

“Huh?”

“Deliveries aren’t heavy all the time, right?”

Haru narrowed his eyes slightly.

“In the morning, and before closing.  
Other than that, there aren’t many.”

“Then we make it a rest area only in between.  
Put up a rope on the pallet side so people don’t go toward the vehicle lane.  
Guide it as a place that can be used only for a short time…”

“Don’t say it like it’s simple.”

Haru’s voice was sharp.

But his eyes were not completely rejecting the idea.

“You need staff to guide people.  
You need rope.  
You need signage.  
Who’s going to do that?”

“…We’ll find someone who can.”

“That’s the hardest part.”

Haru said that and began walking toward the back of the venue.

Yuto hesitated for a moment, then followed him.

“Wait.  
I’ll talk with them too.”

“Why?”

“Not just because it showed up as a priority condition.  
Because that place looks like it could be used.”

Haru stopped.

“…I don’t hate that answer.”

The two headed for the venue management tent.

Inside, a middle-aged man wearing an armband was dealing with vendors while holding a wireless radio.  
He looked busy.

“Excuse me.”

When Yuto spoke, the man turned around.

“Yes. Lost item?”

“No.  
We’d like to talk briefly about a place to rest.”

“A place to rest?”

Haru spoke from the side.

“Could we use the loading space only during times when there aren’t many deliveries?  
Rope it off and make it a short-time rest area.”

The man’s expression immediately stiffened.

“That’s dangerous.  
Vehicles enter there.”

“Not all the time, right?”

Yuto continued.

“Even just thirty minutes or an hour, avoiding delivery times.  
I think it would be better to create a safe place to rest than to have people collapse or feel unwell.”

“But we don’t have enough people.”

“We’ll do the guidance.”

Haru said it.

Yuto looked sideways at him.

Haru, who had just been asking “who’s going to do it,” had said it himself.

“…You—”

“Just keep going.”

Haru said quietly.

The manager looked back and forth between them.

A voice came from the radio.  
Somewhere in the venue, a child was crying.  
Outside, people were starting to gather in the shade of the tents to avoid the strong sunlight.

“Ten minutes.”

The manager said.

“Huh?”

“You can try it for ten minutes until the next delivery.  
Don’t let people go toward the vehicle side.  
If anything happens, close it immediately.  
If that’s acceptable, try it.”

“…We’ll do it!”

Yuto answered at once.

Those ten minutes passed in an instant.

Haru ran to find rope.  
Yuto borrowed thick paper and a pen for signage.  
They moved unused pallets to the side and secured a path.

Yuto quickly wrote.

“Rest available  
10 minutes left  
Free  
Please don’t push yourself.”

“Not bad wording.”

Haru said while stretching the rope.

“I thought the time limit should be written.”

“Correct.  
If you raise expectations too much, it causes trouble when closing.”

The two moved with a speed that did not feel like a first meeting.

The first person to enter was a mother with a small child.

“Can we sit here?”

“Yes.  
But only for ten minutes until the next delivery.”

“Even ten minutes helps…!”

The mother sat her child on the edge of a pallet and sat down herself along the wall.

Next came an elderly woman holding shopping bags.  
A young couple wiping sweat from their faces.  
A vendor working at the venue.

It was supposed to be only ten minutes.

But in that short time, eight people rested.

Just past nine minutes, Yuto’s phone vibrated.

“Temporary stay point confirmed.  
Part of the priority condition has been achieved.”

“It came…!”

But there was no time to celebrate.

The manager with the radio hurried toward them.

“Close it. A delivery vehicle is early!”

“Understood!”

Yuto and Haru immediately called out to the users.

“Sorry, a vehicle is coming in, so please move this way for now!”

The resting people looked surprised, but they stood up obediently.

But then—

“Mom, my shoe…!”

The child’s sandal had slipped under a pallet.

The reversing sound of the delivery vehicle came closer.

Beep. Beep. Beep.

Yuto held his breath.

“I’ll get it!”

Haru was about to go inside the rope when—

“Wait!”

Yuto grabbed Haru’s arm.

The gap under the pallet.  
The shadow of a cart.  
And the sound of the vehicle reversing.

“…First, we stop it. Safety comes first.”

“What are you—”

Yuto stopped the foot he had almost stepped forward with and gripped his phone again.

The phone vibrated strongly.

“Emergency event detected.  
Confirm response action?”

A choice he had never seen before appeared on the screen.

“Safety-priority support:  
Select immediate action.”

Yuto’s finger stopped above the screen.

The sound of the delivery vehicle drew closer.

## Afterword

Episode 44 depicted the “town outskirts on the weekend” as a stage different from anything before.  
This is not a well-arranged support area like the station front or shopping street, but a place with realistic barriers: logistics, lack of staff, and safety management.

This time, I was conscious of the following structure.

- Setup: Yuto heads to the weekend outskirts and learns how few resting places there are
- Development: He meets Haru, a player who had already been observing the area
- Turn: They try a small contest by turning a loading space into a ten-minute-only resting place
- Ending: The improvement nearly succeeds, but an emergency occurs involving a delivery vehicle and a child’s sandal

This time, Yuto does not merely make a proposal. He runs in the field, negotiates, and cooperates on the spot with Haru, whom he has only just met.  
And at the end, the never-before-seen “safety-priority support” option appears.

In the next episode, Yuto will choose which action to take.  
That decision will affect not only Cooling Credit, but also his relationship with Haru and his evaluation in the town outskirts area.
