# Episode 65: Fifteen Minutes to Keep It

[← Top / README](../../README.md)

Episode 65 follows the fifteen-minute alternative-operation test at Location D of North Seventh Wharf. Can people still rest safely if the mist is shut down and only shade, airflow, water, and guidance remain? Under the eyes of MIST CROW, the Metropolitan Cooling Evaluation Bureau, and FROST-9, Yuto must prove his idea of “reducing without erasing” through results.

---

## Main Text

“Location D: Alternative Operation Verification”
“Time Remaining: 00:15:00”

At the outer edge of North Seventh Wharf, the small emergency rest space had only a roof, four benches, one water station, and two mist nozzles. The white mist was still drifting in the rising heat.

Yuto’s phone displayed the criteria: continued use, safe access to water, airflow efficiency, reduction of cooling load, and emergency exit rate. Failure meant automatic approval of complete shutdown.

MIST CROW stood apart with a tablet. “Once the test begins, I will not intervene. Guidance, movement, and equipment operation are your responsibility. If danger appears, the test ends immediately.”

A surveillance drone hovered overhead. The third-party real-time evaluation began.

Yuto explained the plan to the users. For fifteen minutes the mist would stop, while water and fans remained. The seating would be rearranged for better airflow, and cooling towels would be available. Anyone feeling unwell could leave immediately; nobody was being asked to endure the test.

Before the mist stopped, Yuto noticed that the benches blocked the sea breeze. With help from two users, he angled all four benches slightly toward the airflow. It was only a few degrees, but wind began passing between the seats.

“More wind than before,” a worker said.

Yuto’s phone responded: “Airflow route: improvement candidate confirmed.”

Then the mist stopped.

“Time Remaining: 00:12:48”

Heat returned at once. A child whispered, “Hot.”

People moved toward the single water station, creating a new problem: crowding would block the airflow, and anyone waiting outside the roof would be pushed into direct sun.

Yuto split the flow. People with bottles refilled first and returned to their seats; cup users stayed near the dispenser. Just then Haru arrived carrying a cooler bag from the port first-aid station with twenty cooling towels, small cold packs, and thin neck cloths.

Users now had choices: sit in the airflow, refill water, or use a cooling towel. One worker remarked that the towel was better than mist after work because his clothes stayed dry.

Yuto wrote it down immediately. Cooling did not have to mean the same method for everyone.

“Time Remaining: 00:09:31”

MIST CROW watched the data. “User retention is holding. But density at the water station is high.”

Yuto and Haru moved a low unused loading platform under the roof and turned it into a small table for towels, cold packs, and paper cups. Three separate signs divided the flow. The crowd around the water station dispersed.

MIST CROW murmured, “Interesting.”

“What is?” Yuto asked.

“You reduced cooling output, yet user retention increased.”

Yuto checked his phone.

“Continued Stay Rate: 112%”

MIST CROW explained that people would normally leave when the mist stopped, but Yuto had reduced the reasons to leave: shade, wind, water, and towels were individually small measures, yet together they made continued use possible.

Then, with 6 minutes 48 seconds remaining, the mother with a child spoke up. The child’s face was red and breathing faster.

Yuto did not hesitate. If the child needed mist, he would bring it back—even if that weakened the test.

But Nagi called remotely with another option: the two mist units could be controlled independently. The entrance-side unit could run at 30% while the second remained off.

Yuto proposed localized cooling rather than restoring the whole system. MIST CROW confirmed it was within the test conditions.

The entrance mist restarted weakly. The mother and child moved closer, Haru supplied a cold pack, and after a short while the child’s breathing eased.

“Emergency Exit Rate: 0%”
“Localized Cooling Response: Effective”
“Surrounding Cooling Load: -61%”

The reduction was not the full 67%, but the site was still using far less cooling than before while responding to someone who needed extra help.

With 3 minutes 12 seconds remaining, the evaluation bureau issued an interim result:

“Continued Use: Good”
“Water Route: Improvement Confirmed”
“Localized Cooling: Appropriate”
“Load Reduction: Confirmed”
“Overall: Continue Observation”

Not success yet. Not failure either.

The users were still there: elderly women with cooling towels, a worker drinking water in the breeze, another person choosing the dry area away from mist, and the child resting on the mother’s lap.

“We’re keeping it going,” Haru said. “But watch it to the end.”

Then, with only a minute left, MIST CROW’s tablet flashed red.

“Location D: External Demand Surge”

Nagi’s voice came over the channel. “Yuto, there’s a cooling problem at the Number 2 Rest Building. People from Location A are starting to move!”

That was the building HEATWAVE-3 had planned to use as the alternative destination.

The small Location D shelter was already nearly full.

“Verification Remaining: 00:00:32”

From the port side, helmeted workers appeared—one, two, then more than ten.

Yuto’s phone vibrated.

“Location D: Rapid User Increase Predicted”
“Current Alternative Operation May Exceed Capacity”

The fifteen-minute test was almost over.

But the real test was only beginning.

## Afterword

Episode 65 tested whether Location D could remain useful after shutting down most of its mist cooling.

Key points:

- Yuto angled the benches toward the sea breeze and improved airflow.
- Crowding at the single water station was reduced by separating bottle refill, seated waiting, and towel-use flows.
- Haru brought cooling towels and cold packs, adding cooling methods that did not require mist.
- Continued user stay reached 112%, suggesting that shade, airflow, water, and towels could sustain the space even with reduced mechanical cooling.
- When a child showed signs of heat stress, Yuto used only one mist unit at 30% for localized cooling rather than restoring the full system.
- Cooling load still fell by 61%, and the third-party evaluation continued rather than failing the proposal.
- Just before the test ended, a cooling problem at the Number 2 Rest Building began sending more than ten workers toward Location D.

Yuto proved that “reduce” does not have to mean “erase.” But Location D is small, and no amount of routing can create unlimited capacity.

Next episode, Yuto must turn “reduction that preserves” into “expansion that receives” as new users arrive. What happened at the Number 2 Rest Building? Was HEATWAVE-3’s 40% reduction a mistake? And can Yuto’s alternative operation become an answer for the wider 5.000 CC project?
