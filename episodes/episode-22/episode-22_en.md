# Episode 22: The Poster Without a Name

[← Top / README](../../README.md)

Episode 22 is the moment when, immediately after Yuto has shared his boundaries with the player community, he receives a small seed of discomfort from outside those boundaries.

It is not yet a major incident, but the issues of appropriation, attribution, and credit begin to show their faces in a very minor form.

---

Thursday.

Soma Yuto felt a vague discomfort in his fingertips as he replaced the in-store POP signs at the convenience store.

A poster for a new ice cream.

A discount-sale notice.

A small heatstroke-prevention notice had been added as well.

“Remember to stay hydrated, and so on…”

The wording was ordinary, the kind that could be found anywhere.

But over the past few weeks, ever since he had become aware that words could be placed on blueprints and project documents, he had grown sensitive even to short phrases like these.

After finishing the noon shift, he left the store.

Outside, the heat was merciless.

Even so, posters and signs for COOL STREET and COOL WATER had increased throughout town, and compared with before, the sense that someone was trying to cool the city had become stronger.

As usual, Yuto walked through the shopping street.

The shade of street trees.

Small plates on benches.

A large COOL STREET poster.

Suddenly, his eyes stopped on the lower section of that poster.

“In this shopping street:  
- Take a break on shaded benches  
- Reduce strain on your body by walking along cool routes  
- Let us all increase the number of places where people can feel, ‘Take a breath here.’”

“…Huh?”

Yuto stopped at the third line.

The phrase “Take a breath here.”

It was the wording he had written yesterday on the round sticker he placed at the station stair landing.

Of course, someone else in the world could have used a similar expression.

But the timing was too close.

Under the shade of the street trees, his phone vibrated lightly.

“COOL STREET project document updated:  
A new slogan has been added.  
The phrase ‘Take a breath here’ was extracted from multiple daily-life cooling logs.”

“Multiple… right.”

Yuto let out a small breath as he looked at the screen.

Multiple logs.

Not just his own.

Someone else might have written a similar phrase too.

“But the log from the round sticker on the station stairs is definitely included, isn't it?”

When he checked the Cooling Commons page, it did indeed say the following.

“Referenced daily-life cooling logs, excerpt:  
- Station stair landing log: informal sign reading ‘Take a breath here’  
- Shopping street bench log: ‘Take a short break in a slightly cooler place’  
- Riverside shade log: ‘Do not push yourself; rest in the shade’

→ Slogan proposal adopted: ‘Take a breath here.’”

“So it does say the phrase was extracted from logs.”

Inside the system, no lie had been told.

There were multiple sources.

Yuto's own name was also linked to it as a small ID.

But on the poster—

“Slogan: Take a breath here”

“Cooperation: COOL STREET Executive Committee”

That was all.

“Names disappear in this kind of form, huh…”

Yuto sat down in the shade of a street tree.

The first time he had placed a note in the shade of a tree.

The first time he had marked the waiting line at a bus stop.

The first time he had put a round sticker on the station stairs.

Every one of those actions had been a step taken as an ordinary resident.

Now, a phrase that had emerged from those steps was about to be posted all over town as a slogan.

That itself was not a bad thing.

If anything, he wanted it to spread.

“But the part about whose words they were disappears so easily.”

The words he had heard in the chat the previous night returned to his mind.

“It could become a place where the people who share the most lose the most.”

“It's not like I'm losing anything yet.  
But it doesn't exactly feel like I gained anything either.”

There would surely be people who saw the poster and felt helped by the phrase “Take a breath here.”

That genuinely made him happy.

But almost nobody would know whose daily life had produced the words behind it.

His phone vibrated.

“Message from nearby player  
Sender: WindArc”

“WindArc:  
Did you see the ‘Take a breath here’ poster in the shopping street?  
Looks like that slogan proposal got adopted.”

“I saw it.”

Yuto sent a short reply.

“Yuto:  
I saw it.  
I'm happy, but it also feels a little strange.”

A reply came immediately.

“WindArc:  
I get it.  
It's nice when your words spread,  
but it feels a little uncomfortable when your name isn't written anywhere.”

That single sentence made him feel slightly relieved.

“So I'm not the only one who feels this strange discomfort.”

WindArc's next message was a little longer.

“WindArc:  
For the record, the internal Cooling Commons log records that the slogan was extracted from your station stair log.  
But on posters and public materials, it only says ‘created by the COOL STREET Executive Committee.’

Apparently, for now, that's intentional.  
If one person's name is displayed,  
people might misunderstand it as meaning ‘this phrase belongs only to that person.’”

“…I understand the logic.”

Cooling Commons.

A shared resource.

Ideas anyone can use.

Given that nature, it made sense to avoid presenting a phrase in a way that looked like one person had exclusive ownership of it.

He understood that reasoning.

“But I still feel like there should be some way to see whose daily life it came from.”

Yuto thought for a while.

Cooling Commons as a place that remains open and available to anyone.

And fair return to the people who provided the ideas and words.

Balancing those two things was not simple.

At that moment, another notification appeared on the screen.

“Notice from the institutional side:  
Trial release of the ‘Word Credit’ viewing page for Cooling Commons contributors.”

“Word Credit…?”

Yuto opened the page.

It showed several phrases and the IDs associated with them.

“Slogan: ‘Take a breath here’  
Source logs:  
- Station stair landing log (ID: Yuto-01)  
- Shopping street bench log (ID: Anon-13)  
- Riverside tree-shade log (ID: WindArc-02)

Internal credit:  
- Soma Yuto: Word Contribution Points +3  
- Anonymous player: +2  
- WindArc: +2”

“…So internally, names are attached properly.”

The names that disappeared on the visible poster remained on the hidden credit page.

“But almost only players will see this page.”

Ordinary people would not access it.

Some corporate or municipal staff might see it, but even then, only in a limited way.

“They're trying to separate ‘my words spread through the world’ from ‘my name spreads through the world.’”

That was one possible policy.

Words are shared openly.

Credit remains as an internal evaluation point.

“The issue is how far that internal credit actually returns to the lives of residents.”

Points.

Evaluation.

Rankings.

Unless those things connect to something concrete like electricity-bill assistance, priority access to cooling equipment, or work opportunities, internal credit would end as mere status.

Yuto opened a new page in his notebook.

“Notes on Word Credit

- Cooling Commons spreads slogans and words openly, while recording whose logs they were extracted from as internal credit.

- On the poster, only ‘created by the Executive Committee’ is written, and individual names do not appear.

- This appears to be a policy to avoid making the phrase look like the exclusive property of one person.

- However, from the perspective of a resident, there is still a distance between ‘words born from my life spreading into the world’ and ‘how that returns to my own life.’”

“How it returns, huh.”

Contribution points +3.

The number was small.

But what mattered was that he still could not see what would happen when such credit accumulated.

His phone vibrated again.

“Message from nearby player  
Sender: Hidden-rank player”

“Hidden-rank player:  
You saw the Word Credit page.  
At this stage, it remains only an internal evaluation,  
but in the future, proposals are being considered to link it to things such as:  
- priority slots for participation in cooling projects  
- integration with livelihood support.”

“Livelihood support…”

That phrase felt quite significant to Yuto.

“So word credit might someday touch rent or electricity bills, even if only a little.”

Of course, not right away.

As long as it was still under consideration, it remained something for the future.

But at least he understood that there was a direction toward not letting it end as mere points.

“Hidden-rank player:  
Until then, two things should help support that uncomfortable feeling:  
- being able to check where your words spread through the internal credit page  
- being able to share that fact within the player community.”

“The player community…”

The night talk from yesterday.

The discussion of boundaries.

The discussion of refuges.

Now the topic of Word Credit was being added to that.

“But I still want to remember the discomfort I felt when I saw a poster without a name.”

Yuto added another line to his notebook.

“Today's discomfort:  
- I am happy that the slogan ‘Take a breath here’ appeared on a poster.  
But the fact that the names of the residents, including myself, do not appear at all leaves a slight roughness.”

When he returned to his apartment in the evening, the air in the room was comfortably cool.

The air conditioner.

The heat-insulation sheet.

The furniture arrangement.

All of them belonged to a place he had cooled for himself.

The cooling log for this room would not be placed on Cooling Commons.

It would remain here, as part of the foundation of his life.

Yuto wrote the day's summary in his notebook.

“Today's events:  
- A COOL STREET poster containing the slogan ‘Take a breath here’ appeared in the shopping street  
→ Inside Cooling Commons, it is recorded that the phrase was extracted from my words and those of other players  
→ On the poster, only ‘created by the Executive Committee’ is written  
- The Word Credit viewing page was released on a trial basis  
→ I confirmed that I had been assigned a small number of points  
- A hidden-rank player explained that there are future proposals to link this with livelihood support”

“Today's observations:  
- A two-layer structure is forming, where words spread openly while names remain internally  
- Between those layers, there is still a gap around how this returns to my own life  
- Linking credit with livelihood support may become a way to fill that gap”

Then he wrote his usual final line.

“Today's line:  
- The discomfort I felt on seeing a poster without a name is an important discomfort for thinking about how to grow Cooling Commons.”

When he checked his phone, the numbers for the day were modest.

“Earned today: 0.015 CC  
Cumulative: 0.557 CC”

“Not bad, I guess.”

Yuto closed his notebook and exhaled quietly.

The world was still hot.

But within that heat, the slogan “Take a breath here” had begun to spread through town, and behind it, a small credit page had begun to glow.

Seeing both the light and the shadow of that would surely become important in the story ahead — that much, at least, he could clearly feel.

---

Thank you for reading Episode 22.

This episode depicted a two-layer structure: a phrase close to Yuto's own words spreads through the town as a slogan, while his name disappears from the visible poster and remains only in internal logs.

The heart of this episode is the happiness that the slogan “Take a breath here” was adopted, alongside the uncomfortable feeling Yuto experiences when he sees a poster without a name.

At the same time, through elements such as the Word Credit viewing page and future proposals for linkage with livelihood support, the episode shows a possible direction in which Cooling Commons may become not merely a place for sharing ideas, but a system that can eventually connect back to everyday life.

This state of carrying both discomfort and hope will lead into future episodes dealing with appropriation, return of value, inequality, and the design of open licenses.

I hope you will continue following Yuto as he remembers the roughness he felt when seeing a poster without a name, and as he chooses how to act from there.
