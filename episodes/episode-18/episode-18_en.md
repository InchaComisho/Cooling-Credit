# Episode 18: A Day to Cool for Myself

[← Top / README](../../README.md)

Episode 18 takes place just before Yuto reaches 0.5 CC and has him reconsider the boundary between cooling for himself and cooling for someone else.

As cooperation with institutions and companies continues to advance, a quiet question rises: what about his own life?

---

Monday.

Soma Yuto stared at his phone screen without moving for a while.

“Cumulative Cooling Credit: 0.478 CC”

“…Only 0.022 left.”

0.5 CC.

On a global scale, it was not an impressive number.

But for Yuto, it looked like a meaningful milestone.

His convenience-store shift started in the evening.

The daytime was free.

Still, he could feel a faint fatigue accumulated deep inside his body.

For the past several days, he had been thinking about street trees.

The riverside.

The suburban shopping center.

Institutional signs.

Requests for comments from companies.

Everything had been about the outside.

“…Maybe today can be a day to cool things for myself.”

He murmured it and surprised himself slightly.

Until now, it had been easier to act when he could say it was for someone else or for the town.

Even cooling his own room had carried something like guilt.

But now that institutional and corporate discussions were beginning to move forward, continuing to ignore himself seemed unnatural in the opposite direction.

Yuto picked up the air-conditioner remote.

An old, inexpensive unit.

He had barely touched the cooling button.

“Using this is basically negative from the CC perspective.”

It consumed electricity and expelled heat.

From the perspective of the cooling system as a whole, depending on how it was used, air conditioning could even worsen the surrounding environment.

Even so.

“They've told me again and again that if I collapse, none of this matters.”

The news.

Municipal announcements.

Chats among players.

The warning not to endure too much was repeated just as often as the warning not to overcool.

Yuto took a deep breath and pressed the power button.

The air conditioner started with a clumsy mechanical sound.

A few seconds later, cool air slowly began pushing through the room.

At that moment, his phone vibrated briefly.

“Indoor cooling mode detected.  
The current action is classified as cooling for personal safety.  
No direct addition to or deduction from Cooling Credit will be applied.”

“…There's even a judgment for that?”

Yuto laughed despite himself.

“It's like the system is telling me to stay alive.”

Personal safety.

The action was not converted into a cooling score, but the system still recognized it as something necessary.

The cool air gradually spread through the room.

Yuto closed the window and opened indoor mode in **ThermoBalance**.

“Indoor temperature: 31.2°C → predicted 28.5°C  
Heat-stress index: High → Moderate”

“It was still high, even though it felt better than outside…”

What he had considered tolerable was apparently already leaning toward dangerous according to the system.

He weakened the air-conditioner airflow slightly and turned on the electric fan.

Circulation of cool air.

The fan moved the colder air that tended to settle near the floor through the rest of the room.

“This is another form of letting wind through.”

Yet his own room had almost never appeared in his usual logs.

The town.

Stores.

The riverside.

Parking lots.

All of them were about the outside.

“I defined my town pretty thoroughly in Part 1, but I hardly talked about my own room.”

The thought made him smile a little.

“Then today will be the day I add my room to the cooling logs.”

Yuto opened his notebook and wrote at the top of a new page:

“Cooling Log for My Room — First Entry”

Then he looked around.

A single six-tatami room.

One window.

Old blackout curtains.

An air conditioner.

An electric fan.

A small refrigerator.

“Until now, I treated this as a room where I endure the heat to save money.”

But after hearing about Cooling Commons and COOL STREET, he understood that not collapsing himself was also an essential condition.

“If I'm not healthy, I can't cool the town or write reports.”

Yuto stood by the window.

When he opened the curtain slightly, strong sunlight entered.

Heat through the glass.

Radiation from the metal window frame.

**ThermoBalance** detected it.

“Surface temperature near window: +3.7°C above room average  
Direct sunlight intrusion: Moderate to High”

“The curtain is working, but it isn't enough.”

Yuto pulled the white leisure sheet from the corner of the room.

“I first used this for a bench in the alley. So this is where I left it afterward.”

He laid it on the floor near the window to reduce reflected heat from below.

He also attached a simple aluminum heat-shield sheet between the curtain and the window.

The DIY work was rough, but better than doing nothing.

A few minutes later, he scanned again.

“Surface temperature near window: +2.1°C above room average  
Direct sunlight intrusion: Moderate — slight improvement”

“A little better.”

Yuto also shifted the position of his desk.

Somewhere less exposed to direct sunlight from the window.

Somewhere the air-conditioner airflow would not hit him directly, but would circulate around the room first.

“This is completely a movement-route design for myself.”

The work desk.

The chair.

The electric fan.

The bed.

He arranged them into a layout that was slightly easier to cool and move through.

His phone vibrated.

“Would you like to begin a Self-Environment Optimization Log?  
This log will not be made public to other players, but parts of it may be referenced as a living foundation within Cooling Commons.”

“My room is part of Cooling Commons too?”

Laughing, he tapped yes.

The system asked for a title, so after thinking for a moment, he entered:

“Cooling My Room as a Living Foundation — Soma Yuto”

At that moment, something clicked into place deep inside his chest.

“My living foundation…”

Until now, all the discussion had been about towns, institutions, and companies. He had not properly dealt with his own body and room.

But no matter how many cooler routes existed in town, it meant little if he returned home exhausted and unable to function.

“A day spent cooling for myself may not increase my Cooling Credit,  
but in the long run, it's a necessary investment.”

Then another notification arrived.

“Message from nearby player  
Sender: WindArc

‘Haven't you been moving around too much these past few days?  
Are you properly cooling your own room?’”

“…Are you monitoring me?”

Yuto smiled wryly and wrote a reply.

“‘I just started logging the cooling of my room for the first time.  
The system called it self-environment optimization.’”

A reply came tens of seconds later.

“WindArc:  
‘That's extremely important.  
I often tell the kitchen staff here to cool their own rooms too.  
If they collapse, nothing else matters.’”

“Of course it comes back to that.”

Earning through cooling.

Participating in institutions.

Commenting on corporate projects.

Before all of those things came one condition: staying alive.

“Then today, I'll make it completely a day to cool for myself.”

Yuto wrote a new section in his notebook.

“Cooling for myself:  
- Secure at least several hours each day when the room stays below 30°C  
- Reduce direct sunlight  
- Create a path for airflow  
- Create a place inside my room that says, ‘It is okay to be here’”

“A place where it's okay to be isn't only needed in the town. It's needed inside my room too.”

After moving the desk, the feeling of being cornered by sunlight while sitting had weakened.

He also shifted the bed slightly so the air-conditioner airflow would not hit it directly.

Before leaving for his convenience-store shift in the evening, he scanned the room once more.

“Indoor temperature: 28.3°C  
Heat-stress index: Moderate → Near Low  
Self-Environment Optimization Level: Lv. 1”

“There are levels for this too…?”

He gave a half laugh, but also felt relieved.

When he returned at night after his shift, the room air did not feel as heavy as it had at noon.

The air-conditioner was not set particularly low.

But the window insulation and furniture layout seemed to have changed how heat accumulated.

His phone vibrated.

“Earned today: 0.019 CC  
Cumulative total: 0.497 CC”

“…Just missed it.”

One more step to 0.5.

But today's cooling had not been for the number.

“0.5 can wait until tomorrow.”

Yuto opened his notebook and summarized the day.

“Today's events:  
- Seriously designed the cooling of my own room for the first time  
  → Air conditioner on / window heat shielding / furniture layout revised  
  → Began private Self-Environment Optimization Log  
- Exchanged a few messages with WindArc about cooling to avoid collapse”

“Today's observations:  
- My body and room can also be treated as a living foundation within Cooling Commons  
- Before towns, institutions, and companies, I need to decide how to cool myself in order to live  
- Even on a day when CC does not increase, a day spent cooling for myself is not wasted”

Finally, one line.

“Today's line:  
- Before cooling for others, I am allowed to stay somewhere properly cool myself.”

Yuto put down his pen and weakened the air-conditioner airflow slightly.

The world was still hot.

But in the middle of it, the air in his own room had become a little kinder.

---

Thank you for reading Episode 18.

This episode deliberately turned the cooling perspective back toward Yuto's own room and body at a point when cooperation with institutions and companies had begun advancing.

The viewpoint that had expanded outward toward towns, public spaces, and corporate facilities temporarily returned inward, confirming that the question of how to cool one's own living foundation also belongs within the thinking behind Cooling Commons.

By giving Yuto the sense that he is allowed to be somewhere cool before cooling for others, this episode creates a foundation that can keep him from slipping too far into self-sacrifice when harder institutional and corporate projects, or ethically destabilizing situations, appear later.

As the external scale continues to grow, I hope you will keep following how he continues to protect his own life as well.
