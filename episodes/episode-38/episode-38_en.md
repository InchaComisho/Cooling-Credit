# Episode 38: Small Frictions

[← Top / README](../../README.md)

Episode 38, the second episode of Part 4, depicts the “small frictions” that appear after the first day of operation.  
Although the system has begun to move, not everything goes smoothly in the field. Rather, this is the episode where small misunderstandings begin to appear.

---

## Main Text

Tuesday.  
Soma Yuto sat on a bench in the station-front plaza, looking over the usage reports that had continued since the previous day.

If he looked only at the numbers, things were going well.  
Application-free place-based support was being used often, and voice guidance was receiving good feedback.  
But the field notes contained several entries that caught his attention.

“There are voices saying the guidance is too short, which actually creates anxiety.”  
“There was a case where a family member who had not received an explanation about proxy use became confused.”  
“Some users hesitated, wondering if it was really all right to use it for free.”  
“There were scenes where people used the space as a place to cool down, but did not recognize it as part of the support system.”

“…So it isn’t only going well.”

Yuto closed the notes.

The easier support is to understand, the better.  
But shortened wording can sometimes look like insufficient explanation.

WindArc arrived.

“Good morning.  
Compared with the first day, a few points of concern have started to appear.”

“I’m seeing them.  
Keeping the guidance short was good, but it seems some people feel anxious when it’s too short.”

“Yes.  
Also, because we did not state clearly enough that it is free,  
there were cases where people held back.”

Yuto leaned back against the bench.

“Making something gentle is hard…”

“It really is.  
Clarity and reassurance are not the same thing.”

In the station-front plaza, an elderly man was standing in front of the information board.  
After reading it, he frowned slightly.

Yuto quietly watched him.

The man started toward the bench, then stopped again.  
After a while, he spoke to a nearby guidance staff member.

“Is it really all right to sit here?”

The staff member smiled and nodded.

“Yes.  
This is a place for people to rest.  
You can use it for free, and no application is required.”

“…I see.  
I suppose I was holding back a little.”

After saying that, the man finally sat down on the bench.

Watching that exchange, Yuto thought.

Whether a system reaches someone  
is not decided only by whether correct information exists.  
It is also decided by whether the body can understand that it does not need to hold back.

“When the explanation is short, some people feel anxious.  
But if it is long, people don’t read it.  
The question is how to find the space between those two.”

“Yes.  
At the design meeting, there is a proposal to make the guidance two-layered.”

“Two-layered?”

“Yes.  
The top layer is short, with supplemental information underneath.  
First show ‘You can rest here,’ ‘Free,’ and ‘No application required,’  
then add explanations about proxy use and voice guidance below.”

“I see.  
So it works both for people who read and people who don’t.”

“Exactly.  
Also, for first-time users, they are considering having staff add one line such as, ‘Please feel free to sit down.’”

“So it needs not only words, but atmosphere.”

“Exactly.”

Before noon, similar voices were also appearing in the shopping street.  
Many people came to cool down.  
But quite a few of them had little sense that they were receiving support.

“It helps as a place to rest for a bit.”  
“But I didn’t think it was part of a system.”  
“Isn’t this kind of thing only for people who applied?”

As Yuto collected those voices, he thought for a while.

If support is too visible, people brace themselves. If it is too invisible, it is not recognized as a system.  
That boundary was now shaking.

In the afternoon, WindArc sent an additional update.

“WindArc:  
The guidance text will be repaired right away.  
We will keep the short wording, while clearly stating the three points: ‘free,’ ‘no application required,’ and ‘available to anyone.’”

“That sounds right.”

“WindArc:  
Also, for staff who speak to people first,  
we will create shared phrases for ‘one line that prevents people from holding back.’”

“For example?”

“WindArc:  
‘This is a place where you can sit if you are tired.’  
‘Please feel free.’  
‘All you need to do is use it.’  
That kind of thing.”

Yuto nodded as he looked at the message.

“Sometimes a line like that is what works the most.”

“Yes.  
A system begins with written wording, but reassurance reaches people through another person’s words.”

In the evening, Yuto sat once more on the bench in the station-front plaza.  
There were slightly fewer people than at midday.  
Because of that, he could see the arrangement of the benches and the flow of wind more clearly.

“A gentle design isn’t about being complete.  
It means continuing to fix small frictions like this…”

As he murmured that, the elderly man from earlier bowed slightly on his way home.

“Thank you.  
Once I properly understood that it was all right to sit down, I felt much easier.”

Yuto bowed his head lightly.

“Thank you, too.  
I’m glad you used it.”

The man smiled a little and walked slowly toward the station.

Watching him go, Yuto added to his notebook.

“Operation Day 2 Log  
・There are cases where short guidance creates anxiety  
・It is necessary to clearly state free, no application required, and available to anyone  
・The first spoken line has the effect of loosening hesitation  
・Support will not take root unless both clarity and reassurance are arranged.”

“Today’s realization  
・A system becomes truly usable by finding small frictions after it has been designed  
・What loosens hesitation is not a careful explanation first, but an atmosphere that says it is all right to sit down  
・Part 4 will become a story of adjusting the details of operation.”

Finally, one line.

“Today’s line  
・Gentleness is decided less by the first design than by the details repaired afterward.”

His phone showed that his Cooling Credit had increased slightly.

“Earned today: 0.008 CC  
Total: 0.751 CC”

“Little by little.”

Yuto put his phone in his pocket and looked up at the mist in front of the station.  
The system had begun to move.  
And now, adjustment in the true sense had begun.

## Afterword

Episode 38 depicted the “small frictions” that appeared in the early movement of Part 4.  
Once support begins to be used, it becomes necessary to balance understandability with reassurance.

This episode focused especially on the following points.  
- Anxiety created by shortening the guidance
- Hesitation caused by not clearly stating that it is free and requires no application
- The way one line from a staff member can change the impression of the system

In other words, even if the system itself is correct, its usability changes depending on how it is conveyed in the field.  
Part 4 will continue depicting the process through which support moves closer to a form that actually reaches people by adjusting these details.

Yuto’s role has also shifted slightly from proposing systems to collecting and returning the frictions of operation.  
From the next episode onward, we will follow how far this “adjustment” becomes familiar within the town.
