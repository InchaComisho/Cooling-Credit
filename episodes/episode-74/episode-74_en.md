# Episode 74 — The Map That Makes 2.0%

## Foreword

Episode 74 marks the start of Phase 1 of the official wide-area operation at North Pier Seven.
To protect 38 workers in the northern cargo-handling zone and 12 overheated containers, Yuto must secure at least 2.0% of cooling headroom from Points A through D. The question is no longer simply what to cut, but how to divide capacity while keeping every site usable.

---

## Main Story

“Official Wide-Area Operation — Phase 1”

“Required cooling headroom: 2.0% or more”
“Time limit: 00:24:59”

“Proposer: Yuto Soma”

North Pier Seven — Northern Cargo-Handling Zone.

Four output sliders were displayed on Yuto’s phone.

“A: Third Worker Rest Building”
“B: Water Station for Waiting Vehicles”
“C: Harbor Perimeter Shaded Bench Area”
“D: Neighborhood Emergency Rest Space”

Beside them, a new item flashed red.

“Northern Cargo-Handling Zone
Workers under high-heat alert: 38
Overheated containers: 12
Additional required headroom: 2.0%”

“Two percent...”

On paper, it looked small.

But at North Pier Seven, even 0.1% at any location already had people depending on it.

At Point A were workers who had difficulty moving.
At Point B were drivers who could not leave the waiting line.
At Point C was the auxiliary terminal supporting people heading toward the northern passage.
At Point D were elderly residents and parents with children from the nearby neighborhood.

“Where do you cut?” FROST-9 asked.

White jacket.
Silver control unit.

Her tone could have been testing him—or simply asking for an answer.

“I don’t cut any of them,” Yuto said.

“...That answer again?” HEATWAVE-3 said from a short distance away.

“If you want to create two percent, something has to come down.”

“I’ll lower them,” Yuto corrected.

“But I won’t cut them off.
I’ll change how they’re used and create headroom that way.”

FROST-9 said nothing.

Yuto enlarged the map.

A. B. C. D.

Their current outputs appeared.

“A: 55%”
“B: Water and mist operation 48%”
“C: Shaded bench area 10%”
“D: Local cooling 16%”

“First, C.”

Yuto pointed at it.

“The shade has spread farther now.
There aren’t many users.
But I don’t want it at zero. Keep the L-4 auxiliary branch on standby and lower normal output from 10% to 4%.”

“That frees 0.6%,” Nagi said over comms.

“L-4 remains on standby.
It can activate only if emergency demand appears in the northern passage or at Point D.”

“Next?” FROST-9 asked.

“B.”

Yuto brought up the water station for waiting vehicles.

“Reduce mist operation from 48% to 25%.
But keep the water supply intact.
Drivers who can’t run cabin air conditioning can still cool down with water.”

“That should free roughly 0.7%,” RIVER-EDGE said over comms.

“Point B’s vehicle queue has shortened a little for now.
If water supply is prioritized, we can keep operating even with less mist.
But once we pass noon, it’ll get difficult.”

“Only for now,” Yuto said.

“Twenty minutes.”

“That makes 1.3% total,” HEATWAVE-3 counted flatly.

“Still short.”

“A and D, a little at a time.”

Point A.
The Third Worker Rest Building.

The place he had protected yesterday.

“A goes from 55% to 48%.
We avoid raising the room temperature too quickly because of the workers who can’t move easily.
Short-stay users split toward the Second Rest Building and the relay booth.”

“0.4%,” Nagi said.

“But if that route breaks down, we restore it immediately.”

“That brings us to 1.7.”

“D goes from 16% to 13%.
Reduce local mist slightly and prioritize shade, airflow, water, and cooling towels.”

“0.3.”

FROST-9 looked at the totals.

“C: +0.6%”
“B: +0.7%”
“A: +0.4%”
“D: +0.3%”

“Total secured headroom: 2.0%”

A blue line extended across the map toward the northern cargo-handling zone.

“Exactly two,” HEATWAVE-3 said.

“No margin.”

“That’s why I’m adding restore conditions to every point.”

Yuto opened the proposal screen.

“Phase 1 — Yuto Soma Proposal”

“Point C: 10% → 4%
L-4 auxiliary branch remains on standby
Return 0.6% immediately if emergency demand occurs”

“Point B: 48% → 25%
Water supply prioritized
Mist reduction limited to 20 minutes”

“Point A: 55% → 48%
Indoor priority for people with mobility difficulty
Short-stay users guided to the Second Rest Building via the relay booth”

“Point D: 16% → 13%
Local mist reduced
Shade, airflow, water, and towels maintained”

“Northern Cargo-Handling Zone:
Distribute the secured 2.0% in stages between overheated containers and workers under high-heat alert”

“Staged distribution?” FROST-9 asked.

“I don’t want to send everything to the containers from the start.
We watch container temperatures and worker heat-stress levels, then move the allocation every five minutes.”

“That’s too slow,” HEATWAVE-3 said.

“The containers won’t wait.”

“Neither will the workers,” Yuto replied.

“So we start half and half.
Add more to containers that enter the danger zone.
Add more to work areas where the number of people under heat-stress alert rises.”

“Half and half?”

“Yes.
I don’t want people and cargo competing from the very beginning.”

FROST-9 read the proposal.

Time remaining:

“00:17:31”

“Approved,” she said.

Yuto looked up.

“But on two conditions.”

“What are they?”

“First: at the initial evaluation five minutes from now, if even one site records an emergency departure, I restore that site immediately.”

“Understood.”

“Second: if temperature-priority cargo in the northern cargo-handling zone enters the danger range, I will forcibly reduce the workers’ allocation.”

Yuto fell silent for a moment.

They were harsh conditions.

But he had already lived through the night of the medical shipment.

He knew there were loads whose temperature simply could not be allowed to fail.

“Understood.”

FROST-9 operated the control unit.

“Phase 1, begin.”

The map of the entire harbor moved.

“C: 10% → 4%”
“B: 48% → 25%”
“A: 55% → 48%”
“D: 16% → 13%”

“Secured headroom: 2.0%”

The display for the northern cargo-handling zone changed.

“Overheated container cooling: +1.0%”
“Worker rest / local airflow: +1.0%”

Cooling fans on the container side began to turn.

Gwoooom.

In the workers’ rest section, overhead airflow strengthened slightly.

“It’s coming through.”

Yuto held his breath.

But communications arrived from every site almost immediately.

“Haru:
Point A has begun guiding short-stay users.
The mobility-limited workers indoors are fine.”

“RIVER-EDGE:
Point B mist is reduced.
The water line is longer, but still moving.”

“Nagi:
Point C normal output is 4%.
L-4 is on standby. Pipe temperature is stable.”

“Soma:
Point D mist is at 13%.
People are still staying under the shade. I added cooling towels.”

Yuto checked every site status.

“A: Yellow”
“B: Yellow”
“C: Blue”
“D: Yellow”
“Northern Cargo: Red → Yellow”

“The northern zone turned yellow,” HEATWAVE-3 said.

“Container temperatures have started dropping too.”

Data appeared on Yuto’s phone.

“Average overheated-container temperature: 41.8°C → 39.9°C”

“Workers under high-heat alert: 38 → 31”

“This might work,” Soma said over comms.

Yuto smiled faintly.

Then RIVER-EDGE’s voice cut sharply in from Point B.

“Yuto, we have a problem.
The waiting-vehicle queue is growing earlier than expected.”

“How many?”

“Twelve more than before.
A lot of drivers are waiting with their air conditioning off.
The water line is over twenty people now.”

Point B shifted from yellow to orange.

“Point B heat-stress alert: rising”
“Water-flow congestion detected”

“It was supposed to be only twenty minutes...” Yuto said.

“We haven’t even reached five.”

FROST-9 looked at the map.

“Restore B.”

“If we do, the 2.0% for the northern cargo zone collapses.”

“Then lower A or D further.”

“A has people who can’t move easily.”

“D has children and elderly users.”

The options narrowed again.

Then L-4 at Point C began flashing.

“L-4 auxiliary branch: sudden user increase detected in the northern passage”

“The northern passage too?”

Nagi’s voice came over comms.

“People are leaving the Point B waiting line on foot to look for water.
They’re starting to flow into a cooling gap.”

New warnings piled onto Yuto’s screen.

“Phase 1 — Initial evaluation in: 00:02:48”

“Point B: water congestion”
“Northern passage: user inflow”
“L-4 auxiliary branch: emergency activation candidate”

“Two minutes, forty-eight seconds...”

The 2.0% they had only just created was already beginning to unravel.

Yuto stared at the map.

Lower A?
Lower D?
Restore B?
Use L-4 for the northern passage?

The real difficulty of North Pier Seven was not creating two percent.

It was keeping that two percent without dropping anyone along the way.

## Afterword

In Episode 74, Yuto made his first proposal to create a total of 2.0% cooling headroom from Points A through D so that cooling could be sent to 38 workers and 12 overheated containers in the northern cargo-handling zone.

Key points in this episode:

- Point C was reduced from 10% to 4%, while L-4 remained on emergency standby, freeing 0.6%.
- Point B reduced mist operation from 48% to 25% while prioritizing water supply, freeing 0.7%.
- Point A was adjusted from 55% to 48%, keeping workers with mobility difficulty indoors while routing short-stay users through the relay booth to the Second Rest Building, freeing 0.4%.
- Point D was reduced from 16% to 13%, preserving shade, airflow, water, and cooling towels while freeing 0.3%.
- The resulting 2.0% was divided initially into 1.0% for container cooling and 1.0% for worker local airflow.
- Average overheated-container temperature fell from 41.8°C to 39.9°C, while the number of workers under high-heat alert dropped from 38 to 31.
- However, Point B quickly saw a surge in waiting vehicles and its water line, while users also began flowing into the northern passage, putting the newly created 2.0% allocation at risk.

Yuto succeeded in creating 2.0% not by shutting down one of Points A through D, but by adjusting each of them a little.
Yet in a wide-area operation, the moment numerical headroom is created, human movement changes too. Reducing the mist at Point B caused drivers in the waiting line to seek water and move toward the northern passage.

Next time, with less than three minutes remaining before the initial evaluation, Yuto must urgently redistribute resources while protecting Point B, the northern passage, and the northern cargo-handling zone at the same time.
Will he activate the L-4 auxiliary branch for the northern passage, or lower output somewhere else again? He now has to preserve the headroom he created while responding to the chain reaction of people moving through the port.
