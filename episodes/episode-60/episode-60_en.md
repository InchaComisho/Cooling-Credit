# Episode 60: Choosing Not to Become the Operator

[← Top / README](../../README.md)

Episode 60 is a major resolution for the shared cooling line arc.  
Although they succeeded in protecting the medical delivery container and the waiting station, the final remaining question was: who will operate this line? For Yuto, it is a major opportunity to rise through Cooling Credit, but also a heavy choice that may cost him his freedom.

---

## Main Text

“Shared Cooling Line  
Provisional Operator Selection”

“Candidates:  
Soma Yuto  
Haru  
Nagi  
Sōma  
Katase  
R-01”

“Selection deadline: 00:09:59”

On the rooftop of the old waiting room.

The night wind cooled Yuto’s sweat-damp face.

The medical delivery container had avoided its allowable upper limit.  
The waiting station temperature had returned to around twenty-two degrees.  
Venue Mist V-3A was stable at 6%.

If judged by the numbers alone, it was a success.

But on Yuto’s phone, the heaviest display yet had appeared.

“Provisional operator”

“…Operator,”

Yuto said quietly.

“That means someone who keeps watching this line every day, right?”

Leaning against the rooftop valve, R-01 answered.

“Not exactly.  
It does not mean someone who operates everything every day.”

“Then what do they do?”

“They decide priorities.”

R-01 looked down over the night warehouse district.

“Do you prioritize medical delivery?  
Do you prioritize the people in the waiting station?  
Do you protect the venue equipment?  
Do you stop old equipment?  
When something becomes insufficient, they make the decision to cut someone off.”

Yuto lost his words.

Tonight, he had faced the same kind of choice again and again.

Whether to stop cooling.  
Whether to move ventilation.  
Whether to protect cargo.  
Whether to protect people.

But each time, he had listened to the field, consulted his companions, and searched for another path.

But if he became the operator.

Every day.  
At any time.  
In places where no one else was watching.

He might have to decide the priorities alone.

Yuto’s phone vibrated.

“Candidate registration confirmed”  
“If selected, provisional operation rewards will be generated”

“Base reward: 0.030 CC per day”  
“Emergency response evaluation: additional calculation”  
“Shared cooling line performance reward: eligible for distribution”

“…0.030 CC a day.”

Yuto’s heart jumped.

Until now, the CC he had earned from field improvements had been 0.004 or 0.007.  
Even limited challenges had been large at 0.050 or 0.100.

0.030 CC every day.

And with performance rewards.

This was a shortcut upward.

Past a cumulative 1.0 CC, and even higher beyond that.

“Do you want it?”

R-01 asked.

Yuto answered honestly.

“…I do.”

“Then choose it.”

“But…”

“Are you afraid?”

“I am.”

Yuto tightened his grip on the phone.

“Just because I can earn from it, I don’t think it is right for me to decide everything alone.”

Nagi came up onto the rooftop.

She turned off her light and looked up at the sky.

“I think that is fine.”

“Nagi-san.”

“What Yuto was able to do tonight was not because you knew everything.  
It was because you asked Haru, Katase-san, Sōma, and Sakakibara-san about what you did not know.”

“If I become the operator, can I no longer ask?”

“You can.  
But once the system selects one person as operator, the final responsibility leans toward that person.”

Nagi looked at the candidate list on the screen.

“I think what this line needs is not one person’s correct answer, but a role that connects different correct answers from each site.”

Just then, a communication came in.

“Yuto, can you hear me?”

It was Haru’s voice.

“I can hear you.”

“Venue V-3A is stable at 6%.  
Mist is at the minimum.  
It is no longer overcooling a place where no one is present.”

“Thank you.”

“And about the operator.  
Remove me from the candidates.”

“What?”

“I am better suited to watching the field.  
I cannot decide priorities in front of a screen every day.”

“But Haru knows safety routes best.”

“That is why I can go when I am needed.  
That is enough.”

The communication ended.

Immediately, another voice came in.

“Yuto-san.”

It was Sōma.

“I cannot be the operator either.  
I want the reward, but if I judged alone, I would probably lean too much toward efficiency.”

“Sōma-san.”

“But call me when there are people on site.  
If it is about moving people, I can do that.”

His voice sounded slightly embarrassed.

“Today, it was not bad.”

Yuto smiled a little.

“Understood.”

Next came Katase’s voice.

“Soma-kun.  
Remove me from the provisional operator candidates too.”

“You too, Katase-san?”

“I am the site supervisor for the night shift.  
I understand delivery.  
But I do not know everything about the venue, the waiting room, or the past of the equipment.”

“But you said you would take responsibility.”

“I will take responsibility for my site.  
But holding responsibility for this entire line alone is different.”

Yuto looked at the candidate list.

“Soma Yuto”  
“Nagi”  
“R-01”

And Sakakibara in the old control seat.

He was not on the candidate list.  
Most likely, because of his age and health condition, or because his old authority had expired, he was not eligible for selection.

“What about you, R-01-san?”

Yuto asked.

R-01 stayed silent for a moment.

“I am not qualified to return as operator.”

“Why not?”

“I thought only about protecting logistics.  
I pushed human occupancy outside the numbers.”

“But protecting medical delivery was not wrong.”

“It was not wrong.  
But it was insufficient.”

R-01 continued quietly.

“Optimization that cuts people off ultimately cannot protect logistics either.  
If the people working in the field collapse, the cargo will not be delivered.”

Yuto nodded at those words.

“Then Nagi-san?”

Nagi shook her head.

“I will not do it alone either.”

“But the candidates…”

Nagi operated her phone.

“Look carefully at the candidate selection screen.”

Yuto checked the screen.

Beneath the individual candidate names, there was faint text.

“Collaborative operation protocol: locked”

“Collaborative operation?”

Nagi said:

“There is probably a way other than choosing one person.”

“But it is locked.”

“It opens if we meet the conditions.”

R-01 peered at the screen.

His expression changed slightly.

“…Joint evaluation.”

“What is that?”

“In tonight’s logs, there was a four-location coordination bonus.  
It is a record of different people supplementing each other’s decisions at multiple sites.”

“The one that gave Yuto the reward multiplier?”

“Yes.”

“That becomes the condition?”

“It is possible.”

Yuto opened his phone history.

“Four-location coordination bonus: active”  
“Joint work confirmed”  
“Team contribution correction: active”  
“Final emergency condition: progress 94%”

There was one more item there.

“Collaborative operation protocol unlock conditions  
・Simultaneous response across multiple sites  
・Priority allocation not based on individual judgment  
・Approval from site supervisor  
・Registration of three or more continuing collaborators”

“Three or more continuing collaborators.”

Yuto looked at the communication history.

Haru.  
Nagi.  
Sōma.  
Katase.  
R-01.

All of them had been involved in tonight’s decisions.

“We can ask everyone to register as collaborators.”

“Do not say it so lightly.”

R-01 said.

“If they register, they will be called in during emergencies.  
Response logs will remain.  
There may be rewards, but responsibility also arises.”

“I will still ask.”

Yuto connected the line.

“Haru.  
Nagi-san.  
Sōma-san.  
Katase-san.  
R-01-san.”

Voices overlapped on the night rooftop.

“I’m listening.”

“I’m here.”

“Yes.”

“I hear you.”

“…What is it?”

“I will not become the operator alone.”

Yuto said it clearly.

“But I also do not want to abandon this line.”

The display for 0.030 CC per day remained on the screen.

He wanted it.

If he wanted to rise, he should accept it.

But he did not want to become an operator who stopped listening to people’s voices in exchange for that number.

“So I want to open the collaborative operation protocol.  
I will become the field player representative.  
But I will not make decisions alone.”

Haru answered immediately.

“If the role is safety route lead, I will register.  
But no reckless late-night calls.”

“I promise.”

Nagi continued.

“If the role is equipment confirmation and temperature logs, I will register.  
But technical judgment must not be pushed onto one person.”

“Of course.”

Then Sōma’s voice.

“I will handle user routes and field communication.  
If that is the role, I will do it.  
And the reward… make sure it is properly distributed.”

“That is important.”

A small laugh passed between them.

Katase spoke.

“I will register as delivery site approver.  
However, any switch that affects logistics must always be checked with the site.”

“Yes.”

Last came R-01.

A long silence.

Yuto waited.

Eventually, R-01 said:

“I will register as the person providing equipment maintenance and logistics priority information.  
I will not be the decider.”

“Thank you.”

“No thanks are needed.  
This time, build a system that does not cut people off.”

Yuto opened the collaborative operation protocol screen.

“Continuing collaborators: 5”  
“Site supervisor approval: confirmed”  
“Joint work log: confirmed”  
“Multiple-site simultaneous response: confirmed”

“Unlock collaborative operation protocol?”

“I unlock it.”

Yuto pressed the button.

The screen shone white.

“Collaborative operation protocol: unlocked”  
“Shared cooling line provisional operation structure registered”

“Field player representative: Soma Yuto”  
“Safety route: Haru”  
“Equipment and temperature logs: Nagi”  
“User route and field communication: Sōma”  
“Delivery site approval: Katase”  
“Equipment maintenance and logistics information: R-01”

Then the reward display changed.

“Individual provisional operation reward: not eligible”  
“Collaborative operation reward: daily pool 0.050 CC”  
“Distributed to participants according to contribution logs”

“0.050 CC, split among everyone.”

Sōma said.

“It may be less than 0.030 alone.”

“But people who work on active days receive more.”

Nagi said.

“That means people who go to the field are rewarded for their effort.”

Yuto looked at the display.

The path to taking 0.030 CC every day alone had disappeared.

Even so, strangely, he felt no regret.

Rather than earning a large amount alone,  
keeping this line from placing anyone in cold or danger would leave more places to earn the next CC.

The phone vibrated.

“Limited Challenge Second Condition achieved”

“Night delivery waiting station: confirmed balance between comfort and logistics cooling”  
“Shared cooling line: collaborative operation structure confirmed”

“Final emergency condition: achieved”

Numbers appeared all at once.

“Base reward: +0.100 CC”  
“Final emergency reward: +0.150 CC”  
“Team contribution multiplier: ×1.4”  
“Collaborative operation selection correction: +0.031 CC”

“This challenge earned: +0.381 CC”

Yuto opened his eyes wide.

“…Zero point three eight one?”

The cumulative display jumped.

“Cumulative: 1.243 CC”

It had crossed 1.0 CC.

The tiny 0.004.  
The 0.007.  
The 0.010.

Everything he had built up until today finally connected to a larger number.

Haru’s voice.

“You did it.  
First stage of rising upward, I guess.”

Sōma laughed.

“But calculate everyone’s share properly.”

“I will.”

Nagi said:

“Do not look only at the numbers and drop someone again.”

“Yes.”

R-01 looked up at the night sky.

“Tell Sakakibara this.  
The line is not finished yet.”

At that moment, an unfamiliar notification arrived on Yuto’s phone.

“Upper player observation notification”

“Your shared cooling line collaborative operation log  
has been registered as an achievement eligible for the metropolitan ranking”

“Metropolitan ranking?”

Yuto scrolled the screen.

“Current rank: outside ranking”  
“Next update: in 72 hours”

And below that.

“Viewed by an observed player”

“Viewer ID: FROST-9”

“…Who is FROST-9?”

On the night rooftop, Yuto stared at that name.

The shared cooling line had been protected.

His CC had increased significantly.

But Yuto’s rise was beginning to be noticed beyond the small line at the edge of town, by the world of much larger players.

## Afterword

In Episode 60, after successfully completing the emergency response for the shared cooling line, Yuto chose the “collaborative operation protocol” supported by his companions instead of becoming an individual provisional operator.

The key points this time were as follows.

- If Yuto became an individual provisional operator, he might have earned 0.030 CC per day plus performance rewards
- However, instead of holding priorities and responsibility alone, Yuto chose to share judgment with companions who each had site-specific expertise
- Haru handles safety routes, Nagi equipment and temperature logs, Sōma user routes and field communication, Katase delivery site approval, and R-01 equipment maintenance and logistics information
- Under collaborative operation, a daily pool of 0.050 CC is distributed according to contribution logs
- By completing the final emergency condition of the limited challenge, Yuto earned +0.381 CC in this challenge
- Yuto’s cumulative Cooling Credit reached 1.243 CC, crossing 1.0 CC for the first time
- At the end, the collaborative operation achievement was registered as eligible for the metropolitan ranking, and the mysterious upper player “FROST-9” had viewed Yuto’s log

Episode 60 became the episode where Yuto deliberately avoided the “individual seat that could earn a lot” and instead chose a collaborative structure that could continue earning over the long term.  
In the short term, it may look as if he reduced his personal reward, but Yuto has gained a new achievement as field player representative and an entrance into the metropolitan rankings.

From the next episode, after an after-story for the shared cooling line arc, the metropolitan ranking and the upper player FROST-9 will become a new axis.  
Yuto’s small improvement logs no longer end inside the edge-of-town line.
