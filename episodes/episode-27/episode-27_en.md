# Episode 27: The Side That Received Support and the Side Watching

[← Top / README](../../README.md)

Episode 27 is the day after the support notification has fallen, when the faces of those who received it and those who did not become visible.

Yuto's own life is shaken slightly as he stands in three positions at once: the side receiving support, the side watching support, and the side thinking about where the line of support should be drawn.

---

## Main Text

Tuesday.

Soma Yuto stared at the notification on his phone while feeling the weak breeze from the air conditioner.

The notice about the Daily-Life Support Link had arrived yesterday.

It was a proposal to partially subsidize the electricity fees for household cooling used between 1 p.m. and 4 p.m. during the predicted heat-wave peak.

Eligible players would be selected from among those with Daily-Life Support Link Lv.1 or higher, while also taking housing conditions and other factors into account—a vague line of explanation had been attached.

"I guess people who get selected will receive individual notifications."

His convenience store shift was in the morning.

After 1 p.m., whether he stayed home or went out into town depended on the day.

Today, the notification arrived before his shift.

> Daily-Life Support Link Trial Slot: Updated from provisional registration to confirmed registration.
>
> Target: Soma Yuto  
> Residential Area: Apartment building / Daily-Life Support Link Lv.1
>
> Details:  
> ・On the next predicted heat-wave peak day, part of the electricity cost for household cooling used between 1 p.m. and 4 p.m. will be subsidized.  
> ・Subsidy cap: A fixed portion of cooling power consumption during the target period.  
> Details will be sent on the morning of the target day.

"...So I got it."

The moment he saw the notification, several feelings collided inside his chest.

Honestly, he was glad.

His electricity bill was tight every month.

Being able to use the air conditioner on a peak day without hesitating was genuinely helpful for his daily life.

At the same time, the words he had seen in the player chat from a Bronze-rank player came back to him: "Looks like I'm not eligible, so I feel a little complicated about it."

"If I'm on the selected side, that means someone else isn't."

He ate bread, drank barley tea, and headed to the convenience store.

Morning shift.

Deliveries.

Register.

The work itself was the same as usual.

But in one corner of his mind, a concrete plan kept flickering: what should he do with the air conditioner in his room three afternoons from now?

During his break, he opened the player chat in a corner of the back room.

> Bronze Player A:  
> "I didn't get a confirmed registration notification for the support link.  
> Half of me feels like, well, of course.  
> The other half is honestly a little jealous."

> Bronze Player B:  
> "I'm Lv.0, so I'm completely outside the target group.  
> But if people like Soma or WindArc, who have actually been active, receive it, I can understand that too."

> WindArc:  
> "I'm a restaurant worker, so household cooling support doesn't even fit my situation.  
> It would be amazing if the kitchen could be cooled instead."

Yuto looked at the screen and let out a short breath.

"The side that got it, the side that didn't, and the side that doesn't fit the frame at all... all in the same chat."

How should he handle the fact that he was on the selected side?

Should he stay silent and let it pass?

Or should he say it honestly?

Either choice carried a kind of pain.

He moved his finger across the screen.

> Yuto:  
> "I received a confirmed registration notification for the Daily-Life Support Link.  
> It seems to apply to household cooling between 1 p.m. and 4 p.m. on the peak day.
>
> Honestly, I'm grateful.  
> But I also want to think carefully about what I write and leave behind, so it doesn't look as if only the selected side gets to be comfortable."

Several replies came back within seconds.

> Bronze Player A:  
> "Thank you for telling us.  
> It's much better to hear you honestly say you're grateful than to have people stay strangely silent."

> Bronze Player B:  
> "I agree.  
> A world where selected people hold back and say nothing sounds even more painful."

> WindArc:  
> "If the selected side writes how they felt and how they used the support, it should help the design side adjust the next round.  
> Like a discomfort report."

The anxiety in Yuto's chest took on a little more shape.

"So the selected side has a role: to write both the experience of support and the discomfort around it."

In the afternoon, after his shift ended, he stepped outside.

The shopping street.

The station front.

On the way toward the riverbank, a new notification arrived on his phone.

> Cooling Commons Daily-Life Support Link:  
> Guidance for the pre-log form for selected support recipients
>
> Details:  
> ・On the support day, please briefly describe what kind of daily-life pattern you expect when using cooling.  
> Examples: staying home, going out, work, family structure, and so on.  
> ・This will be linked to a post-support form asking how the support helped and where you felt discomfort.

"...So there are pre-logs and post-logs too?"

Before and after the support, daily life would be recorded as a log.

Just like cooling itself, support was also treated as loggable data.

"Everything becomes a log in this world."

Even as he grumbled, Yuto faced his phone again.

> Pre-log draft:
>
> ・At-home status: I plan to be home for one to two hours during the peak period.  
> I expect to return home after my morning shift.
>
> ・Household: Single-person household.
>
> ・Planned cooling use:  
> If the room temperature exceeds 30°C, I will turn on the air conditioner at around 28°C.  
> I will also use an electric fan.
>
> ・Purpose in daily life:  
> Rest after work.  
> Organizing cooling logs, notes, and simple reports.
>
> ・Concerns:  
> The perceived gap with people who did not receive support.  
> I do not want to make the room colder just because support is available.

As he wrote, he laughed a little at himself.

"I doubt many people write 'I don't want to make it colder just because I have support' in a support log."

But to him, that line mattered.

Just because support was available did not mean he intended to change his way of thinking about cooling.

It only meant he could choose cooling to avoid collapse with a little more peace of mind.

At the entrance to the riverbank, he stood beneath the shade and felt the wind.

The COOL WATER sign and the words "Please rest in the shade" were still there.

For people resting here, household electricity support had nothing to do with it.

But this too was support for daily life.

"Support isn't only about electricity bills."

Shade.

Benches.

Mist.

Those too were cooling that supported life.

But once support took the form of electricity fees, the topic of numbers and inequality became suddenly real.

When he returned to his apartment, he scanned the air in the room.

> Indoor temperature: 29.7°C  
> Heat stress index: Medium  
> Daily-Life Support Link trial slot: Confirmed registration

"Three days from now, on the peak day, I guess I'll have to write down how I actually spent it."

Yuto opened his notebook and began summarizing the day.

> Today's events  
> ・Received confirmed registration for the Daily-Life Support Link trial slot.  
> → I will be eligible for partial electricity-fee support for household cooling between 1 p.m. and 4 p.m. on the next heat-wave peak day.
>
> ・In the player chat:  
> → Bronze players outside the target group expressed mixed feelings of envy and acceptance.  
> → People outside the frame entirely, such as kitchen workers, wanted different kinds of support.  
> → There was an expectation that the selected side should honestly say they are grateful while also writing down discomfort.
>
> ・In the pre-log form for selected recipients:  
> → I wrote my planned cooling use, daily-life purpose, and concerns about support and inequality.

> Today's realizations  
> ・When the selected side, the unselected side, and people outside the frame talk about support in the same place, being honest can sometimes make the atmosphere easier than staying silent.  
> ・Support logs are also treated as part of Cooling Commons and become material for the next institutional adjustment.  
> ・By thinking about non-monetary support such as shade and benches alongside electricity-fee support, it becomes possible to weaken the view that support only means money.

Finally, he wrote his usual line.

> Today's line  
> As someone on the selected side, writing both gratitude and roughness together is also one of the cooling actions of Part 3.

When he checked his phone, today's Cooling Credit had increased only slightly.

> Earned today: 0.017 CC  
> Total: 0.626 CC

"The number matters less than that afternoon three days from now."

Yuto closed his notebook and kept the air conditioner at its usual setting.

The world was still hot.

And within that heat, three kinds of lives—the side that received support, the side that did not, and the side outside the frame—had begun to walk quietly through the same chat and the same town.

## Afterword

Thank you for reading Episode 27, Part 3 Episode 3.

In this episode, I depicted the following:

- The Daily-Life Support Link trial slot changes from provisional registration to confirmed registration, placing Yuto on the recipient side.
- In the player chat, people in the target group, outside the target group, and outside the frame entirely exchange honest feelings about support.
- Through the pre-log form for selected support recipients, Yuto puts into words in advance how he will use and feel the support.

The important points are these:

- The selected side does not hide the fact that they feel grateful, but also expresses roughness, inequality, and caution about how the support is used.
- Support logs are treated as part of Cooling Commons, becoming material for the next adjustment of who receives what kind of support.
- By placing monetary support such as electricity-fee subsidies alongside environmental support such as shade, benches, and mist, the story weakens the idea that support means only money.

In Part 3, the story will continue to deepen the relationship between support and inequality, and between institutions and daily life, through scenes such as:

- The actual support experience on the peak day.
- Discomfort reports from people who did not receive support or were outside the frame.
- Corporate attempts to connect support and contribution scores to sales and brand image.
