# Episode 11: How Far Is My Town?

[← Top / README](../../README.md)

In Episode 11, Yuto walks through his familiar town again with slightly different eyes.

While continuing cooling actions within his living zone, he begins to grasp, at a sensory level, how far he should act as an individual and where things should be handed over to systems or teams.

---

Monday.

For the first time in a while, Soma Yuto had a weekday with no special plans.

His convenience-store shift began in the afternoon.

The morning was free.

The conversation at the café yesterday still remained in his head.

“Today's role:  
- Properly remember the discomfort  
- Continue cooling within the living zone”

Yuto looked again at the sentence he had written at the end of his notebook.

“…In the end, what I do doesn't really change.”

Walk through the town.

Read the heat.

Cool it a little.

Write a log.

Through the conversation with ShiroReflect, he had felt the presence of systems.

He had also heard the term Cooling Commons.

But what he could do now was still to keep moving inside his living zone.

Yuto picked up his phone and opened **ThermoBalance**.

“Cumulative Cooling Credit: 0.318 CC”

“First, I'll aim for 0.5.”

It was not a grand concrete goal.

But as a near-future milestone, it felt just right.

He opened the apartment door and stepped outside.

The air was already lukewarm.

Clear sky.

High humidity.

The way heat spread felt the same as always, and yet slightly different.

“How far is my town?”

Those words suddenly surfaced.

The places he had cooled so far:

The convenience-store back room.

The bus stop in front of the station.

The street trees in the shopping street.

The riverside entrance.

The suburban chain store surroundings.

To Yuto, they were parts of his living zone and also cooling targets.

But that range had limits.

Distance he could walk.

Routes he often used.

Places he could stop by on the way to work or shopping.

“On the other hand, there are also many places that aren't my town.”

Large factories.

Highway interchanges.

Huge shopping centers.

Port facilities.

They were outside the living zone.

Places an individual would not easily visit on foot.

“Those must be someone else's town.”

Thinking that made him feel a little lighter.

He did not need to cool everything himself.

He only needed to cool the range visible to him.

The parts still missing would be seen by someone else.

Systems and projects would pick them up.

“Then I'll look for places inside my town that I haven't touched yet.”

Today, Yuto chose a slightly roundabout route.

Not the station front, but a road through the residential area.

It was a place he had never seriously looked at as a cooling target.

Narrow alleys.

Low buildings.

Laundry.

Potted plants.

The smell of daily life was strong, and unlike commercial facilities, there was less obvious design intent from someone else.

When he roughly scanned it with **ThermoBalance**, the whole area was pale orange.

“Residential area average surface temperature: Medium  
Heat stagnation: Locally Medium to High  
Cooling potential: Medium”

“Everything is medium…”

Yuto laughed a little.

“But medium is the easiest to overlook.”

Everywhere was somewhat hot and somewhat bearable.

No one treated it as an overwhelmingly painful place.

So measures were postponed.

As he walked through the alley, he came out in front of a small apartment building.

That corner alone had a strange accumulation of heat.

He saw outdoor air-conditioning units lined up.

A second-floor balcony.

A first-floor window.

Air from the outdoor units gathered in part of the alley.

Two old bicycles were placed there.

Probably someone's commute bikes.

“This must be quietly rough in the morning and evening…”

Yuto scanned the positions of the outdoor units and the bicycles.

“Localized exhaust-heat concentration: Medium to High  
Human stay rate: Low to Medium (during entry and exit)  
Cooling potential: Low to Medium”

“The potential is low…”

Few people stayed there.

They only passed through briefly.

Even if he cooled it, the impact would not be very large.

Even so, for the person who used those bicycles every day, it was definitely a burden.

“This is the small inconvenience of daily life.”

Large projects would not even look at it.

But for residents, it existed as a real inconvenience.

“This is my town too.”

Once he thought that, he could not just leave it alone.

Yuto checked whether any residents were nearby, then lightly called out near the mailboxes.

“Excuse me, do these bicycles belong to someone in this apartment?”

A man who seemed to be a resident came out and answered, “Yes.”

“Isn't it hot here?”

“It is.  
In summer, if I leave my bicycle here, I'm already sweating before I even ride it.”

“Right.”

Yuto explained while showing the airflow from the outdoor units.

“If the position could be changed just a little, I think it would reduce the burden.”

Move the bicycles slightly away from the direct blast zone of the outdoor units.

Leave open space in front of the units.

Create a path for the air to escape.

“I don't really mind where they go.”

The man shrugged.

“Then may I shift them over here?”

“Please do.”

That was enough to create the conditions.

Yuto pushed the bicycles slightly and moved them to a position where the wind from the outdoor units was weaker.

In return, a small space opened in front of the units.

His phone vibrated.

“Localized exhaust heat dispersal detected  
Bicycle starting environment: Slight improvement  
+0.004 CC”

“Small…”

But the number had definitely moved.

The man looked at the new bicycle position and nodded a little.

“This alone should make quite a difference.”

“It quietly helps in summer.”

Those words made Yuto quietly happy.

“Cooling that reduces one person's inconvenience inside the living zone may be small from the whole perspective,  
but as part of my town, it's valuable enough.”

Thinking that, Yuto wrote it in his notebook.

“Residential apartment front:  
- Bicycle parking in front of outdoor units  
→ Moved bicycles to a weaker-wind position  
→ Exhaust heat dispersal + reduced burden before riding”

“From the perspective of Cooling Commons or system talk, something like this may be a trivial log.”

Large-scale projects.

Urban design.

Reflectivity.

Disaster risk reduction.

Compared with those keywords, the position of bicycles in front of outdoor units was a tiny matter.

But from a resident's perspective, making the start of a daily bicycle ride a little easier had clear meaning.

“It's okay for my town to include details like this.”

As Yuto passed through the narrow residential alley, he turned his eyes toward another place.

In front of a small nursery school.

During drop-off and pick-up hours, strollers, children, and parents gathered there.

There were few people in the morning, but the sunlight was already strong.

There was a small roof near the entrance.

But the place where parents stopped often extended a little beyond the edge of that roof.

“The shade is just a little short…”

Yuto scanned the front of the nursery with **ThermoBalance**.

“Nursery entrance shaded zone: Narrow  
Drop-off and pick-up stay positions: Slight bias outside shaded zone  
Cooling potential: Medium”

“This is also cooling inside daily life.”

Children.

Parents.

Strollers.

People vulnerable to heat gathered there.

Yuto spoke to a nursery teacher who came out through the entrance.

“Excuse me, isn't the area in front of the entrance hot?”

“It is.  
In summer, parents look pretty exhausted during drop-off and pick-up.”

“If there were a way to increase shade just a little, could we try it?”

“What would you do?”

“Guide standing positions slightly toward the shaded side under the roof.”

Yuto explained while showing how the roof shadow fell and where parents and children tended to stand.

“Even placing a marker here would probably change where people stand.”

“A marker…”

“For example, stickers.”

The nursery teacher thought for a moment.

“If there is no safety problem, it might be worth trying.”

“Thank you.”

On the spot, Yuto suggested a simple idea.

Place small animal stickers on the floor in front of the entrance.

Visually show, “Please wait here,” within the range where the roof casts shade.

Children often notice such markers.

Parents adjust their standing positions to match them.

Over several days, they discussed it with the nursery side, and the stickers were actually placed.

On the first day of the trial, **ThermoBalance** displayed:

“Nursery entrance shaded stay positions: +21% compared with previous day  
Heat-stress index (estimated): Slight decrease  
Safety contribution: Slight to Medium”

“This kind of thing is also my town.”

Yuto quietly nodded while looking at the nursery entrance.

By the time he finished his afternoon convenience-store shift, several small notifications had accumulated on his phone.

“Cooling log updated: Residential apartment front / Nursery entrance”

“A nearby player viewed your ‘Daily Life Cooling Log (Second Edition)’”

“WindArc:  
‘The children and nursery example is really good.  
It might be applicable to the family restaurant entrance at the suburban store too.’”

“…So the daily route perspective really is helping someone.”

Yuto wrote the day's summary on a new page in his notebook.

“Today's observations:  
- The range of my town can include small inconveniences visible to me as a resident  
- Small cooling actions, such as bicycles in front of outdoor units and standing positions at nursery entrances, have enough value  
- Daily life cooling logs can be applied to other players' sites”

When he finished writing, his pen stopped for a moment.

“Even after hearing about systems, the fact that what I do doesn't change is pretty important.”

Cooling Commons.

Open licenses.

Ideas and credit.

All of that was still in the future.

For now, he would simply keep cooling his own town, with his own eyes, little by little.

“Toward the end of Part 1, I guess I just need to hold onto the sense of how far my town goes.”

Yuto murmured as if telling himself.

His cumulative Cooling Credit continued to rise slowly.

“Cumulative Cooling Credit: 0.352 CC”

Still small.

But certainly not zero.

Holding that number and the feeling of his town, Yuto quietly closed his notebook.

---

Thank you for reading Episode 11.

This time, after sensing the presence of systems, Yuto looked again at his own living zone.

Small cooling actions such as shifting bicycles away from outdoor-unit exhaust and guiding standing positions at a nursery entrance may look minor from the perspective of city-scale projects, but they have real value for residents.

As Part 1 approaches its ending, Yuto begins to develop a sense of how far his town extends and how far he should act as an individual. This gives him a steadier axis when he steps into systems and teams in Part 2.

I hope you continue to enjoy seeing how that axis grows within the story.
