# Episode 13: The Sound of Systems Arriving

[← Top / README](../../README.md)

In Episode 13, the newly unlocked mission category, **System and Project Cooperation**, begins to descend into Yuto's living zone.

Part 2 does not begin with his sudden participation in a gigantic project. It begins with a small effect appearing in his own town.

---

Wednesday.

Soma Yuto woke to the sound of a notification from his phone.

It sounded slightly different from the usual Cooling Credit alert.

“A new mission category is now available.  
Category: System and Project Cooperation”

“…So it really came.”

At the end of Part 1, he had seen the message announcing the end of Season 1.

He knew that a system-level mission category would be unlocked afterward.

Even so, when it arrived as an actual notification, he felt a little tense.

“What do you actually do in System and Project Cooperation…?”

Yuto opened **ThermoBalance** beside his pillow.

One new tab had appeared.

“System and Project Cooperation  
- Municipal cooperation missions  
- Corporate cooling project support  
- Cooling Commons pilot”

“Pilot…”

The name Cooling Commons, which he had heard yesterday, was there.

Not a formal version.

Trial operation.

An experiment.

Even so, seeing an institutional framework lined up on the screen as missions gave it a strange sense of reality.

“But jumping straight into this would feel wrong.”

Yuto took a deep breath and closed the screen.

What he needed to do today came first: live his life.

His convenience-store job.

Cooling actions.

Logs.

While doing those things, he would gradually observe how far the system had descended into his town.

He bit into cheap toast, drank barley tea, got ready, and went outside.

The morning air was already lukewarm.

But the sounds of the town seemed slightly different from usual.

“Maybe this is what it sounds like when a system arrives.”

There was no literal sound.

Traffic.

Construction noise.

People talking.

Yet he felt that shadows of cooling and systems had begun mixing into all of them.

On the way to the convenience store, Yuto passed through the shopping street.

As usual, people were sitting in the shade of the street trees.

The bench had moved toward the shade and become established as a cool zone.

Then a new poster entered the edge of his vision.

“This shopping street is part of the regional cooling project, COOL STREET.  
Please cooperate by using shade and improving walking routes.  
(Municipality + Shopping Street Association)”

“…There it is.”

The municipality.

The shopping street association.

COOL STREET.

It was exactly one form of System and Project Cooperation.

Yuto moved closer to the poster.

Near the bottom was a small **ThermoBalance** logo and the words, “Linked to regional missions.”

“So cooling actions here will also be reflected in a municipal project.”

Until now, the shade beneath the street tree had appeared as cooperation among individual players.

Now it had been labeled with the name of a municipal project and the shopping street association.

“This is the sound of systems arriving.”

Yuto opened the regional mission screen in **ThermoBalance**.

“Regional Cooling Project: COOL STREET  
Target: Entire shopping street  
Objectives: Reduce pedestrian heat stress / Improve comfort during stays  
Ways to cooperate:  
- Encourage the use of shade  
- Improve movement routes  
- Propose cooling ideas for store fronts”

“The content itself isn't very different from what we've already been doing.”

Shade.

Movement routes.

Store fronts.

But this time, it would move through a combination of municipality, shopping street, and players.

“So this is the everyday-life version of a system mission.”

As he walked through the shopping street, Yuto watched the shade and people's movement as usual.

Benches.

Store fronts.

Vending machines.

Alleys.

Over all of them, the presence of the poster lightly overlapped.

“This area is part of COOL STREET.”

It was one sign that the world had begun consciously thinking about cooling.

He arrived at the convenience store.

The back-room shutter.

Ventilation.

He could not look at the screen while working, but in the back of his mind was the feeling that this store might someday be included in a system mission too.

Before noon, after finishing his shift, Yuto passed through the shopping street again.

Someone had stopped beneath the street-tree shade to look at the poster.

“What is COOL STREET?”

“Apparently it's some project to make the area cooler.”

It was only a passing conversation.

Even so, he could tell that the town's relationship with heat had changed slightly.

His phone vibrated.

“Start of regional cooling project COOL STREET detected.  
Your past logs have been registered as project reference materials.”

“Of course they have.”

Yuto opened the details.

“Reference materials:  
- Shopping-street tree rest-point log  
- Bench-position adjustment log  
- Shade-usage improvement log  
Provider: Soma Yuto (Bronze tier)  
Reference format: Cooling Commons trial framework (limited)”

“Cooling Commons is already being tested in places like this…”

A small beta symbol appeared next to the name.

It was not yet a formal system.

But reference and return of value for ideas had begun inside a regional project.

In the afternoon, Yuto headed to the riverside.

A new sign had been placed in the entrance plaza.

“The riverside entrance is a trial point for the regional cooling project COOL WATER.  
Please cooperate by using tree shade and wind.  
(Municipality + Environmental Organization)”

“The names are actually pretty good…”

COOL STREET.

COOL WATER.

Both were words designed to present cooling as something positive.

More people than before were sitting in the tree shade.

The sign made the value of the shade visible.

His phone vibrated.

“Start of regional cooling project COOL WATER detected.  
The riverside entrance shade-guidance log has been registered as reference material.  
Added to the Cooling Commons trial return framework.”

“…So this is how systems layer themselves over daily life.”

At first, there had only been papers from himself, WindArc, and an unseen player.

Now, a sign from the municipality and an environmental organization had been layered onto them.

Residents' ideas.

Players' logs.

Institutional projects.

All of them overlapped in the same place.

“I don't dislike this.”

Yuto sat in the shade for a while and looked up at the sky.

The heat was still there.

The sun was still merciless.

But in several places around town, signs expressing awareness of cooling had begun overlapping with old logs.

“So my town has become a test field for the system.”

The thought made him feel a little proud.

In the evening, after returning to his apartment, Yuto opened the System and Project Cooperation tab in **ThermoBalance** again.

One new mission was displayed there.

“Mission:  
Submit a COOL STREET / COOL WATER report from the perspective of a living-zone player

Details:  
- Describe what changed for residents before and after the projects began.  
- Include how the town looks, how people move, how the heat feels, discomfort, expectations, and related observations.  
Rewards:  
- Cooling Credit (small)  
- Evaluation points within the Cooling Commons trial framework”

“A report-submission mission…”

This was different from the cooling logs he had written before.

The system itself was asking for the eyes of a resident.

“My role was to remember the discomfort. This is exactly that.”

Yuto opened his notebook and wrote down the main points.

“System report mission:  
- Before and after COOL STREET / COOL WATER  
- What changed for me as a resident  
- How the town looks / how people move / how the heat feels / discomfort and expectations”

“This fits the beginning of Part 2.”

What he had experienced in Part 1.

Changes in the town.

Institutional signs.

The presence of Cooling Commons.

Now he would put them into words and submit them.

“All right. I'll write.”

Yuto opened the report-entry screen in **ThermoBalance**.

After hesitating over the title field, he entered:

“The Beginning of COOL STREET and COOL WATER, Seen by a Resident”

In the description field, he wrote briefly:

“Notes on how the town, the heat, and my own position changed when cooling-project signs appeared in the shopping street and at the riverside entrance.  
About the moment when systems and daily life began to overlap.”

Into the main text, he poured the sensations of the entire day.

The conversation of people reading the signs.

The overlap of posters and handwritten papers.

The increase in people sitting in shade.

His feeling that his town had become a test field for institutions.

When he pressed submit, a quiet notification appeared.

“System and Project Cooperation report accepted.  
It will be reflected in the Cooling Commons trial evaluation.”

“Trial evaluation…”

It sounded like the door to Part 2 opening just a little.

His cumulative Cooling Credit did not increase much that day.

“Earned today: 0.021 CC  
Cumulative total: 0.386 CC”

“The number itself is small.”

But the number was not the only thing that had changed.

How the town looked.

The presence of systems.

His own role.

All of them had become slightly clearer.

At the end of his notebook, Yuto added one line.

“Today's observations:  
- Systems do not only descend from above; they quietly layer themselves over daily life  
- My town can become the stage for those trials  
- When that happens, recording change through the eyes of a resident becomes the first role of Part 2”

He put down the pen and closed the notebook.

“So the system arc really begins now.”

Yuto let out a quiet breath.

The world was still hot.

But his town now contained more than heat alone. Cooling-project signs and past logs stood side by side.

Thank you for reading Episode 13.

The COOL STREET and COOL WATER signs appear, and earlier daily-life cooling logs become reference materials for those projects. Individual actions and institutional frameworks are beginning to overlap quietly.

At this stage, the first step of Part 2 is a mission asking Yuto to report institutional change through the eyes of a resident.

As cooperation with Cooling Commons, companies, and municipalities becomes more concrete, I hope you will continue following how Yuto preserves his resident's perspective.
