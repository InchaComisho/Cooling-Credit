# Episode 33: What Lies Beyond Transparency

[← Top / README](../../README.md)

Episode 33 is written as the episode where, after the way results are presented has changed, voices begin to call for actual expansion.  
This time, beyond the recognition that transparency alone is not enough, Yuto begins looking for another route for people who cannot receive support.

---

## Main Text

Monday.

Soma Yuto stopped in front of the shopping street’s support-record poster and looked up at it.

Just as before, the poster now included the note that it contained the voices of residents who had been outside the support target. If he scanned the QR code, he could also read the design background and the discomfort reports.

The presentation had certainly changed.

It no longer showed only clean achievements. It also showed the remaining gaps.

But another feeling remained inside Yuto.

“...It only became visible.”

Transparency.

It mattered.

But being visible and being helped were not the same thing.

After finishing his morning shift, Yuto opened a message from WindArc.

“WindArc:
The poster changed quite a lot yesterday.
But now that there is more transparency, I’m starting to wonder what comes next.”

“Of course it would come to that.”

Yuto replied right away.

“Yuto:
It became easier to see, but the heat felt by the people who didn’t receive support hasn’t changed.”

A reply came a few seconds later.

“WindArc:
Exactly.
That is why, as the next stage of support, there is now talk of making the following proposals more concrete:

- cooling support for workplaces
- temporary-use support outside the home
- alternative support for people who were outside the target group.”

“Alternative support...”

That phrase stayed with him.

People who could not receive subsidies for home air-conditioning.

People who could not stay at home during the day.

People who were not included in the conditions for the support link at all.

Creating another entrance for those people.

As an expansion of support, it felt like a natural direction.

“What kinds of forms would that take?”

“WindArc:
For example:

- extending mist operation at station and shopping-street rest points
- opening ‘cool-down time slots’ at restaurants and retail shops
- rest-support benefits for people who submit workplace cooling logs

Something like that.
The direction is to include not only people at home, but also people moving around outside.”

“That’s extremely important.”

Yuto thought of the bus stop in front of the station.

The line waiting in the sun.

The benches in the shopping street.

The back room at work.

Parents taking children to and from daycare.

The more support leaned toward “home,” the easier it became for people who were not at home to disappear.

To fill that hole, support based on places, not just households, was necessary.

That afternoon, when Yuto stepped out through the back entrance of the convenience store, he narrowed his eyes at the heat.

The back room had air-conditioning.

But the moment he went outside, heat clung to his skin.

“We really do need workplace support...”

One of his coworkers said this while wiping away sweat.

“Apparently there’s been talk lately about cooling support for people who work outside.”

“Really?”

“It’s still just a rumor, but something like temporary-use support outside the home, or rest support.”

Yuto was a little surprised.

The rumor had already reached the workplace.

That meant the discussion was beginning to become concrete.

Then he noticed a small notice posted on the back-room wall.

“Please do not overexert yourself in the heat.
If you feel unwell, please take a break without hesitation.”

It was a message that had always been there.

But today, it looked strangely distant.

“There are people for whom ‘please take a break’ is enough.
But there are also people who can’t take one.”

After his shift ended, Yuto met WindArc at the station-front bench.

A faint evening breeze passed through.

Even so, heat still rose from the ground.

“Is the workplace support moving at all?”

“Yes.
It is still at the proposal stage, but there is a push to start with support for jobs where it is difficult to take breaks.”

“Jobs where it’s difficult to take breaks...”

“Food service, logistics, cleaning, customer service.
There was also an opinion that people moving with children and outside sales workers should be included.”

“That’s much closer to reality.”

“Yes.
It can loosen the impression that support is only for people who are at home.”

Yuto nodded and looked down the shopping street.

Transparency had increased.

But that alone was not enough.

Only there did the discussion about expanding support finally begin.

“After changing the way it is shown, the next step is expansion.”

“Exactly.
And to create that expansion, we need the voices of people who could not receive support.”

“Another discomfort report, then.”

“Yes.
But this time, I want it to say not only what is lacking, but also where support can realistically be expanded.”

Yuto thought for a moment.

“For people moving around outside, realistic options might be:

- extending cool-down time in the shopping street
- making workplace breaks easier to take
- temporary cooling spaces at stations and bus stops.

Those seem possible.”

“They are very realistic.”

“If we place those beside home-based support, then both ‘places of living’ and ‘places of movement’ need to be supported.”

“That is exactly it.”

That night, Yuto returned to his room and opened his notebook.

“Today’s events

- After the support-record poster became more transparent, I confirmed the feeling that being visible does not mean being helped.
- WindArc shared proposals for the next stage: workplace support, outside-the-home support, and alternative support.
  - longer operation at station and shopping-street rest points
  - cool-down time slots at restaurants and retail shops
  - the possibility of rest support
- Even at work, support for occupations where it is difficult to take breaks has begun to take concrete shape.”

“Today’s realization

- Transparency is important, but it does not close the gap by itself.
- Support must extend not only to air-conditioning for people at home, but also to places for people moving outside.
- The next focus of Part 3 is what to add after visualization.”

Finally, he wrote one line.

“Today’s line:
In a city where transparency has increased, the remaining gap will stay as it is unless we ask how far support should expand.”

When he checked his phone, his Cooling Credit had increased slightly.

“Earned today: 0.012 CC
Total: 0.700 CC”

“Exactly 0.7...”

The number felt like a milestone, but emotionally, he was still in the middle of things.

The world was still hot.

But beyond the gap that had become visible, Yuto and the others had finally begun thinking about support that could spread wider.

## Afterword

Episode 33 depicted the flow from increased transparency around support records to the search for wider support, beginning with the feeling that simply making things visible does not save the people still left in the heat.  
This time, proposals began to take shape for workplace support, outside-the-home support, and alternative support for people who cannot enter the home air-conditioning subsidy system, cannot stay at home during the day, or work in jobs where breaks are difficult to take.

The important point here is not to treat transparency as the goal.  
Even if the voices of people outside the support target become visible, some people still remain in the heat.  
That is why the story moves toward mechanisms that support both places of living and places of movement, such as rest support, temporary cooling spaces, and cool-down time slots.

The next major issue in Part 3 is how far this discussion of expansion can become concrete, and how companies and local governments will receive it.  
I hope you will continue following what Yuto tries to add beyond the gap that has become visible.
