# Episode 23: Not Stolen, But Not Enough

[← Top / README](../../README.md)

Episode 23 turns the rough feeling Yuto had toward the nameless poster into his first clear conversation about it.

The perspectives of institutions, companies, and resident players begin to collide more directly, and words are finally given to a state that is not appropriation, but still lacks sufficient return.

---

Friday.

After finishing his midday shift at the convenience store, Soma Yuto left a little more slowly than usual.

Outside, the heat had not changed.

But over the past few days, posters and signs for **COOL STREET** and **COOL WATER** had increased, adding a slightly thicker layer of intention to cool the city.

As he headed toward the shopping street, his feet naturally stopped in front of one of the posters.

A slogan written in large letters: “Take a Breath Here.”

Below it were only the words: “Cooperation: COOL STREET Executive Committee.”

His name was not there.

WindArc's name was not there.

The names of the other resident players were not there either.

“It hasn't been stolen.  
But it feels like it hasn't reached back, either.”

Yuto murmured quietly.

Inside the Cooling Commons credit page, his ID, WindArc's ID, and the anonymous player's ID were all recorded.

In other words, the system had properly preserved whose logs the words had been extracted from.

So it was not appropriation.

But the return was still insufficient.

That feeling quietly smoldered in his chest.

His smartphone vibrated.

“Message from nearby player  
Sender: WindArc”

“WindArc:  
I had a chance to talk briefly with someone from the COOL STREET office today.  
They told me to say something if I had concerns about how the ‘Take a Breath Here’ slogan was being handled,  
so I honestly told them about the uneasy feeling of seeing a poster with no names on it.”

“…He said it.”

Yuto straightened slightly.

WindArc had taken the thing Yuto was feeling to the institutional side.

That must have taken considerable courage.

He opened the rest of the reply.

“WindArc:  
To summarize the office's reaction,  
it was something like this:  
‘We have no intention of stealing anything,  
but we are aware that the way we return value is still insufficient.’”

“…So they understand that much.”

Still looking at the poster, Yuto felt some of the tension leave his shoulders.

“They aren't trying to steal it.  
But they don't yet know how to return it properly.”

WindArc's message continued.

“WindArc:  
Their reasoning is that  
if they tie the slogan too strongly to one person's name,  
it might become harder to use in an open space.

That is why the posters and public materials only say ‘Created by the Executive Committee.’  
Instead, they want to return value through internal credit and future forms of life support.”

“I understand the logic.”

Cooling Commons.

A shared resource.

Words anyone can use.

If those words are tied too strongly to one person, the people who want to use them might hesitate.

That was certainly possible.

Even so.

“We need to give a proper name to this state — not stolen, but not enough.”

Yuto drew a slow breath in the shade of the shopping street.

At that moment, another notification arrived.

“Message from hidden-rank player”

“Hidden-rank player:  
Do you have a little time?  
I would like to organize the language around a state that is not appropriation, but still lacks sufficient return.”

“…Here it comes.”

Yuto tapped “Yes” and opened a simple voice-call mode.

Audio only.

He could not see the other person's face.

But the voice had a calm firmness to it.

“Soma-san, are you in the shopping street now?”

“Yes.  
I'm in front of the ‘Take a Breath Here’ poster.”

“Good.  
Then let's talk while you look at that poster.”

The hidden-rank player — a voice different from ShiroReflect's — paused briefly before continuing.

“First, I want to confirm something.  
The current situation is not intentional appropriation.”

“I understand that.”

“Cooling Commons internal logs properly record which words were extracted from whose daily life.  
In other words, the system is not moving in a direction that hides the source.”

“Yes.”

“However, on the outside face — posters and public communications — individual names do not appear.”

“That's the issue.”

“Let us temporarily call this state anonymous contribution.”

“Anonymous contribution…”

“There is someone's contribution.  
But the name is not visible from outside.  
It is evaluated internally, but not recognized in everyday public life.”

“That really is a little different from calling it appropriation,  
and it doesn't feel completely fair either.”

“Exactly.  
It is not theft.  
But if the system leans too heavily toward anonymous contribution,  
it can become a state where nothing returns to the life of the contributor.”

The words were direct.

“So in Cooling Commons terms, a world made only of anonymous contribution would be bad, right?”

“Personally, I think so.  
Both openness and return are necessary.”

“And right now, the return side is still too thin?”

“Yes.  
At present, return is limited to  
- internal credit points  
- consideration of future life support.”

“Points alone don't change life.”

“Correct.  
So the insufficiency you feel can be called a legitimate discomfort — the sense that anonymous contribution alone can exhaust the people who contribute.”

“…That makes me feel a little relieved.”

“That discomfort is important data when designing Cooling Commons.”

The hidden-rank player's voice softened slightly.

“Then what should be done?”

“Yes.”

“One direction is a two-layer structure:  
- the outside face keeps slogans open  
- the inside face increases concrete return to the residents who contributed.”

“The credit page I saw earlier is the inside face.”

“Yes.  
It is still experimental, but ideas are gradually emerging to connect it with  
- life support  
- priority access to cooling equipment  
- education and work opportunities.”

“If that becomes real, then even when seeing a poster without names,  
it might become acceptable because something is being returned on the inside.”

“Ideally, yes.”

Yuto looked up at the poster.

“But right now, the name is absent, and not much is returning on the inside either.”

“Exactly.”

“So I don't have to force myself to swallow this as a positive thing?”

“No.  
You do not need to swallow it.  
Rather, I want you to leave a log saying that it feels insufficient.”

“A log…”

“It can be in your notebook, or you can submit it as a discomfort report within Cooling Commons.”

“There is a discomfort report function?”

“It is experimental, but yes.”

Yuto could not help laughing.

“Everything in this world is experimental.”

“We cannot know without testing.”

There was a faint laugh in the hidden-rank player's voice as well.

“So putting the state of ‘not stolen, but not enough’ into words and logging it is also part of Cooling Commons.”

“That is correct.”

Before the call ended, the other player added one more thing.

“If you are willing,  
please write a short discomfort report about how you felt when you saw the poster without names.  
The design side will read it.”

“I understand.”

After the call ended, Yuto opened his notebook in the shade of the street trees.

“Discomfort Report Draft  
Title: The Day I Saw a Poster Without Names

Notes:  
- I am happy that the phrase ‘Take a Breath Here,’ extracted from daily life logs, has spread as a city poster.

- At the same time, the poster contains none of the names of the resident players, including myself.

- Since the internal credit page records contributor IDs, I understand that this is not intentional appropriation.

- However, at present, that internal credit is not yet connected to realistic support such as rent, electricity costs, or cooling equipment.

- For that reason, I feel a quiet discomfort toward the state of ‘not stolen, but insufficient as return.’”

“Something like this.”

Yuto reread it and exhaled softly.

“It isn't a purely positive report,  
but I was told that this kind of thing can also remain as a Cooling Commons log.”

That night, back in his apartment, he formally submitted the discomfort report.

A quiet message appeared on the screen.

“Discomfort report received.  
It will be treated as reference material for the Cooling Commons design team.”

“It isn't appropriation.  
But it is insufficient.  
It starts with giving that state a name.”

Yuto added one final line to his notebook.

“Today's line:  
Understanding that something was not stolen and admitting that it is still insufficient can coexist.  
Logging both of those is my role right now.”

When he checked his phone, today's number was small.

“Earned today: 0.011 CC  
Cumulative: 0.568 CC”

“As a number, today was thin…  
but another log has been added on the rules side.”

The world was still hot.

But within that heat, the state of not being appropriation and yet lacking return had begun to be given words and examined inside Cooling Commons.

---

Thank you for reading Episode 23.

This episode took the uneasy feeling of seeing a phrase close to Yuto's own words spread through the city without his name attached and, through a conversation with an institution-oriented player, put it into words as “anonymous contribution” and “a state that is not appropriation, but still lacks sufficient return.”

The important point here is not a simple binary of “appropriation or not.” It is the recognition that understanding something was not stolen and still admitting that the return is insufficient can coexist.

By leaving that recognition as a discomfort report, Cooling Commons designers gain concrete material for understanding where residents begin to feel friction.

When more direct issues of appropriation, attribution, and linkage with life support appear later, the nuance developed in this episode — not stolen, but not enough — should deepen Yuto's judgments and hesitations.

Please continue watching how Yuto walks along this subtle line.
